## Problem

We cannot explain why eleven otherwise qualifying prime-field curves were passed over in the six timestamp streams reported by Valsorda and Anthropic, because the original candidate-acceptance code, its state and a complete run log are unavailable. The tested set is the eleven unpublished candidates explicitly named in those reports: five over the 239-bit ANSI field, two over P-192's field, one over P-256's field and three over P-224's field. A historical selection conclusion must classify the published and omitted candidates under one independently specified rule, rather than infer motive from the final constants alone.

The contemporaneous November 1997 draft supports a **broader numerical cohort** than that named eleven. A source-bound replay of the inherited prime-order census finds 69 seed positions with at least one qualifying root branch in the recovered prefixes: 10 published and 59 unpublished. The extra 48 consist of 17 selected-root orders wider than their fields and 31 positions prime-order only on the other root. The draft states neither exclusion; its near-prime procedure might admit still more positions. Any all-eleven historical or hidden-weakness claim must justify restricting the cohort before interpreting a pattern in those eleven. The 69 are eligible positions under the listed text, not a count of actual program visits.

A subsequent corrected `l_max=255` replay and independent point certificates raise the **observed lower bound to 99** positions: the 69 full-prime cases plus 30 smooth-cofactor cases, for 10 published and 89 unpublished. The first 130-position calculation is invalid because it misread P-224's unseeded quadratic-twist order as a seeded coefficient sign; its raw files and correction are retained in the linked near-prime report. The 10,083 censored positions in the retained point-count archive prevent a complete near-prime census. A cofactor-one preference, consistent with all ten publications, removes the 30 additions, but the 1997 draft's stated rule does not require it.
