## LEADS

Wave-1 (relations) left two things open that every later item inherits: its μ_B records assume `B | p−1`, which the barriers lead on multiplicative cosets shows is dead on secp256k1 (`p−1 = 2·3·7·13441·P_233`), and its "union-of-cosets predicate for deployability" patches that at a cost of `r` extra multiplications per fixing without changing the divisibility requirement. The five leads below are forced by that gap and by the wave-1 presentation records that silently assume the predicate is a sparse binomial.

1. **Auxiliary-curve torsion factor base through an x-only ladder** `[adaptation]` — *Forced by* wave-1 "Union-of-cosets predicate for deployability" and the barriers "Multiplicative-coset factor base on calibration primes". *Mechanism:* pick any curve `E_aux/F_p` with `B | #E_aux(F_p)` and cyclic `B`-torsion; define `F_tors = {P ∈ E : Z_B(x(P)) = 0}` where `(X_B : Z_B)` is the projective x-only Montgomery ladder for `[B]` on `E_aux` (twist-agnostic, no inversions). `F_tors` is the x-coordinate image of the nonzero `B`-torsion of `E_aux`, about `(B−1)/2` x-values, roughly half landing on `E`. The predicate is the kernel polynomial of the cyclic `B`-isogeny `E_aux → E_aux/⟨G⟩`: degree `(B−1)/2`, splits completely over `F_p`, evaluated in `O(log B)` ring operations — the elliptic analogue of `x^B − 1`. Run the whole ladder in `R = F_p[X]/(h)` with `h = S_{m+1}(fixed; X)` of degree `d = 2^{m−1}` and take `gcd(Z_B mod h, h)`: wave-1's resultant tower verbatim, binomial replaced by a straight-line program. `E_aux` exists for every `p`: random curves have a divisor in `[B, 2B]` with Ford-density ≈ 0.1, so ~10 SEA counts suffice at 256 bits. *Reduces ECDLP to:* exactly the wave-1 μ_B relation stage (`B^{m−1}` fixings × `Õ(d log B)`) and its joint-solve exponent `e_k`. *Exponent:* relation stage `Õ(N)` floor, same as μ_B; delta against rho's `1/2` is `+1/2` unless `e_k < k`; delta against wave-1 is 0 — this moves a deployability condition, not an exponent. *Obstruction:* the binomial's algebra is gone (lead 4); the `N/B` descent gate (IDEA-20260801-003) is unchanged. *Falsification:* on calibration primes from `crypto/docs/ECDLP_CALIBRATION_PRIMES.md`, if the aggregated zero count of `S_{m+1}` on `F_tors^m` falls more than 3σ below a random symmetric set of equal size, or if ladder-mod-`h` roots differ from brute-force membership, the lead dies. Artifacts to extend: `cryptanalysis/experiments/factor-base-yield-v2` (read its yield metric definition), the `ic` executable's "bounded factor-base comparisons".

2. **Dickson line `D_B(x) = 2` for `B | p+1` (the nonsplit torus `T_2`)** `[adaptation]` — *Forced by* the same deployability gap and by the μ_B record's silent choice of the split torus. *Mechanism:* `x = z + z^{−1}` with `z^{p+1} = 1`, `z^B = 1` lies in `F_p`; the predicate `D_B(x) − 2 = 0` (Dickson polynomial, degree `B`, `≈ B/2` roots) is evaluated by the Lucas ladder in `O(log B)` multiplications mod `h`, so the wave-1 tower runs unchanged. Unifying remark: μ_B, Dickson and lead 1 are the x-coordinate `B`-torsion of the split node, the nonsplit node and a smooth cubic — the three one-dimensional group schemes with a cheap x-only ladder (the cusp `G_a` has no small subgroups). Twist corollary: `B | #E_aux` and `B | #E_aux^t` with `B` odd forces `B | p+1`, so the ladder-agnostic torsion of a twist pair collapses onto the Dickson line. *Exponent:* identical to lead 1 (delta 0). *Obstruction:* `secp256k1`'s `p+1 = 16·(2^252 − 2^28 − 61)` has unknown divisor structure; the method is worthless there unless a divisor near `N^{1/m}` exists. *Falsification:* factor `(p+1)/16` by ECM for one CPU-day in a script under `crypto/sage/` next to `crypto/secp256k1_cm_audit` and `RESEARCH_SECP256K1_CM.md`; if no divisor product lands in `[2^40, 2^70]`, record a no-go for the Dickson axis on secp256k1 (lead 1 then remains the only deployable route).

3. **Exceptional-map barrier: group-scheme predicates exhaust cheap complete splitters** `[adaptation]` — *Forced by* the sweep record "Scheme-degree barrier for algebraic factor bases over F_p", which bounds `|F| ≤ 3·deg` but says nothing about *evaluation depth*. *Mechanism:* any predicate of the form `f^{∘k}(x) = c` for a fixed rational `f` costs `k` steps mod `h` and has degree `(deg f)^k`; the fibre over `c` has `(deg f)^k` points over `F̄_p` but, for a *generic* quadratic `f`, the `F_p`-fibre averages 1 and its maximum over `c` is `O(k · log p)` (random-mapping heuristics for quadratic dynamics), versus `2^k` for `x^{2^k}`, `D_{2^k}` and Lattès `[2^k]` on `E_aux`. Ritt–Fried classification of maps with full rational fibres along iterates recovers exactly `x^n`, Dickson, and Lattès — leads 1 and 2. *Reduces to:* nothing new; it is a barrier statement that any further "cheap predicate" proposal must either be one of the three or exhibit a fibre law beating `O(k log p)`. *Exponent:* none claimed. *Obstruction:* the fibre-size heuristic is not a theorem; only the exceptional-polynomial classification is. *Falsification:* 50 random quadratics at `p ≈ 2^20`, `k = 12`: compute `max_c #{x : f^{∘k}(x) = c}`; prediction `≤ 60` for all 50 versus `2^11` roots for `D_{2^{12}}(x) − 2` with `2^12 | p+1`; a generic `f` with a fibre `≥ 500` falsifies and is itself a new factor-base family. Read `RESEARCH_PKM_CRITERION.md` and `research/semaev_alternatives_audit_20261104` first: if PKM's predicate is an iterated or composed polynomial, record this as its evaluation-depth complement.

Surfaces: `RESEARCH_GROEBNER_F4.md`, `RESEARCH_SEMAEV_DECOMPOSITION.md`, `crypto-autoresearcher/groebner_compare` (read for the F4 harness and metric names). This is Record 2.
