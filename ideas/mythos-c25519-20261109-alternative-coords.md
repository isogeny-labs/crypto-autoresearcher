# Curve25519 — coordinate systems other than extended Edwards and XZ Montgomery

Conventions throughout: $p = 2^{255}-19$; Montgomery $E_M\colon v^2 = u^3 + Au^2 + u$, $A = 486662$, $B = 1$; twisted Edwards $E\colon -x^2 + y^2 = 1 + d x^2 y^2$, $d = -121665/121666$; $a=-1$ is a square, $d$ is a non-square; $E(\mathbb F_p) \cong \mathbb Z/8 \times \mathbb Z/\ell$. Write $i := \sqrt{-1} = 2^{(p-1)/4}$ (the repo's `sqrt_m1`). The rational 4-torsion is
$$E(\mathbb F_p)[4] = \{\,O=(0,1),\; T_2=(0,-1),\; T_4=(i,0),\; -T_4=(-i,0)\,\} = \{P : x(P)\,y(P) = 0\}.$$
Translation by $T_2$ is $(x,y)\mapsto(-x,-y)$; translation by $T_4$ is $(x,y)\mapsto(iy, ix)$ (direct from the complete law: $x_3 = (i\cdot y_1 + 0)/1$, $y_3 = (0 - a\cdot i\cdot x_1)/1 = ix_1$). These two facts recur in almost every exceptional-locus statement below.

Baseline (HCD 2008, $a=-1$): extended add $8M + 1c_{2d}$, doubling $4M+4S$ (or $3M+4S$ when $T_3$ is not needed). On this curve $2d$ is a full-size constant, so $1c_{2d} = 1M$; $121665$, $121666$, $486662$, $486664$ are small, so $c_{\text{small}} \ll M$. Cost of the repo as written (BRIEF §1): `EdPoint::double` = $5M+4S$ (the $2Z^2$ is a `fe_mul`), `EdPoint::add` = $11M + 1I$ (two `fe_mul` by 2, `fe_mul(d, t2)` separately, and `ed25519_d()` recomputes $d$ with `fe_inv` on every call); `x25519::ladder` = $9M + 1c$ per bit with every square done by `fe_mul` and `a24` by `fe_mul`.

## LEADS

**1. Projective twisted Edwards $(X{:}Y{:}Z)$, no $T$ — `[known]`, negative with count.**
Map $x = X/Z,\ y = Y/Z$; curve $(-X^2+Y^2)Z^2 = Z^4 + dX^2Y^2$. Addition (BBJLP 2008, $a=-1$):
$A = Z_1Z_2,\ B = A^2,\ C = X_1X_2,\ D = Y_1Y_2,\ E = dCD,\ F = B-E,\ G = B+E,$
$X_3 = AF\big((X_1+Y_1)(X_2+Y_2) - C - D\big),\ Y_3 = AG(D+C),\ Z_3 = FG$: $10M+1S+1c_d+7a$; mixed ($Z_2=1$): $9M+1S+1c_d$.
Doubling: $B=(X_1+Y_1)^2,\ C=X_1^2,\ D=Y_1^2,\ F=D-C,\ H=Z_1^2,\ J=F-2H,\ X_3=(B-C-D)J,\ Y_3=-F(C+D),\ Z_3=FJ$: $3M+4S+6a$.
Complete on all of $E(\mathbb F_p)$ ($F = Z_1^2Z_2^2 - dX_1X_2Y_1Y_2 \ne 0$ because $d$ is a non-square; BBJLP Thm 3.3). Symmetry: $(X{:}Y{:}Z)\sim(\lambda X{:}\lambda Y{:}\lambda Z)$; buys 3 elements of state instead of 4. Verdict: in double-and-add the extended/completed schedule (lead 4) costs the same $3M+4S$ per doubling and $8M+1c$ per addition, so projective loses exactly $2M+1S$ per addition and gains nothing. Per 255-bit scalar: projective $\approx 2162M+1147S$ vs completed $\approx 1908M+1020S$. Surface: none recommended; `tests/formula_counts.rs` as a negative control.

**2. Inverted twisted Edwards $(X{:}Y{:}Z)$, $x = Z/X,\ y = Z/Y$ — `[known]`, negative with count, plus an exceptional-locus identity.**
Curve $(X^2 - Y^2)Z^2 = X^2Y^2 + dZ^4$. Addition:
$A=Z_1Z_2,\ B=dA^2,\ C=X_1X_2,\ D=Y_1Y_2,\ E=CD,\ H=C+D,\ I=(X_1+Y_1)(X_2+Y_2)-C-D,$
$X_3=(E+B)H,\ Y_3=(E-B)I,\ Z_3=AHI$: $9M+1S+1c_d$ (mixed $8M+1S+1c_d$).
Doubling: $A=X_1^2,\ B=Y_1^2,\ C=A-B,\ D=A+B,\ E=(X_1+Y_1)^2-A-B,\ X_3=CD,\ Y_3=E(C-2dZ_1^2),\ Z_3=DE$: $3M+4S+1c_{2d}$.
(Checked against the affine law: $X_3/Z_3 = (X_1X_2Y_1Y_2+d)/(X_1Y_2+Y_1X_2)$ and the doubling identity $2X^2Y^2 - C = C - 2d$ from the inverted curve equation.) Unrepresentable points: $xy=0$, i.e. exactly $E(\mathbb F_p)[4]$; the law additionally fails when the output lies there. Not complete: cannot hold $O$, so `scalar_mul` must start at $P$, not at the identity. Scaling symmetry same as lead 1. Verdict: with $a=-1$ the inverted system is dominated — addition $+1M+1S$, doubling $+1c_{2d}$ ($=+1M$) versus the completed schedule; $\approx 2290M+1147S$ per scalar. Inverted coordinates were competitive only for $a=1$ (BL 2007), which Edwards25519 does not use. The reusable fact: its unrepresentable set equals the exceptional-difference set of lead 3.

**3. Dual (d-free) addition in extended coordinates; exceptional locus $= E(\mathbb F_p)[4]$ — `[adaptation]` (formula HCD 2008 §3.2; locus and detection are the contribution). → RECORD 1.**
Affine: $x_3 = \dfrac{x_1y_1 + x_2y_2}{y_1y_2 - x_1x_2},\ y_3 = \dfrac{x_1y_1 - x_2y_2}{x_1y_2 - y_1x_2}$. Extended, $a=-1$:
$A=(Y_1-X_1)(Y_2+X_2),\ B=(Y_1+X_1)(Y_2-X_2),\ C=2Z_1T_2,\ D=2T_1Z_2,\ E=D+C,\ F=B-A,\ G=B+A,\ H=D-C,$
$X_3=EF,\ Y_3=GH,\ T_3=EH,\ Z_3=FG$: $8M+0c+10a$; mixed ($Z_2=1$): $7M$.
Exceptional iff $F=0$ or $G=0$. Solving on this curve (using $d$ non-square to kill the branch $dx_2^2y_2^2 = 1$ and $-1/d$ non-square to kill $dx_1^2x_2^2=-1$): $F=0 \iff P_2 - P_1 \in \{O, T_2\}$; $G=0 \iff P_2 - P_1 \in \{\pm T_4\}$. Hence the law fails exactly when $P_1 - P_2 \in E(\mathbb F_p)[4]$, and failure is detectable branch-free by $Z_3 = 0$. Cannot double. Symmetry: $F$, $G$ are the eigen-combinations of negation $(X,Y)\mapsto(-X,Y)$; the law is the Edwards law composed with translation by $T_4$ (Bernstein–Lange 2011 "complete set of addition laws"). Honest cost note: it beats the standard law by $1M$ only for *uncached* projective–projective additions; with a Niels-cached operand (lead 5) both laws cost $7M$/$8M$. Surface: `EdPoint::add_dual` for the single $sB + (-h)A$ combination in verification and for Straus table construction; `scripts/check_dual_law_e4_locus.sage`.

**4. Completed coordinates $(E{:}F{:}G{:}H) \in \mathbb P^1\times\mathbb P^1$, $x = E/F,\ y = H/G$ — `[known]` (BDLSY 2011 ref10 `ge_p1p1`; HCD §4.3 mixed coordinates). → part of RECORD 4.**
Doubling into completed form: $A=X^2,\ B=Y^2,\ C=2Z^2,\ E=(X+Y)^2-A-B,\ G=B-A,\ F=G-C,\ H=-(A+B)$: $0M+4S$. Conversions: to $(X{:}Y{:}Z)$: $X=EF,\ Y=GH,\ Z=FG$ ($3M$); to $(X{:}Y{:}Z{:}T)$: additionally $T=EH$ ($+1M$). The consumer, not the producer, decides whether $T$ is paid for. Repo delta: `EdPoint::double` always emits $T$ and uses `fe_mul` for $2Z^2$ ($5M+4S$); the completed schedule gives $3M+4S$ when the next op is a doubling (128 of 255 iterations on average) and $4M+4S$ otherwise. Complete (same law). Symmetry: projective scaling acts separately on $(E{:}F)$ and $(H{:}G)$, which is why the two $\mathbb P^1$ factors can be normalised independently.

**5. Niels precomputed operand $(U,V,W) = (Y+X,\ Y-X,\ 2dT)$ with $Z=1$, or $(U,V,Z,W)$ projectively — `[known]` (BDLSY 2011 `ge_precomp`/`ge_cached`). → part of RECORD 4.**
Addition into completed form: $A = (Y_1-X_1)V_2,\ B = (Y_1+X_1)U_2,\ C = T_1W_2,\ D = 2Z_1$ (or $2Z_1Z_2$), $E=B-A,\ F=D-C,\ G=D+C,\ H=B+A$: $3M+0c$ ($4M$ projective), then $3M$ or $4M$ to leave completed form: mixed add $7M$, projective-Niels add $8M$, zero constant multiplies, zero inversions. Repo delta: `EdPoint::add` is $11M + 1I$ per call (BRIEF: `ed25519_d()` calls `fe_inv` inside `add`); 127 inversions per scalar multiplication disappear. Symmetry: negation is $U \leftrightarrow V,\ W \mapsto -W$ — a free swap, which is what makes signed-digit windows cheap. Complete.

**6. Accumulator kept in Niels form $(U,V,Z,T)$ so doubling shares the $Y\pm X$ work — `[speculative]`, neutral/negative with count.**
Doubling: $S_U=U^2,\ S_V=V^2,\ S_Z=Z^2,\ G=UV\ (=Y^2-X^2),\ E'=S_U-S_V\ (=4XY),\ H'=-(S_U+S_V),\ F_2=2G-4S_Z,\ G_2=2G,$
$X_3=E'F_2,\ Y_3=G_2H',\ Z_3=F_2G_2,\ T_3=E'H'$ (uniform scale 4): $5M+3S$, then $U_3=Y_3+X_3,\ V_3=Y_3-X_3$. Versus $4M+4S$: trades $1S$ for $1M$ and saves two additions. Using the curve identity $G = Z^2 + dT^2$ instead of $UV$ costs $1S+1c_d = 1S+1M$, no better. Verdict: a gain only if measured $S \ge M - 2a$; in a backend with specialised squaring it is a loss. Surface: `ecbench` S/M ratio decides (lead 13).

**7. $T_4$-invariant Kummer coordinate $w = d\,x^2y^2$: differential addition with no constant; it is the Montgomery $x$-line of the 4-isogenous curve — `[adaptation]` (likely known in substance: Farashahi–Hosseini 2017; DJP 2014 4-isogeny codomain). → RECORD 2.**
Invariance: $w(-P)=w(P+T_2)=w(P+T_4)=w(P)$, so $w$ is a degree-8 function, a coordinate on $(E/\langle T_4\rangle)/\pm$. Writing $\alpha_i = -x_i^2,\ \beta_i = y_i^2$ with $\alpha_i+\beta_i = 1+w_i$, $\alpha_i\beta_i = -w_i/d$, the product $\big(x(P{+}Q)x(P{-}Q)\big)\big(y(P{+}Q)y(P{-}Q)\big)$ expands to $(w_1(1+w_2)^2 - w_2(1+w_1)^2)/((1-w_1w_2)^2)\cdot(-1/d)\cdot$(sign), whose numerator factors as $(w_1-w_2)(1-w_1w_2)$, giving
$$w(P+Q)\,w(P-Q) = \frac{(w_1-w_2)^2}{(1-w_1w_2)^2},\qquad w(2P) = \frac{4w\big((1+w)^2 - \tfrac{4a}{d}w\big)}{(1-w^2)^2}.$$
Projective $(W{:}Z)$: $U=(Z_1+W_1)(Z_2-W_2),\ V=(Z_1-W_1)(Z_2+W_2),\ W_{P+Q}=Z_{P-Q}(U-V)^2,\ Z_{P+Q}=W_{P-Q}(U+V)^2$ ($4M+2S$; $3M+2S$ if $Z_{P-Q}=1$). Doubling with $4a/d = 486664/121665$ cleared by the free projective factor $121665$: $S_1=(Z+W)^2,\ S_2=(Z-W)^2,\ D_4=S_1-S_2,\ W_2 = D_4(121665\,S_2 - D_4),\ Z_2 = 121665\,S_1S_2$ ($2M+2S+2c_{\text{small}}$). Setting $X_M := Z,\ Z_M := W$ this is *literally* RFC 7748's ladder step with $a_{24}' = -1/121665$, i.e. $x' = 1/w$ (equivalently $w$, the two differ by the 2-torsion translation of $E'$) is the Montgomery coordinate of $E'\colon y'^2 = x'^3 + A'x'^2 + x'$, $A' = 2(A+6)/(2-A) = -243334/121665$ — the De Feo–Jao–Plût codomain of the 4-isogeny with kernel $\langle(1,\sqrt{486664})\rangle = \langle T_4\rangle$. Cost per bit $5M+4S+2c_{\text{small}}$ vs $5M+4S+1c_{\text{small}}$: slower by one small-constant multiply. Degree of the differential-addition map is $(2,2)$, the same as Montgomery's; no lower-degree law appears. Output ambiguity: $w(kP)$ determines $kP$ only modulo $\langle \pm1, T_4\rangle$; recovering $u(kP)$ requires the dual isogeny (lead 8).

**8. Isogeny-shortened X25519: run the ladder on $E'$ with $k/4$ and apply the dual 4-isogeny — `[speculative]`, net $\approx 0$ with count.**
Clamping gives $8 \mid k$. $\hat\varphi\circ\varphi = [4]$, so $x(kP) = \hat\varphi_x\big(\text{ladder}_{E'}(k/4,\ \varphi_x(u))\big)$ with $\varphi_x(u) = 1/w$ computed from $u$ (degree-4 rational map) and $\hat\varphi_x$ another degree-4 $x$-only map. Saving: 2 ladder steps $= 10M+8S+2c$. Cost: two 4-isogeny evaluations at $\approx 6M+2S$ each (SIKE `eval_4_isog` count, recalled) $= 12M+4S$, plus $E'$ has the worse constant $a_{24}' = -1/121665$ ($+1c$ per step, $-253c$). Net $\approx -2M+4S-250c_{\text{small}}$: a wash at best. An 8-isogeny ($k/8$, 252 bits, $E(\mathbb F_p)$ has a rational point of order 8) saves 3 steps but costs $\ge 3$ isogeny evaluations each way; negative. Surface: `x25519_isog.rs` behind `ecbench`, only if lead 13 shows $c_{\text{small}} \approx 0$.

**9. Jacobian / Chudnovsky on the isomorphic short Weierstrass model — `[known]`, negative with count.**
Isomorphism (not merely birational, no exceptional points): $u = x_M + A/3,\ v = y_M$; $v^2 = u^3 + a_4u + a_6$ with $a_4 = (3 - A^2)/3,\ a_6 = (2A^3 - 9A)/27$ (both full-size mod $p$). Jacobian $x = X/Z^2,\ y = Y/Z^3$. Doubling (dbl-2007-bl): $XX=X_1^2,\ YY=Y_1^2,\ YYYY=YY^2,\ ZZ=Z_1^2,\ S=2((X_1+YY)^2-XX-YYYY),\ M=3XX+a_4\,ZZ^2,\ T=M^2-2S,\ X_3=T,\ Y_3=M(S-T)-8YYYY,\ Z_3=(Y_1+Z_1)^2-YY-ZZ$: $1M+8S+1c_{a_4} = 2M+8S$ here. Addition (add-2007-bl): $U_1=X_1Z_2^2,\ U_2=X_2Z_1^2,\ S_1=Y_1Z_2^3,\ S_2=Y_2Z_1^3,\ H=U_2-U_1,\ I=(2H)^2,\ J=HI,\ r=2(S_2-S_1),\ V=U_1I,\ X_3=r^2-J-2V,\ Y_3=r(V-X_3)-2S_1J,\ Z_3=((Z_1+Z_2)^2-Z_1^2-Z_2^2)H$: $11M+5S$; fails when $H=0$ ($P_2=\pm P_1$) — incomplete, branch required. Chudnovsky $(X{:}Y{:}Z{:}Z^2{:}Z^3)$: add $11M+3S$, doubling $\approx 2M+9S+1c$. The $a_4=-3$ form ($3M+5S$ doubling) exists iff $-3/a_4 \in (\mathbb F_p^\times)^4$ (note $p\equiv 5 \bmod 8$, so $-1$ is a square but not a fourth power; a one-line Sage check), and even then doubling is $3M+5S$ vs Edwards $3M+4S$ and addition $11M+5S$ vs $8M$. Per scalar: $\approx 1907M+2675S$ vs $1908M+1020S$. Verdict: no Weierstrass coordinate system approaches the Edwards counts on this curve; the only reason to keep the map is interoperability tests. Surface: `tests/weierstrass_isomorphism.rs` asserting $j$-invariants and group-law agreement.

**10. Classification: every degree-2 "coordinate-only" ladder on Curve25519 is a Möbius conjugate of the Montgomery $u$; every torsion-invariant one is Montgomery on an isogenous curve — `[adaptation]`, a negative-existence result.**
A degree-2 map $f\colon E\to\mathbb P^1$ is the quotient by an involution with fixed points, $P\mapsto T-P$; a ladder needs $f(P)=f(Q)\Rightarrow f(P{+}R)=f(Q{+}R)$ for all $R$, forcing $2T = O$, i.e. $T\in\{O, T_2\}$, and $f$ is then invariant under $P\mapsto -P$ or $P\mapsto T_2-P$; both quotients have coordinate $u$ (the second via $u\mapsto 1/u$), so $f = (\alpha u+\beta)/(\gamma u+\delta)$. Example: Edwards $y = (u-1)/(u+1)$, so the "$y$-only Edwards ladder" (Castryck–Galbraith–Farashahi 2008) is RFC 7748 conjugated by a Möbius map — same $(2,2)$ degree, constants permuted. Degree-$2m$ functions invariant under a torsion subgroup $H$ of order $m$ are $u'\circ\varphi_H$ for the isogeny $\varphi_H\colon E\to E/H$ (lead 7 is $H=\langle T_4\rangle$). Consequence: the angle's "rational map of lower degree" does not exist among ladderable coordinates; only constant handling can change. Buys a stopping criterion for the search. Surface: `docs/formulas/ladder-coordinates.md` plus the Sage checks of lead 7.

**11. Constant-time Ed25519 variable-base multiplication = RFC 7748 ladder + Okeya–Sakurai $y$-recovery + Montgomery→Edwards map — `[adaptation]` (components known; the composition replaces the repo's variable-time double-and-add). → RECORD 3.**
Recovery (verified by expanding $B(y_2-y_1)^2 = (x_3 + A + x_1 + x_2)(x_1-x_2)^2$): for $P=(x_1,y_1)$, $Q=kP=(x_2,y_2)$, $Q{+}P=(x_3,\cdot)$,
$$y_2 = \frac{(x_1x_2+1)(x_1+x_2+2A) - 2A - (x_1-x_2)^2x_3}{2By_1}.$$
Projective with $x_2 = X_2/Z_2,\ x_3 = X_3/Z_3$ from the ladder's two legs: $\;v = 2y_1Z_2Z_3,\ X'=vX_2,\ Z'=vZ_2,\ Y' = \big[(x_1X_2+Z_2)(x_1Z_2+X_2+2AZ_2)-2AZ_2^2\big]Z_3 - (x_1Z_2-X_2)^2X_3$: $\approx 10M+2S+2c_{\text{small}}$. Then $y_E = (u-1)/(u+1),\ x_E = \sqrt{-486664}\,u/v$ with one shared inversion ($1I+3M$). Exceptions: $y_1=0$ ($P=T_2$), $Z_2=0$ or $Z_3=0$ ($kP=O$ or $(k{+}1)P=O$; for $P$ of order $\ell$ and $0<k<\ell-1$ never), $u=-1$ (not rational, since $A-2 = 486660$ must be a non-square — asserted by the test). Cost: $255(5M+4S+1c_{\text{small}}) + 10M+2S+1I \approx 1290M+1022S+1I$, constant-time if the swap is arithmetic; the repo's current path is $\approx 2672M+1020S+127I$ variable-time; an optimal window-4 projective-Niels method is $\approx 1277M+1020S$ with a 16-entry secret-indexed table. Symmetry: the ladder's involution $(X_2{:}Z_2)\leftrightarrow(X_3{:}Z_3)$ makes the scalar read uniform. Surface: `src/ecc/ed25519.rs::scalar_mul_ladder`, `x25519.rs::ladder_with_y`.

**12. Which other models exist rationally, by torsion — `[adaptation]`, negative-existence.**
Hessian needs a rational 3-torsion point: $3\nmid 8\ell$, impossible. Huff and Jacobi-intersection forms need full rational 2-torsion $E[2]\subseteq E(\mathbb F_p)$: $E(\mathbb F_p)[2] = \{O,T_2\}\cong\mathbb Z/2$, impossible (equivalently $A^2-4 = 486660\cdot486664$ is a non-square). Jacobi quartic needs one 2-torsion point (exists) and Edwards with $a=1$ needs a 4-torsion point (exists, but the $a=1$ model has $d_1 = -d$ non-square? — the twist $x\mapsto ix$ sends $a=-1$ to $a=1$ with $d\mapsto -d$; $-d$ is a non-square iff $d$ is, since $-1$ is a square, so the $a=1$ model is also complete but pays $+1M$ per addition and $+1c$ per doubling). Buys: the remaining candidates are Jacobi quartic (extended-Jacobi-quartic doubling counts to be verified against EFD before filing) and nothing else. Surface: `docs/formulas/models-by-torsion.md`.

**13. Measurement: the repo's $S=M$, $c=M$, $I$-per-add regime makes every count above untranslatable until fixed — `[known]`, tooling.**
`x25519::ladder` squares with `fe_mul` and multiplies by `a24` with `fe_mul`; `ed25519.rs` recomputes $d$ by `fe_inv` per addition. Proposal: `fe_sq` everywhere a square is written, `fe_mul_small(u32)` for $121665/121666/486662/486664$, `const D2` hoisted; `ecbench` reports measured $S/M$, $c_{\text{small}}/M$, $I/M$ and a counting-field wrapper asserts the symbolic counts of leads 1–11 exactly. Without this, lead 6 cannot be decided and lead 8 is unmeasurable.
