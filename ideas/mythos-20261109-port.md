# Mythos ECDLP ideation — port (20261109)

Repository brief: `cryptanalysis/ecdlp-ideas/repo-brief.md` at the commits named in it. Records filed: see the `IDEA-*` ids in this PR.

## LEADS

1. **Exit-dimension theorem for tori, pairing-friendly groups and matrix groups.** Fastest known: (ex)TNFS on F_{p^n} (hence on T_n(F_p) and on MOV/Frey–Rück images), L(1/3); GL_n(F_q) via Menezes–Wu eigenvalue reduction to F_{q^i}^*, i ≤ n, then NFS. Exact feature exploited: the group sits in the unit group of a finite-dimensional F_p-algebra, so elements lift to a global ring with a multiplicative norm. Port needs a nontrivial homomorphism E(F_p) → GL_m(F_{p^j}); Lagrange on |GL_m(F_{p^j})| = p^{jm(m−1)/2}∏_{i≤m}(p^{ij}−1) with N prime ≠ p forces N | p^{ij}−1, i.e. mj ≥ k := ord_N(p). Algebraic homomorphisms E → G land in an abelian subvariety (E complete; Chevalley), so they are isogenies only (KR-RHO-755e35). Reduction: none; required exponent would need k ≤ poly(log p), false for secp256k1 (predict k > 2^100). Falsification test: compute k from the factorisation of N−1. **Provably impossible** (Lagrange + completeness). Extends KR-RHO-53c89f and the exit-map lane IDEA-20260727-005/008. `[adaptation]` → Record 2.

2. **Cheon / Pohlig–Hellman on the scalar torsor.** Fastest for smooth-order groups: Pohlig–Hellman; for pairing-friendly groups with auxiliary inputs [k^d]P, Cheon (repo `cryptanalysis/include/ca_cheon.h`, `ca_pohlig.h`). Feature: d | order of the acting group. Port: E(F_p)∖{O} is a torsor under A = (Z/N)^* of order N−1 = ∏q^e; PH on the torsor needs the quotient maps, which are exactly Φ_q(P,Q) = χ_q(log_P Q), χ_q(k) = k^{(N−1)/q}. With evaluators for all q^e, ECDLP reduces to DLs in μ_{q^e} ⊂ F_N^*: NFS in the 256-bit scalar field, L_N(1/3), practical with CADO (repo `cryptanalysis/experiments/cado-*`). With q = 2 alone, M shifted evaluations give a Legendre sequence and Legendre-PRF key recovery (BBUV 2020, O(N log²N/M²)) gives Õ(N^{1/3}). Obstruction: no evaluator is known; generic model Ω(√N) for a balanced predicate; poly-time hardness of the oracle version is equivalent to Legendre-PRF security. Cheap test: toy census of correlation between χ_q(k) and eight coordinate statistics of [k]P with label and G_a controls. `[speculative]` → Record 1.

3. **Class-group reduction via Lenstra's module isomorphism and the GLV hexagonal lattice.** Fastest in Cl(O_D): Hafner–McCurley / Jacobson L(1/2) (Biasse L(1/3) for special D). Feature: composition + reduction gives a canonical representative with a ≤ √(|D|/3) whose norm factors over prime ideals. Port: for j = 0, E(F_p) ≅ Z[ω]/(π−1) (Lenstra 1996); reduction = exact CVP in the hexagonal ideal lattice, Norm(k₁ + k₂ω) ≤ N/3 — the GLV decomposition the repo already builds (`RESEARCH_GLV_HNP.md`, `RESEARCH_GLV_HNP_PHASE2.md`, `RESEARCH_SECP256K1_CM.md`, `secp256k1_cm_audit`). Step 1 ports exactly; step 2 (factor the norm, multiply relations) needs k, i.e. hidden-scalar products = CDH, and Maurer–Wolf (1999) make CDH discrete-log-hard given a smooth auxiliary curve over F_N. Exponent: unchanged. Test: measure max Norm/N (exact CVP 1/3 vs Babai rounding 3/4) and the smoothness-visibility correlation. `[adaptation]` → Record 3.

4. **Koblitz Frobenius-invariance → μ₃-graded Semaev polynomials on j = 0.** Fastest for E(F_{2^m}) Koblitz IC: Frobenius-invariant factor bases, factor n in systems and n² in LA (KR-IC-b0fcda; repo `RESEARCH_KOBLITZ_INDEX_CALCULUS.md`, `RESEARCH_KOBLITZ_SCALING_TARGET.md`, `ecc2k130/`). Feature: a cheap automorphism commuting with decomposition. Port to secp256k1: φ(x,y) = (βx, y) of order 3 gives S_m(βx) = β^{r_m}S_m(x), so S_m is supported on one total-degree residue mod 3 (hand check on S_3: 9 vs 13 monomials). Gain ≤ 3 in monomials for target-free relations and 3 shared systems per target orbit; zero grading once x_R is substituted. Exponent unchanged; KR-IC-5931ee forecloses any exponent reading. Test: Sage monomial counts of S_4, S_5 at a = 0 vs general. Must read `RESEARCH_SYMMETRIZED_SEMAEV.md` first. `[adaptation]` → Record 4.

5. **NFS two-ring smoothness → lifting to characteristic zero.** Fastest in F_p^*: NFS, L(1/3, (64/9)^{1/3}). Feature: Z and Z[α] both map to F_p by ring homomorphisms and "small" elements are smooth with Dickman probability. Port: lift E to E/Q and use small-height points as factor base. Blocked by Néron–Tate counting: #{P ∈ E(Q): naive height ≤ H} ≍ (log H)^{r/2}, so lifts of coordinates ≈ p yield polylog(p) factor-base points unless r ≍ log p/log log p (Mestre bound), and random lifts have average rank < 1 (Bhargava–Shankar); xedni variant succeeds negligibly (JKSST, KR-IC-cd159a). **Provably impossible** as a polynomial-size lifted factor base (Miller 1985). Test: none needed. `[known]`

6. **Jacobian / Gaudry decomposition → genus-1 degeneracy and the missing subfield.** Fastest for genus g ≥ 3: Õ(q^{2−2/g}) with double large primes (KR-IC-a4dd54); for E(F_{q^n}): Õ(q^{2−2/n}) (KR-IC-46d822). Feature: a g-dimensional abelian variety over F_q with group ≈ q^g and a factor base of size q (degree-1 places). Port to E(F_p): dimension 1 over F_p gives Semaev with B ≈ p, the prime-field case already closed (KR-IC-5931ee). To get dimension g with small q you need F_q ⊊ F_p, which does not exist; embedding E into A/F_{p^j}, j ≥ 1, costs ≥ (p^j)^{2−2/g} ≥ p > √p. **Provably impossible** (F_p has no proper subfield; Gaudry exponent). `[known]`

7. **HNP/LLL (Boneh–Venkatesan) as the F_p^* partial-information mechanism.** Fastest with leaked nonce bits: lattice CVP in poly time. Feature: a *linear* relation k_i = a_i + b_iα mod N with visible coefficients and a small unknown. Oracle-free ECDLP: GLV supplies small unknowns (k₁, k₂), but the only relation Q = [k₁]P + [k₂]φ(P) is linear over the group, not over Z/N with visible coefficients; the (k₁,k₂) box has area ≍ N and is unimodularly the full DL, so any lattice reformulation is an interval problem at √N. Blocked by the absence of a leak (and the no-oracle rule). Test: read `RESEARCH_HNP_LANDSCAPE.md`, `RESEARCH_GLV_HNP*.md`, `RESEARCH_LLL_GS_ANALYSIS.md` and confirm they record the oracle-free GLV lattice as DL-equivalent; if not, add that one-line derivation. `[known]`

8. **Matrix groups / Menezes–Wu → mod-ℓ Galois representations; ℓ-descent is vacuous.** Feature of GL_n DLP: eigenvalues are a homomorphism on ⟨A⟩. The only matrix structure E carries is ρ_ℓ: Frob ↦ GL₂(Z/ℓ) on E[ℓ], computable in poly(ℓ, log p). Port: read DL information of Q from ℓ-torsion data. Blocked: for ℓ ∤ N, [ℓ] is bijective on E(F_p), E(F_p)/ℓE(F_p) = 0 and the Kummer map to H¹(F_p, E[ℓ]) is zero; for ℓ = N the map is the identity but requires N-division (degree N² polynomial). **Provably impossible** (Kummer exact sequence). `[adaptation]` (textbook, not a frontier row).

9. **Anomalous / unipotent exits (Smart, Satoh–Araki, Semaev) → G_a.** Fastest for N = p: p-adic elliptic logarithm, poly time; feature: E₁(Q_p) ≅ Ĝ_a via the formal group, i.e. the N-torsion is a p-group. Port blocked by gcd(N, p) = 1: no nontrivial homomorphism Z/N → any p-group (G_a(F_{p^j}), formal groups, Z_p-modules). **Provably impossible** (Lagrange). KR-RHO-53c89f. `[known]`

10. **Algebraic tori and the one-dimensional dichotomy.** Fastest in T_n(F_p): NFS in F_{p^n}; the Rubin–Silverberg rational parametrisation is compression, not load-bearing. Connected 1-dimensional algebraic groups over F_p are G_a (poly DL), G_m and T₂ (L(1/3)), and elliptic curves (√N). T₂(F_p) has order p+1 and receives the trace-0 supersingular curve via the k = 2 pairing — the only realised torus port. For ordinary prime-order E it is blocked by Lead 1; Lüroth (genus 1 is not rational) blocks only compression. Test: none. `[known]`

11. **FFS / Coppersmith "Frobenius preserves degree" and the repo's Koblitz line.** Coppersmith's FFS exploits x ↦ x² being free and degree-preserving in F₂[x]; the repo's `RESEARCH_KOBLITZ_INDEX_CALCULUS.md` and `ecc2k130` experiments are this mechanism on E(F_{2^m}). Prime-field shadow: the only cheap endomorphism of secp256k1 is φ of order 3, so the GGMP factor n becomes 3, and IDEA-20260727-001 measured the relation-matrix side as O(1). Nothing beyond Lead 4. `[known]`

12. **Class-group / Jacobian large-prime variations → Semaev large primes.** GTTD double large primes give 2−2/g; Jacobson uses large primes in Cl(O). The port is filed: IDEA-20260731-012 (large-prime pool); exponent effect requires decomposition cost below B, which IDEA-20260727-003's cost floor denies at current Gröbner costs. `[known]`

13. **NFS individual-logarithm descent → descent trees and claws.** NFS descent rewrites the target as a ratio of medium-smooth integers via 2-dim LLL (rational reconstruction) and descends. Ports filed: IDEA-20260726-005 (depth-2 descent tree), IDEA-20260731-007 (half-arity claw), IDEA-20260801-003 (descent gate). The LLL step itself needs visible smoothness of the hidden scalar, blocked as in Lead 3. `[known]`

14. **Rigour port: Hafner–McCurley equidistribution vs Semaev decomposition counts.** HM's rigorous L(1/2) rests on equidistribution of reduced forms; the Semaev analogue is the exact mean μ(B, m) = C(2B+m−1, m)/N (IDEA-20260805-a25f11) and the YYTK proof behind KR-IC-5931ee. Already filed; no new object. `[known]`
