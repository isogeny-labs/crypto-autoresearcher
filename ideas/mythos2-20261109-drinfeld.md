## LEADS

**1. Scanlon linearity — the theorem, its three ingredients, and the exact failure on E/F_p** `[known]`. *Mechanism.* For a Drinfeld module φ: A = F_q[T] → L{τ} over a finite A-field L with [L:F_q] = n, the map a ↦ φ_a(x) is F_q-linear with an explicit matrix in any F_q-basis of L, so "given x and y = φ_a(x), find a mod Ann(x)" is Gaussian elimination in dimension ≤ rn, cost O((rn)^ω) (Scanlon 2001, recalled; the same linearity makes Drinfeld isogeny computation polynomial — Joux–Narayanan 2019, Wesolowski 2022, recalled). Ingredients: (i) the underlying group scheme is G_a, so module elements carry base-field coordinates; (ii) A is an F_q-algebra and φ is F_q-linear; (iii) the module is an F_q-space of dimension log_q|L|. *Failure when the module is the curve.* For E/F_p with N = #E(F_p) prime, N ≠ p: (i) fails because Hom(E, G_a^m) = 0 (complete → affine is constant), so no algebraic coordinate map is a homomorphism; (ii) fails because Z and Z[ω] have no constant subfield — the only field-linear structure on Z/N is F_N in dimension 1 and its coordinate *is* the discrete log; (iii) fails because that dimension is 1. All three ingredients reappear on the formal group exactly when E(F_p)[p] ≠ 0, i.e. t = 1: Scanlon's theorem is the Drinfeld shadow of Smart/Semaev/Satoh–Araki (KR-RHO-53c89f). *Reduction/exponent.* None new; 0 on the anomalous locus, 1/2 elsewhere. *Obstruction.* Rosenlicht/Chevalley vanishing, already recorded as the transfer-lane "Affine-target vanishing theorem" and the port-lane "Exit-dimension theorem". *Test.* None beyond the recorded CTRL-ANOMALOUS-TRACE1 arm (IDEA-20260805-cc4c2c). Every lead below is judged against this dichotomy; a port that ignores it duplicates the transfer closure.

**2. Carlitz ≠ G_m: the dictionary is not complexity-preserving, but Carlitz is an unconditional sub-√ positive control** `[adaptation]` → RECORD B. *Mechanism.* Carlitz C_T = T + τ is the function-field G_m (C(F_q[T]/P) ≅ A/(P−1), cyclic), yet its DLP is polynomial while F_p^* costs L(1/3): "Drinfeld analogue is easy" carries zero exponent information about E. The surviving use is methodological: C(L) is a cyclic module of size 2^n with generic cost 2^{n/2} and a provable n^ω solve — the only unconditional exponent-0 group available to calibrate the ICEX ladder-slope estimator (IDEA-20260805-4d31bc, -bb4488), which has so far seen exponent-1/2 data and one anomalous control. *Exponent.* 0 on C(L); irrelevant to E. *Obstruction.* `cryptanalysis/include/ca_group.h` is a cyclic-group (Z-action) interface; the Carlitz action is an A-action. *Test.* Slope ≤ 0.05 on the Scanlon arm, 0.50 ± 0.04 on the matched rho arm.

**3. Drinfeld distance k_p = ord_p(t mod p)** `[adaptation]` → RECORD A. *Mechanism.* With unit Frobenius eigenvalue u, t_k ≡ u^k ≡ t^k (mod p), so E(F_{p^k})[p] ≠ 0 iff k_p | k; k_p = 1 iff anomalous; supersingular gives k_p = ∞. The Scanlon-linear locus of E over any extension is E(F_{p^k})[p] with k_p | k. *Reduction.* ECDLP reduces to nothing: even at k = k_p the N-part is a Pohlig–Hellman complement of the p-part. *Exponent.* Working over F_{p^{k_p}} costs ≥ k_p·log p bits per element; prediction k_p ≥ 2^{233} for secp256k1 (q_233 | p−1, recalled). *Obstruction.* Field size and Pohlig–Hellman. *Test.* One exact order computation plus a KS test of k_p against random-unit orders on `docs/ECDLP_CALIBRATION_PRIMES.md` curves.

**4. Carlitz-torsion / additive-subspace factor bases collapse on F_p** `[known]`. *Mechanism.* The function-field analogue of μ_B is Carlitz torsion C[a], an F_q-subspace of L; the binary-field "factor base = subspace V" (KR-IC-73db3f) is its shadow. Over the prime field (the n = 1 case of F_q[T]/(P)), G_a(F_p) = F_p has no proper subgroups and C_a acts as a scalar, so C[a](F_p) ∈ {0, F_p}: no intermediate-size additive base exists. *Exponent.* Not applicable. *Obstruction.* p prime. *Test.* None; it is why KR-IC-73db3f never ported (KR-IC-5931ee).

**5. Norm-one torus base X_B, B | p+1 — the second and last algebraic-subgroup base on the x-line** `[adaptation]` → RECORD C. *Mechanism.* Connected 1-dim groups over F_p living on an open subset of P¹ are G_a, G_m and the nonsplit torus T_2 (forms of G_a over perfect fields are trivial; H¹(Gal, Z/2) gives two tori). Finite subgroups of F_p-points: none for G_a, μ_B for B | p−1 (wave-1 relations record), cyclic of order B | p+1 for T_2. Via x ↦ (x+ι)/(x−ι), ι² = d a non-residue, the order-B subgroup pulls back to X_B = {∞} ∪ roots of D_B(x) = ((x+ι)^B − (x−ι)^B)/(2ι) ∈ F_p[x], degree B−1, all roots in F_p, membership in log₂B F_{p²}-multiplications; the wave-1 resultant tower runs verbatim with D_B in place of X^B − 1. *Reduction.* Same as the μ_B lane: exponent ≥ 1 unless the joint-solve exponent e_k moves. *Exponent delta vs 1/2.* 0 (deployability, not exponent). *Obstruction.* Loses the weighted-homogeneity factor 3 on j = 0 (ι, −ι are not fixed by x ↦ ωx). *Test.* ECM on (p+1)/16 = 2^252 − 2^28 − 61 for secp256k1: a divisor in [2^48, 2^70] makes a 5- or 4-arity base deployable where p−1 = 2·3·7·13441·q_233 (recalled) offered none.

**6. Summation-polynomial degree dichotomy G_a / G_m / E** `[known]`. *Mechanism.* S_m^{G_a} = x_1+…+x_m (degree 1), S_m^{G_m} = x_1⋯x_m − 1 (monomial, so relations are smoothness), S_m^E has degree 2^{m−2} per variable (KR-IC-5a43e5, IDEA-20260805-cf2d5a): the doubling is the non-linearity of the chord law composed m−2 times, and the x-coordinate (degree 2) is the degree-minimising coordinate since a degree-d function gives ≥ d² preimage pairs. *Exponent.* 0 delta; this is the price of lead 1. *Test.* None.

**7. Function-field xedni: lift to a rational elliptic surface over F_p(T)** `[adaptation]` → RECORD D. *Mechanism.* Over Q (KR-IC-cd159a) xedni dies on heights and ranks; over F_p(T) heights are degrees (Shioda–Tate) and MW lattices are explicit, but Silverman's specialisation-injectivity theorem fails outright (finite target): spec: E(F_p(T)) → E_{t_0}(F_p) ≅ Z/N has kernel Λ of index N, and Λ is the hidden relation lattice of the specialised points — knowing Λ ⇔ knowing their logs. The section through a given Q at t_0 has height ≈ N^{2/r} and finding it is an r-sum problem of size 2^rN; checking a candidate by evaluating the section at t_0 costs the same as the group-law computation it certifies. *Exponent needed.* A short vector of Λ not implied by an F_p(T)-relation; prediction: λ_1(Λ) is the Gaussian heuristic of r random points. *Obstruction.* GGM: homomorphic certificates are group-law tautologies (IDEA-20260805-5a9d2c: XEDN relations all ∞-norm 1). *Test.* Full log table at p ≈ 2^20, LLL on Λ for r ≤ 8 surfaces from the census of IDEA-20260805-887d67 (`run_census.sage`), random-point control.
