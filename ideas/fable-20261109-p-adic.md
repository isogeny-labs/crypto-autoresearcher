## LEADS

**Common ground for every lead (why Smart/Satoh–Araki/Semaev do not touch E(F_p), p > 3, even though secp256k1 is ordinary).** "Ordinary" means p ∤ t; "anomalous" means t = 1 exactly — a special ordinary curve. secp256k1 has t = p + 1 − N ≈ 1.27·2^128, so N < p, k < p, and k mod p = k: there is no CRT between N and p to exploit. Smart's map P ↦ t([N]P̃)/p mod p is well defined iff [N]E_1 ⊂ E_2 iff p | N; for p ∤ N it equals N·d_L(P), where d_L(P) ∈ F_p is the fibre coordinate of the chosen lift rule relative to the unique torsion section (Ext¹(Z/N, Z_p) = Z_p/N = 0, Hom(Z/N, Z_p) = 0), so Ẽ(Q_p) ≅ Z/N ⊕ Z_p and the lifted DL splits into a free Z_p-component (computed exactly by the formal log) and the untouched Z/N-component. Semaev's variant needs df_P/f_P, div f_P = N(P) − N(O), to be holomorphic; its residues are ±N ≠ 0 in F_p. In Z[ω]-terms: Z[ω] ⊗ Z_p = Z_p × Z_p, π ↦ (u, p/u) with u ≡ t (mod p), so both images of π − 1 are units iff t ≢ 1; the anomalous attack is the case where (π − 1) acquires a place above p. All four records below are precision controls or refutation measurements on this picture; none moves the exponent 1/2 (KR-RHO-13bf67), and all say so.
