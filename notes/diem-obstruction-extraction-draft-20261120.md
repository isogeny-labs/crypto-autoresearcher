## A. Hypotheses inventory (numbered, with paper/section references)

Standing notation in both papers: k = 𝔽_q, K = 𝔽_{qⁿ}, q a prime power, n ∈ ℕ. In Diem I, `log₂(q)` is **log base 2 of q** (not iterated log) — verified by the papers' own case-distinction arithmetic (see B). σ denotes the (relative) Frobenius of k̄|k, x ↦ x^q.

### Diem I (Compositio)

- **A1. Factor base (Eq. (1), §1 "On the proof")**: F = {P ∈ E(𝔽_{qⁿ}) | φ(P) ∈ ℙ¹(𝔽_q)}, where φ: E → ℙ¹_{𝔽_{qⁿ}} is a degree-2 covering with φ∘[−1] = φ. Size ≤ 2(q+1) (stated at §2.3, Step 5 discussion).
- **A2. Prop 2.1 / Def 2.2 (m-th summation polynomial)**: for φ of degree 2 with φ∘[−1]=φ and m ≥ 2 there is a unique-irreducible multihomogeneous S_{φ,m} ∈ k[X₁,Y₁,…,X_m,Y_m] of multidegree (2^{m−2},…,2^{m−2}) with S_{φ,m}(φ(P₁),…,φ(P_m)) = 0 ⟺ ∃εᵢ∈{±1}: Σ εᵢPᵢ = O.
- **A3. Prop 2.6**: the decomposition algorithm (solve V(S^{(1)},…,S^{(n)}) ⊆ (ℙ¹_k)ⁿ via Rojas' twisted-Chow-form algorithm) runs in expected time Poly(e^{n²}·log q) **[reading note: superscript on e]**.
- **A4. Def 2.4 (φ-isolated decomposition)**: a decomposition (P₁,…,P_n), Pᵢ ∈ E(K), Σ Pᵢ = P, φ(Pᵢ) ∈ ℙ¹(k), is **φ-isolated** if (φ(P₁),…,φ(P_n)) is an *isolated point* of the k-scheme V(S^{(1)},…,S^{(n)}) ⊆ (ℙ¹_k)ⁿ, where the S^{(j)} are the k-basis expansions of S_{φ,n+1}(·,…,·,φ(P)) (Eq. (2)).
- **A5. Condition 2.7 (§2.1, verbatim)**: *"There exists a point R ∈ ℙ¹(k̄) which is a ramification point of φ such that the points R, σ(R), …, σ^{n−1}(R) are all distinct and φ is unramified at σ(R), …, σ^{n−1}(R)."* **[reading note]** The extraction renders "ℙ¹(k̄)" as "P1 (Fq )" and "k̄|k" as "k|k"; overlines are dropped by pdftotext. R ∈ ℙ¹(𝔽_q) literally would be inconsistent (Frobenius fixes 𝔽_q); the construction of §2.2.1 (take a ∈ 𝔽_{qⁿ} in no proper subfield; R := a) and Diem II's equivalent form (Lemma 2.6, A16 below) confirm the k̄/F_{qⁿ}-rational reading. This is the **distinct-Frobenius-conjugates-of-a-ramification-point ramification condition** the WP brief anticipated; the number is exactly 2.7.
- **A6. Prop 2.8**: for (q,n) ≠ (3,2) a φ of degree 2 with φ∘[−1]=φ **satisfying Condition 2.7** can be computed in expected time Poly(n·log q). (§2.2; even characteristic: translate the unique branch point to an a ∈ 𝔽_{qⁿ} outside all proper subfields; odd characteristic: random λ, φ = (x−λ)|_E.)
- **A7. Lemma 2.10 (feasibility of A5)**: for (q,n) ∉ {(2,2),(3,2),(2,3),(2,4)}, a random λ ∈ 𝔽_{qⁿ} satisfies (λ₁−λ)^{qⁱ} ∉ {λ₁−λ, λ₂−λ, λ₃−λ}, i=1,…,n−1, with probability ≥ C > 0 (branch points λᵢ ∈ 𝔽_{q^{6n}}). Note: this holds for **n = 1 trivially/vacuously** (no condition to check).
- **A8. Prop 2.9 (relation-density / decomposition proposition — the headline hypothesis)**: *Let ε > 0. For n large enough and (2+ε)·n² ≤ log₂(q), let E/𝔽_{qⁿ} and φ (deg 2, φ∘[−1]=φ) satisfy Condition 2.7. Then the probability that a uniform point of E(𝔽_{qⁿ}) has a φ-isolated decomposition is at least q^{−1/2}* **[reading note: exponent printed with split fraction; cost accounting at §2.3 Step 6 ("expected number of iterations … is in O(q^{1/2})") and Prop 4.29 (≥ ½·q^{n−1/2} decomposable points out of ≈ qⁿ) fix the reading]**. Footnote 2: "for n large enough" = ∃C: statement holds for all n ≥ C. So the density theorem has standing hypotheses: **ε > 0; n ≥ C (absolute); (2+ε)·n² ≤ log₂ q** (i.e. q ≥ 2^{(2+ε)n²}); Condition 2.7.
- **A9. Prop 2.11 (index calculus under the same regime)**: given q, n with (2+ε)·n² ≤ log₂(q), E/𝔽_{qⁿ}, A,B ∈ E(𝔽_{qⁿ}), B ∈ ⟨A⟩: output discrete log in expected time polynomially bounded in q. Derived by running the algorithm (Steps 1–10, incl. Step 4 = choose φ with Condition 2.7, Step 5 = enumerate F) in parallel with brute force; the *theorem* additionally runs over all factorizations qⁿ "in parallel".
- **A10. Prop 2.12 + Lemma 2.13 (invertibility/uniformity)**: conditional on Step 5's outcome, β′₁ is uniform in ℤ/Nℤ (standard linear-algebra lemma; no geometric content).
- **A11. Prop 3.2 (summation hypersurface)**: H = H_{φ,m} ⊆ (ℙ¹_k)^m, image of the addition kernel N ⊆ E^m: N/[−1] → H finite birational; H a hypersurface of multidegree (2^{m−2},…,2^{m−2}); projections to any m−1 factors flat coverings of degree 2^{m−2}. Prop 3.7: m-th summation polynomial computable in Poly(e^{m²}·log q) time for m ≥ 3, q ≥ 2^{m−2} **[reading note: superscript]**.
- **A12. §4 geometric hypotheses (the zero-dimensionality machinery)**:
  - **Lemma 4.7 (permanent Bézout)**: for multihomogeneous F₁,…,F_n of multidegrees (d_{i,j}), the zero-cycle [V(F₁)]···[V(F_n)] has degree per(D) (permanent); in the uniform case (d,…,d): n!·dⁿ; equality for the degree-zero part ⟺ V(F₁,…,F_n) is zero-dimensional.
  - **Prop 4.9 (Condition 2.7 ⇒ integrality)**: under Condition 2.7, V := ℙ¹_k ×_{Res(ℙ¹_K)} Res(E) (Cartesian square (3)) is **geometrically reduced and geometrically irreducible**.   - **Prop 4.28** (as **corrected in Diem II's appendix**): for M ⊆ {(P₁,…,P_n) | φ(Pᵢ) ∈ ℙ¹(k)}, the number of P ∈ E(K) with a φ-isolated decomposition (P₁,…,P_n) of ±P, Pᵢ ∈ M, is ≥ (#M − n³·n!·2^{2n−n}·(q+1)^{n−1}) / (n!·2^{n²}) — the Diem I print omitted the factor n! in the subtrahend; "All following bounds are correct again" (Diem II appendix).
  - **Prop 4.29 (terminal density statement)**: for ε > 0, n large enough and (2+ε)·n² ≤ log₂(q): at least ½·q^{n−1/2} elements of E(K) have φ-isolated decompositions. "This implies Proposition 2.9."

## B. Main theorems and coverage boundaries

### Diem I (unnumbered "Theorem", p. 76, plus intro results (i)–(iii))

- **Theorem** (verbatim): *"The discrete logarithm problem in the groups of rational points of elliptic curves over finite fields 𝔽_{qⁿ} can be solved in an expected time of e^{Õ(max(log(q), n)²)}."* (Diem II's Theorem 1 restates it with the precise semantics: a single machine and an **absolute constant C** bounding the exponent; "the expected value concerns only the internal choices of the computation; there is no averaging over input classes" — Diem II §1, "Some more information on the results".) All results hold **for all specified instances**, including n = 1 — but see the degeneracy remark below.
- **Result (i)**: for sequences with **nᵢ → ∞ and nᵢ/log(qᵢ) → 0**: expected time (qᵢ^{nᵢ})^{o(1)} — the "first subexponential sequences" claim. Subexponentiality is genuinely delivered only in this window: the proof-regime condition (2+ε)n² ≤ log₂ q (or its case-2 repair at cost Poly(q^m), m ≤ 6n²/log₂ q, i.e. time e^{O(n²)}) requires q exponential in n² to run the geometry as-is, and the subexponentials compare against the group bit-size n·log q.
- **Result (ii)**: β ∈ [½, 1), α := (1−β)/(2β), γ := (2β/(β+1))·(1−1/(β+1)) < 1: for a·log(q)^α ≤ n ≤ b·log(q)^β, time e^{Õ((log qⁿ)^γ)}.
- **Result (iii)**: a·√(log q) ≤ n ≤ b·√(log q): time e^{Õ((log qⁿ)^{2/3})}.
- **Abstract addendum**: "there exists a sequence of prime powers (qᵢ) such that the problem restricted to curves over **𝔽_{qᵢ²}** can be solved in an expected time of e^{O(log(qᵢ)^{2/3})}" — a *sparse sequence* at extension degree 2.
- **What Diem I does NOT cover**:
  - The analytic core (Props 2.9, 2.11, 4.11, 4.29) assumes **n large enough (n ≥ C, absolute)**, **(2+ε)n² ≤ log₂ q** (and Prop 4.11 explicitly **n ≥ 2, log₂ q ≥ 7n**), plus Condition 2.7. Fixed small n — in particular n = 1 — is outside the density theorems.
  - For instances outside the regime (including everything with small n) the theorem is rescued **by brute force / parallel running over factorizations** (§2.3, and §1: the algorithm for Prop 2.11 is applied "in parallel with a brute force computation"). Consequently, at n = 1 the headline bound e^{Õ(log² q)} is *vacuously weaker than Pollard ρ* (e^{Õ(√log q)}); the theorem's coverage of n = 1 is nominal only. The factor base (1) at n = 1 equals **all of E(𝔽_q)** (φ defined over 𝔽_q), size ≈ q: listing it already costs Poly(q) time and every relation is available at once — the construction compresses nothing. This is an explicit degeneracy of *this* construction, proved nowhere as a general impossibility.
  - No non-existence statement of any kind. The word "diagonal" appears **nowhere** in Diem I (grep-verified); there is no diagonal-hypersurface discussion, no lower bound, no statement that factor bases/correspondences cannot exist for prime fields. The closest is the historical remark (p. 77): Semaev's prime-field approach "does (**currently**) not lead to an algorithm which is faster than generic algorithms" — a statement of the art, not a theorem. Footnote 1 cites [Die09] (Math. Comp. 80) as *proving Gaudry's heuristic Õ(q^{2−2/n})-type claim for fixed n ≥ 2* — fixed n ≥ 2 again, never n = 1.

### Diem II

- **Theorem 1** = Diem I's theorem (e^{Õ(max(log q,n)²)}) — quoted as background.
- **Theorem 2 (main)**: *"The discrete logarithm problem in the groups of rational points of elliptic curves over finite fields 𝔽_{qⁿ} can be solved in an expected time of e^{Õ(max(log q, n·log(q)^{1/2}, n^{3/2}))}."* Piecewise: log q for n ≤ log(q)^{1/2}; n·log(q)^{1/2} for log(q)^{1/2} ≤ n ≤ log q; n^{3/2} for log q ≤ n.
- **Corollaries (i)–(iii)**: (i) **nᵢ → ∞ with nᵢ/log(qᵢ)² → 0** ⇒ (qᵢ^{nᵢ})^{o(1)} [enlarges Diem I's window from n = o(log q) to n = o((log q)²)]; (ii) β ∈ [½,1], α = 1/(2β+1), θ = (β+½)/(β+1) (maximal ¾ at α = β = ½): a·log(q)^α ≤ n ≤ b·log(q)^β ⇒ e^{Õ((log qⁿ)^θ)}; in particular **a·log(q)^{1/3} ≤ n ≤ b·log q ⇒ e^{Õ((log qⁿ)^{3/4})}** (the brief's abstract line); (iii) β ∈ [1,2), α = (2−β)/(3β), θ = (3/2)·β/(1+β): covers windows above log q.
- **Theorem 3 (fixed characteristic)**: *Let p be a fixed prime. ECDLP over 𝔽_{p^{mn}} in expected time e^{Õ(max(m, n, min(m·n^{1/2}, n·m^{1/2})))}* — obtained by **factoring the absolute extension degree as mn** and using the better of the two views. Corollaries: (iv) mᵢ, nᵢ → ∞ ⇒ (p^{mᵢnᵢ})^{o(1)}; (v) α ≥ 3, m ≤ a·n^α, n ≤ b·m^α ⇒ e^{Õ((log p^{mn})^{1−1/(1+α)})}.
- **The explicit prime-field non-coverage statement (Diem II §1, p. 1283, verbatim)**: *"We first remark that Theorem 2 does not give a nontrivial result if q is set to p and n is set to the absolute extension degree of the ground field. We therefore consider a factorization of the absolute extension degree in the form mn…"* — i.e. the author himself states that reading q = p, n = [K:𝔽_p] (the prime-field case when [K:𝔽_p] = 1, and the "genuine absolute degree" reading in general) puts the method outside nontrivial range; all Diem II subexponential statements then require the degree to be **composite with both factors growing** (iv), or n ≥ c·log(q)^{1/3} etc. — in particular n → ∞.
- **Internal limitation statements (not obstructions, but scope admissions)**:
  - §1 Outline (verbatim): *"Unfortunately, even with a modified algorithm we cannot prove that one can obtain the expected running time one might expect by heuristic considerations. Indeed, in odd characteristic we can only complete the analysis under the condition that [2^{c·n} ≤ q] for a suitable constant c > 0. In even characteristic the situation is more fortunate and we can complete the analysis if [n^c ≤ q] for a suitable constant c > 0."* **[reading note: exponents partially garbled in extraction; consistent with Prop 4.8's 2^{5n} ≤ q / n³ ≤ q. The even-characteristic qualification is then "not improved by (19)/(20)": §4 states Theorem 3 improves on the even-q variant when m ∈ o(n).]**
  - §2B (verbatim): *"For d = 1, the morphism (V′_a)^δ × V_a^{m−δ} → Res(E) is surjective and therefore, if V_a and V′_a are irreducible, the first item is satisfied; see [Diem 2011b, Remark 4.21]. **However, for d > 1, we cannot even give an example for which we can prove that the first condition holds.** For this reason, we modify the definition of the factor base."* — an honesty statement about what he *cannot prove about his own preliminary construction*; **not** a non-existence theorem, and it concerns subspace-pullback varieties over 𝔽_{qⁿ}, not covers over 𝔽_p.
  - §1 preliminary algorithm: success rests on the explicitly stated heuristic that most 𝔽_q-solutions are isolated.

The n ≥ 2 / m ≥ 2 / 2 ≤ m ≤ n/2 / n ≥ 4 conditions are well-definedness and probability-margin conditions of the construction, and A26's 2^{5n} ≤ q is an *analysis-sufficiency* condition that the theorem statements then bypass by base-change or brute force, always at the cost of losing subexponentiality (which only ever lives in the n → ∞ windows).

## C. Geometric construction summary

**Diem I.** The whole mechanism is anchored on a degree-**2** cover φ: E → ℙ¹_{𝔽_{qⁿ}} with φ∘[−1] = φ (§2.2: φ = α∘x|_E with α ∈ Aut(ℙ¹) a translation/twist chosen so that Condition 2.7 holds). There is **no auxiliary degree-n cover of E and no cover of a prime-field model**. The "cover" that does the work is produced by **Weil restriction (scalar restriction)** and Frobenius:

1. Factor base = V(𝔽_q) under E(K) ≅ Res^k_K(E)(k), where V := ℙ¹_k ×_{(Res ℙ¹_K)} Res(E) via the canonical immersion ι: ℙ¹_k ↪ Res^k_K(ℙ¹_K) (diagram (3)). After base change to k̄: **V_k̄ ≅ E ×_{ℙ¹} σ(E) ×_{ℙ¹} ⋯ ×_{ℙ¹} σ^{n−1}(E)** — the fiber product of the Frobenius twists of E over ℙ¹ (Prop 4.9 proof, Eq. (4)); its normalization π: C → V is a degree-2ⁿ (Z/2)-type elementary-abelian cover C → ℙ¹.
2. **Role of R and its distinct Frobenius conjugates**: R a ramification point of φ means the place R ramifies k(E)/k(x); requiring R, σR, …, σ^{n−1}R distinct *and* φ unramified at σⁱR makes the i-th twist ramify at a place where the previous compositum is unramified — hence each twist contributes a fresh factor of 2 (degree 2ⁿ compositum), i.e. **V geometrically integral** (Prop 4.9), with **X geometrically irreducible** in the relation scheme (Prop 4.16). Genus of C is ≤ (2n−1)(2ⁿ−1) by Kani–Rosen summation over quadratic subextensions with Artin–Schreier/tame ramification counts (Prop 4.10), so **Hasse–Weil on C** yields #V(k) ≥ ½(q+1) whenever n ≥ 2, log₂ q ≥ 7n (Prop 4.11). Without Condition 2.7 this integrality (and hence the density count) is not available — the paper never claims it fails, but all counting theorems die with it.
3. **Relations**: the summation hypersurface H_{φ,n+1} ⊆ (ℙ¹)^{n+1} (image of the addition kernel in E^{n+1}; Prop 3.2) is scalar-restricted and intersected with (ιⁿ × id) to give X ⊆ (ℙ¹_k)ⁿ × Res(ℙ¹_K); X(k) parametrizes tuples (Q₁,…,Q_n,Q) of "degree-1-with-respect-to-φ" points summing to Q. A **decomposition is φ-isolated iff its tuple is an isolated k-point** of the fiber (Def 2.4); a permanent-Bézout bound (Lemma 4.7: n!·dⁿ, d = 2^{n−1}) bounds total isolated tuples, and the exceptional divisor B (Lemma 4.26) carves out the (n−1)-dimensional bad locus whose (q+1)^{n−1}·(n³·n!·2^{2(n−1)n}) points are excluded; comparing #V(k)ⁿ/2ⁿ against #B(k)·(fiber degree n!·2^{(n−1)n}) yields ≥ ½q^{n−1/2} decomposable points under (2+ε)n² ≤ log₂ q (Props 4.28, 4.29). The Diem I introduction flags this as the hard part: showing the system is zero-dimensional "with a sufficiently high probability" — "the most difficult part of the proof" — via "intersection theory in products of projective lines" on Weil restrictions.
4. Diem's framing (§1, verbatim): "the factor base is defined in an **algebraic** rather than an arithmetic way; in particular, there is **no smoothness bound**"; and "one might argue that we implicitly use the isomorphism E(𝔽_{qⁿ}) ≅ Res^{𝔽_{qⁿ}}_{𝔽_q}(E)(𝔽_q) … Weil restrictions are of crucial importance for the **analysis** of the algorithm, but the algorithm itself can be formulated without even mentioning Weil restrictions."

**Diem II.** Same index-calculus skeleton; the geometry of the factor base changes:

1. The factor base is **enlarged** from "φ(P) ∈ ℙ¹(𝔽_q)" to **x(P) ∈ Uᵢ**, a union of F_q-vector subspaces Uᵢ ⊂ 𝔽_{qⁿ} with K = ⊔Uᵢ, m ≈ √(log₂ q) pieces of dimension d ≈ n/m. Geometrically each Fᵢ corresponds to Vᵢ(k), Vᵢ := (𝔸_k[Uᵢ]) ×_{𝔸_k[K]} Res^k_K(E_a), where 𝔸_k[Uᵢ] ⊆ 𝔸_k[K] = Res^k_K(𝔸¹_K) are **group subvarieties of the Weil-restricted affine line** (basis-free description via 𝔸_k[V] := Spec k[V^∨]; §1 "Weil restrictions and the scalar restriction functor" — verbatim: "The application of the scalar restriction functor, that is, the formation of Weil restrictions, **is crucial** in this work").
2. **Condition 2.1** (0 unramified with two K-rational preimages) makes Vᵢ → 𝔸_k[Uᵢ] a degree-2ⁿ flat covering **unramified over 0**, which is the base point of the tangent-space criterion; Prop 2.2 proves the restricted addition map a′_m: V₁×⋯×V_m → Res(E) is unramified at ((P₀)^},…,(P₀)^}) by identifying T₀(𝔸_k[V]) ≅ V, hence is **dominant** (Remark 2.3). Dominance is what "most fibers are zero-dimensional" needs.
3. **Irreducibility via Diem I's ramification mechanism, on lines only**: each Uᵢ is built to contain a *line* Tᵢ = μᵢ⁻¹·𝔽_q whose preimage W_a is geometrically irreducible by Diem I's Condition 2.7(II)-mechanism (Lemma 2.6, citing Diem I Prop 4.9); Lemma 2.5 propagates irreducibility from the line to all of Vᵢ. (In even characteristic the lines are set to {0}: the ramification trick is characteristic-specific.)
4. **Decomposition without summation polynomials**: membership in Fᵢ plus ΣPᵢ = P is encoded by principal divisors of functions in L((m+1)O·P) (Riemann–Roch; E(K) ≅ Cl⁰(E)); "isolated" = isolated reduced k-point of the fiber (V₁×⋯×V_m)_{P^} (Def 3.2). Bound ≤ e^{C·mn} isolated decompositions (Prop 3.3); the ramification determinant polynomial d of the addition map cuts the non-isolated locus, of size O(n⁵4ⁿ q^{n−1}) (odd q, Prop 4.2/4.5), O(2^m n² q^{n−1}) (even q, j≠0, Prop 4.6), and **0** (even q, j = 0: dx|_E holomorphic ⇒ a′_m everywhere unramified ⇒ "every decomposition is isolated", Prop 4.7). Against #F₁×⋯×F_m ≥ 4^{−n}qⁿ this yields success probability 1/e^{O(mn)} under 2^{5n} ≤ q (or the even-q conditions) (Prop 4.8).
5. Key structural contrast for the research program: both papers' "covers" are built from **Frobenius-twist fiber products over ℙ¹ of the degree-2 x-cover of E defined over the extension field 𝔽_{qⁿ}**, plus **subspace preimages in the Weil restriction** — the n of the extension degree is consumed as the *number of twists* (Diem I) or the *dimension of the restricted space split into m pieces* (Diem II). Neither paper constructs, rules out, or comments on covers ϕ: C → E′ over a **prime** base field.

## E. Verdict

**No.** Neither Diem I nor Diem II contains a proposition with prime-field contrapositive force for correspondence-based / auxiliary-cover relation engines.

The precise statements examined and their status:

1. The strongest-sounding candidate, **Diem II §1 (p. 1283)**: *"We first remark that Theorem 2 does not give a nontrivial result if q is set to p and n is set to the absolute extension degree of the ground field."* — This is a **scope remark about his own theorem** (the subexponential windows require n growing with q), not an obstruction to any method over 𝔽_p. It closes precisely one door: the claim "Diem's theorems apply to ECDLP over prime fields." It does not close (and does not come near closing) any door for 𝔽_p correspondence engines.
2. **Diem I Condition 2.7** and its Diem II reformulation (A5, A19): at n = 1 the condition becomes vacuous (a single ramification point; empty conjugacy check), and Lemma 2.10 shows it is satisfiable for all n ≥ 1 anyway. A condition that is *satisfiable* at n = 1 cannot obstruct at n = 1; what happens at n = 1 is that the condition's *payoff* evaporates (V = E; factor base = whole group; see D6), which is a compression/complexity degeneracy of Diem's own construction, not a non-existence result.
3. **Diem II §2B** ("we cannot even give an example for which we can prove that the first condition holds" for d > 1): a recorded limitation-of-proof for one preliminary construction — usable rhetorically at most; it proves nothing.
4. All hard analytic hypotheses — "(2+ε)·n² ≤ log₂(q)" and "n large enough" (Diem I, Props 2.9/2.11/4.29), "n ≥ 2, log₂(q) ≥ 7n" (Diem I, Prop 4.11), "2 ≤ m ≤ n/2, q ≥ 4, n ≥ 4" (Diem II, §2E/Prop 2.10), "2^{5n} ≤ q" (Diem II, Prop 4.8) — are **sufficiency conditions for density of isolated decompositions** in the twist-fiber-product / subspace-pullback family. None is proved necessary; none is shown to fail at n = 1 (most are undefined or vacuous there); the theorems are then extended to *all* instances (including n = 1) by brute-force parallel fallbacks precisely because the geometry need not say anything there.
5. **No non-existence apparatus appears anywhere**: grep over the full text of both papers finds no "diagonal", no lower bounds, no statements of the form "no factor base / no correspondence / no family of relations exists", and no discussion of obstructions to applicability beyond the four items above.

**Consequence for G31-005 WP1**: The empirical negatives for bielliptic/Plücker/Prym/Kummer/Hesse/split-Jacobian relation engines over 𝔽_p **cannot** be upgraded to theorem-scoped closures by citing Diem I or Diem II. The papers establish the opposite direction of housekeeping only: they (a) prove rigorously that Diem's own family achieves subexponential ECDLP exactly when the extension degree grows (n → ∞ with n = o((log q)²) in Diem II, and related windows), (b) explicitly decline prime-field coverage (E-item 1), and (c) exhibit, as arithmetic of one construction, why a {x ∈ 𝔽_q}-type algebraic factor base gives no compression at n = 1 (D6). Anything stronger would need new input; the correct citation posture is "not-covered", and any note claiming "Diem proves an obstruction for n = 1" should be flagged as unsupported.

(ii) Diem I Prop 4.10(a)'s genus bound factors are quoted as (2n−1)(2ⁿ−1) on the reconstructed reading. (iii) Diem II Prop 4.8's displayed probability carries an explicit "(1 − 1/O(mn))-type" second factor that could not be disambiguated glyph-by-glyph; the proof's closing line ("in 1/e^{O(mn)}") is quoted instead. (iv) arXiv does not host either paper; the brief's arXiv ID 1108.6316 belongs to a different paper (Yamabe solitons) and the two Leipzig mirror URLs are dead — MSP and Cambridge publisher PDFs were used instead, both at full-text depth.
