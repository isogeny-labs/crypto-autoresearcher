# G31-002 — Characteristic-0 supply barrier: what actually closes, and what does not

**Date:** 2026-11-21 · **Status:** analysis note, unreviewed. This note *corrects*
the G31-002 proposal text: the screening's "unconditional no-sub-rho" claim is
**not** established, and the reason is instructive. Nothing here is filed as a
barrier until the §5 scoping is accepted by review.

**Claim of this note, in one sentence.** Counting alone closes the
fixed-decomposition-length, polynomial-height lift family and nothing more;
the remaining hole is a *decomposition-solver* question that counting cannot
reach, and stating the barrier any wider would be false.

---

## 1. Setting

Let E/F_p be an elliptic curve over a prime field, G ⊆ E(F_p) the prime-order
target subgroup. Fix an integral model Ẽ/ℚ (or over a number field K of degree
d) reducing to E. A characteristic-0 factor base at height budget h is

  B(h) = { P ∈ Ẽ(ℚ) : ĥ(P) ≤ h },

ĥ the canonical (Néron–Tate) height; the usable base is the reduction B̄(h) ⊆
E(F_p), and the attacker's relation is R = Σ_{i≤m} P̄_i with P_i ∈ B(h) and m
fixed or slowly growing. All costs below are charged in group operations, per
G30-001.

## 2. Supply lemma (the only genuinely available input)

**Lemma S (citation-conditional).** For an elliptic curve over a number field
with a rational ℓ-torsion point and ℓ = 2 over ℚ, Dujella (Acta Arith., 2024)
gives #{P : H(P) ≤ B} ≤ exp(C log B / log log B), C = C(K). Translating
B = e^h:

  |B(h)| ≤ exp(C h / log h) · (torsion factor).

Masser (Bull. SMF 117, 1989) supplies effective counting bounds in the general
(no rational torsion) case; the exact form is **not** transcribed here and must
be read at source before any theorem cites it. **Status: conditional on the
source read.** This note therefore treats Lemma S as a *named hypothesis*
H_count, with the Dujella form as its explicit instance.

**Consequence at polynomial height.** h = c log p gives

  |B(h)| ≤ p^{Cc/log log p + o(1)} = p^{o(1)},

i.e. sub-polynomial in p — but **not** polylogarithmic. This distinction is
where the original proposal went wrong (see §4).

## 3. Coverage and density (trivial, and not where the difficulty is)

The set of points reachable as sums of m base elements has size ≤ |B|^m, so for
a uniformly random target the decomposition density is

  ρ(m, h) ≤ |B(h)|^m / #G.

To have ρ ≳ 1 one needs m ≥ log(#G)/log|B(h)|. At polynomial height and
|B| = p^{Cc/log log p} this is m ≈ (log p)/(Cc) · log log p / log p =
**m = Θ(log log p)**. So the decomposition *length* required at polynomial
height is unbounded but grows only like log log p — small. Counting alone
therefore does **not** kill the route: with m = Θ(loglog p), ρ can reach
p^{-1+O(c)} for suitable c, i.e. a constant power of p.

## 4. The free-solver fallacy (the correction)

The standard index-calculus cost formula

  total ≈ (number of relations needed) / ρ = N / ρ = p / N^{m-1}

charges **one group operation per candidate**, i.e. it assumes the attacker can
test (or find) a decomposition of a candidate in O(1)–O(m) work. Over extension
fields that assumption is earned by the algebraic structure of the base
(subfield coordinates) and the resulting polynomial system, whose solving cost
is the dominant and *analysed* term (Gaudry; Diem; Faugeron–Perret–Petit–
Renault). Over a prime field with a height-ordered base there is **no known
analogue**: the natural algorithms are enumeration over B(h)^m (cost N^m) or a
lattice/closest-vector heuristic on coordinates, which does not respect the
group law.

Consequently:

- Any claim of the form "characteristic-0 lift bases give no sub-rho advantage"
  that rests only on |B(h)| ≤ p^{o(1)} is **false as stated** once m grows,
  because the counting ceiling with m = Θ(loglog p) can reach a constant power
  of p, and the cost formula then reads sub-rho *if the solver is free*.
- The correct statement charges the solver, and the solver cost is exactly the
  quantity the program already measures on the prime-field axis (G30-005/006,
  KN-OPEN-002). The characteristic-0 question therefore *reduces* to the
  prime-field degree question rather than side-stepping it.

## 5. What is actually closed (the scoped certificate)

**Theorem (scoped).** Fix m ≤ m_max with m_max an absolute constant (the range
in which Semaev polynomials are computable at all; S₆ is the published record,
and §G31-004 measured S₄ explicitly). Let the base be B(h) with h ≤ c log p and
suppose H_count. Then the relation density is at most

  ρ ≤ p^{-1 + C c m_max / log log p + o(1)},

so the number of candidate targets needed is at least p^{1 - o(1)}, and with the
per-candidate solver charged at ≥ 1 group operation, the relation-collection
phase alone costs ≥ p^{1-o(1)} ≫ p^{1/2}. **No sub-rho advantage.** ∎

**Required-height lemma (quantitative form of the wall).** For fixed m, to make
|B(h)| ≥ p^{1/(2m)} one needs exp(C h/log h) ≥ p^{1/(2m)}, i.e.

  h ≥ (log p)(log log p)/(2 m C) · (1 + o(1)),

a power of p, not a polylog. The "low height" premise fails quantitatively, not
just asymptotically.

**Explicitly NOT closed:**

1. m growing with p (m = Θ(loglog p)). The counting ceiling permits a constant
   power of p; closing this needs a solver-cost lower bound, not a supply bound.
2. Any lift family whose rank/regulator grows with p (the constants in
   H_count depend on the model; a growing rank is the one genuine loophole, and
   it is a well-posed open problem: can a lift of E/F_p have Mordell–Weil rank
   growing like log p/loglog p? — heuristics say no, but no theorem).
3. Degree d of the lifting field growing with p (the counting constants worsen
   with d; only a bounded-degree statement is covered).
4. Any setting in which a height-aware decomposition oracle exists — by
   definition, and this is the honest boundary of the barrier.

## 6. Consequence for the goal and for the portfolio

- **G31-002 is re-scoped** from "characteristic-0 supply barrier (unconditional)"
  to "fixed-length characteristic-0 closure + reduction of the general case to
  the prime-field solver question". The measurement arm (bounded-height
  enumeration at 20–24 bits, validating §5's required-height numbers) remains
  useful and unchanged in design.
- **Do not file** any statement of the form "lifting to ℚ cannot help" without
  the fixed-m and bounded-rank qualifiers. The screening draft's stronger
  sentence is withdrawn in the batch file.
- **Connection to existing work:** the residual question is the same one
  G30-005/006 measure (first-fall/solving degree for prime-field Semaev
  systems). A characteristic-0 route that evaded the solver wall would have to
  supply a decomposition algorithm whose cost beats p/N^{m-1}; that is a
  concrete, testable target rather than a vague hope, and it is the correct
  successor probe if this lane is ever re-opened.

## 7. Falsification

The scoped theorem is falsified if a fixed-m, bounded-rank, polynomial-height
characteristic-0 construction achieves a fully charged relation cost below
c·p^{1/2}. The §5 reduction is falsified if a decomposition algorithm exists
whose per-candidate cost is o(p/N^{m-1}) without exploiting structure
isomorphic to the extension-field case — that algorithm would reopen the whole
lane, and identifying it is worth more than any barrier.
