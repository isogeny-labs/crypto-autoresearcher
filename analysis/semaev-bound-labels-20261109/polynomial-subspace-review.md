# Independent review: explicit polynomial subspace

Date: 2026-11-09. Source-only review of `polynomial-subspace-candidate.md`. No execution.

**The exact two-point construction is correct for r>=1, n>4r, and irreducible p of degree n.** The largest coefficient-polynomial degree is at most 4r, so equality in the quotient field is equality in F2[t]. The proposed UFD square-root descent is valid, including its separate zero-factor case.

There is a simpler proof, also identified by the parent during review. If x and y are both nonconstant with degrees a,b>0, the term (xy)² has degree 2(a+b), strictly greater than each of deg(xy), deg(x²), deg(y²), and deg(1). Its nonzero leading term cannot cancel. Thus at least one of x,y is constant. Over F2 its value is zero or one: F(x,0)=x²+1 forces x=1, and F(x,1)=x forces x=0, with the symmetric cases identical. Hence the only solutions are (0,1),(1,0).

The Boolean ideal is therefore precisely the affine line ideal generated by all nonconstant-coordinate bits and x_0+y_0+1, together with field equations in the ordinary polynomial presentation. The normalized Boolean descent remains quadratic even though polynomial degrees in t reach 4r. Here k=r+1 and n>4r; the earlier odd-diagonal hypotheses and degree-two codimension/lower-bound formulas do not apply.

## A checked small-dimension degree implication

For r=1 write x=a+bt and y=c+dt. The nonzero polynomial-coordinate rows are

    a+c+1,
    ad+bc,
    ad+bc+bd+b+d,
    bd.

Thus b+d is in their span. At cap two, the affine relations c=a+1 and d=b can be substituted using products of available affine consequences with affine cofactors; these products satisfy the strict degree-two rule. The row ad+bc then becomes b. Consequently b,d,a+c+1, the entire affine ideal basis, are available at cap two, giving LFD<=2. At cap one the only nonzero affine input is a+c+1; b is missing. Therefore this original coordinate presentation has **exact LFD2** for r=1.

This is an algebraic derivation, not a new run. It already prevents transferring the earlier containing-1 lower bound three to this different dimension regime. It does not decide the derivation degree for unbounded r. No constant all-r upper bound or growing lower bound is asserted by this review.
