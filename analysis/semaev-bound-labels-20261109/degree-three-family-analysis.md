# Degree-three analysis of the count-controlled family

Date: 2026-11-09. Source-only derivation. No full-closure computation, random search, native build or experiment. This note gives an exact unique-zero reduction and saturation-sensitive degree-three criteria. It does not prove that every qualifying family member has LFD three, nor produce an infinite degree-four lower bound.

## 1. Trace-slice normal form with one selector bit

Let n=2r+1, k=r+1, z=a6=1, and take a qualifying subspace V containing 1 with exactly the two Semaev zeros (0,1),(1,0). Since Tr_K(1)=1,

 V=F2*1 direct_sum W, where W=V intersect ker Tr_K and dim W=r.

The known trace consequence is Tr_K(x+y)=1. After eliminating it, write

 x=t+u, y=t+1+v, t in F2, u,v in W.

Define A(w)=w²+w and

 H(u,v)=(uv)²+uv+u+v².

Direct expansion of the original Semaev polynomial gives the exact identity

 F(t+u,t+1+v)=H(u,v)+t A(u+v).                 (1)

The two selector branches are

 t=0: H(u,v)=0,
 t=1: H(v,u)=0,                               (2)

because H(u,v)+H(v,u)=A(u+v). Both have the unique solution u=v=0, by the original exactly-two-zero property.

Every coordinate of H lies in ker Tr_K. With a basis w_1,...,w_r of W and Boolean coordinates u_i,v_i, its full tensor is

 H=sum_(i,j) u_i v_j*((w_i w_j)²+w_i w_j)
      +sum_i u_i w_i +sum_i v_i w_i².          (3)

Thus H is a square system of at most 2r independent quadratic output equations in 2r Boolean variables, with a prescribed field-multiplication tensor and unique zero. This is not an arbitrary square MQ system.

## 2. A sharp affine-ideal transfer

Let h=d_LFD(H), and let d be the LFD of the original count-controlled system. Then

 h <= d <= h+1.                               (4)

This sharper SAT transfer is available because the relevant ideals are explicitly affine, unlike the general one-quadratic-definitional-extension case.

The ideal of H is generated by all 2r Boolean coordinate variables u_i,v_i (plus field equations). Therefore its LFD is the largest degree needed to derive those linear coordinates, whenever that largest degree is at least two. Here h>=2: the coordinate span of H has dimension at most 2r, and at least one nonzero quadratic part exists. Indeed for nonzero w in W, w is neither 0 nor 1, so A(w²)!=0. A space of dimension at most 2r containing a nonzero quadratic cannot contain all 2r independent coordinate linears. Thus not all required linears are available at degree one.

For the lower inequality, derive the original affine ideal generators at degree d and restrict to t=0. Restriction never increases degree, so all u_i,v_i derive from H within d. For the upper inequality, the t=0 and t=1 systems are related by swapping u,v. Derivations of all coordinate linears at degree h exist on both branches. Multiply their lifted derivations by 1+t and t respectively. The generalized branching argument keeps degree at most h+1 because h>=2 includes the original quadratic generators. Adding the two gates derives every u_i,v_i at h+1. The eliminated trace relation was already available in W2. These are all original affine ideal generators, proving the upper bound.

For the literal n=3 control, W=span{t_field}, and H has rows d and b(d+1); it derives b,d at degree two. Hence h=2 and (4), together with the previously proved original lower bound three, gives original d=3. This is an algebraic consistency check, not a new execution.

## 3. Unique-zero systems reduce to one-coordinate UNSAT slices

For each Boolean coordinate X_j of H, let e_j be the refutation degree of the inconsistent slice H|_(X_j=1), and put e=max_j e_j. Then

 e <= h <= max(3,e+1).                         (5)

The first inequality follows by restricting a derivation of X_j to X_j=1. Conversely, a slice refutation lifts after multiplying by X_j, giving X_j in degree at most max(2,e_j)+1; the max with two is necessary because the original generators are quadratic. This yields the stated bound after deriving every coordinate.

The small-degree threshold must not be omitted: an original quadratic may restrict to a constant or linear contradiction, while multiplying the original polynomial by the selector is still a strict degree-three step. For growing degrees, the comparison has additive overhead at most one.

Consequently a growing refutation lower bound for a single coordinate slice of this precise unique-zero family would prove growing LFD for the original two-solution family. This is a concrete target with a codimension-one selection cost, rather than a high-codimension import of a known hard instance.

## 4. Exact degree-three closure as a quadratic feedback fixed point

For any of the quadratic systems above, work in its Boolean ring and define Q_0 as the span of the original generators in degree at most two. Define the increasing sequence

 U_j=Q_j + sum_i X_i Q_j subset B_(<=3),
 Q_(j+1)=U_j intersect B_(<=2).                 (6)

All products are reduced in the Boolean ring. The eventual fixed point Q_infty satisfies

 W3=Q_infty + sum_i X_i Q_infty,
 W3 intersect B_(<=2)=Q_infty.                 (7)

This follows directly from the strict multiplication rule: at degree three, exactly the consequences of degree at most two may be multiplied by a variable. Iteration in (6) feeds every newly discovered quadratic or linear consequence back into that rule. The process stabilizes in the finite-dimensional space B_(<=2).

This is why the first degree-three Macaulay rank is insufficient. It computes U_0, but a fall into Q_1 can generate new products and further falls. Equation (6), or an independently checked invariant space, is required.

## 5. A finite algebraic sufficient condition for LFD at least four

A proposed quadratic space Q containing the generators certifies the full degree-three closure if

 (Q+sum_i X_i Q) intersect B_(<=2)=Q.           (8)

If (8) holds and Q omits one of the known affine ideal generators, then that generator is absent from W3 and the LFD is at least four. For H, the known affine generators are all u_i,v_i. For the original trace-reduced system (1), they are all u_i,v_i while t remains free.

Condition (8) can be stated as an exact rank identity. Use columns for the generating polynomials in Q and all their variable products, and rows for monomial coefficients in degree at most three. Let A3 record their cubic homogeneous parts and A_low their degree-at-most-two parts. Then

 dim((Q+sum X_iQ) intersect B_(<=2))
   =rank([A3;A_low])-rank(A3).

Since Q is already contained in that intersection, equality of this difference with dim Q is equivalent to (8). This is a finite algebraic condition on the actual field-product tensor (3) and a specified candidate Q. It is not a claim that the equality holds generically or on any untested family member.

A route to an infinite lower bound four would be to construct qualifying W_r and explicit spaces Q_r satisfying (8) while omitting a coordinate linear. The known trace/Frobenius degree-three falls must be included in Q_r. An argument counting only original equations would miss the feedback in (6).

## 6. Exact degree-three dichotomy for the known affine ideals

For the count-controlled original system, the previously proved lower bound is d>=3. Hence:

- if the full fixed point Q_infty for (1) contains all u_i,v_i, then d=3;
- if it omits even one, then d>=4.

For H, the corresponding test decides h<=3 versus h>=4. By (4), h>=4 implies original d>=4, but h=3 leaves original d in {3,4}; the original-system fixed point resolves that remaining one-degree gap.

No all-family outcome of this dichotomy is established here. The n=3 control satisfies the first branch. The existing larger generic Semaev certificates have different parameters/subspaces and cannot be substituted for a proof about every z=a6=1 two-point member.

## 7. A tempting but invalid linear shortcut

The algebraic identity H(u,v)+H(v,u)=A(u+v) is not, by itself, a derivation of A(u+v)=0 from the H system. H(v,u) is the other branch's equation, not an available original generator in the first branch. Likewise subtracting the t=0 and t=1 equations of (1) is not allowed without retaining their selector gates.

The original affine symmetry swaps u,v *and* sends t to t+1. It preserves (1), but shifting t alone does not. Treating both branches as simultaneous unconditional equations would incorrectly derive u+v=0 and could manufacture an unjustified constant-degree solver. The gates in the proof of (4) are the legitimate way to combine branch information.

## 8. Remaining proof obligation

The progress is an exact unique-zero family, an additive-one SAT transfer, and a fully saturated degree-three criterion expressed in the actual tensor. The missing step is an algebraic description of Q_infty or a verified closed candidate Q_r for infinitely many qualifying subspaces. No generic-rank assumption, random-subspace heuristic, or initial-Macaulay truncation fills that gap.

A useful next theoretical question is whether the trace-zero subspace W can be chosen so that W and W² are complementary in ker Tr_K. Then the linear part (u,v)->u+v² is invertible and H becomes a prescribed quadratic fixed-point system after an output-coordinate change. This observation is only a possible normal form; neither complementarity for the count-controlled family nor a lower-bound theorem for that fixed-point system is proved here.
