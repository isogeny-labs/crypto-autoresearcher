# Lower-bound transfer: affine restriction cost and a one-bit extension normal form

Date: 2026-11-09. Source-only deduction, no experiments/builds. This note provides two quantitative transfer statements and their limitations. It does not construct an infinite hard family or prove unbounded LFD.

## 1. A usable lower-bound transfer through affine restrictions

Let F be a Boolean polynomial system, with field equations, and let rho restrict its variables to an affine subspace A of codimension t. Suppose the restricted system G=rho(F) is unsatisfiable and has refutation degree d>=3. Then

 d <= max(d_LFD(F), t, 2).

In particular, if d>t, then d_LFD(F)>=d. F itself need not be unsatisfiable.

**Proof.** Use an invertible affine coordinate change so that A is given by t coordinate assignments. Its indicator e_A is the product of the t affine selector factors and has degree t. No common zero of F lies on A, because G is unsatisfiable. Therefore e_A vanishes on every Boolean zero of F. The Boolean field equations make the ideal radical, so e_A belongs to the ideal generated by F and the field equations.

By the definition of LFD, e_A has a derivation at degree at most max(d_LFD(F),t), or at max(d_LFD(F),t,2) if we explicitly include the Boolean-closure bridge's degree-two threshold. Applying rho to this derivation never increases degree, and e_A restricts to 1. It is therefore a refutation of G at that degree. This proves the inequality.

The indicator is essential. An arbitrary SAT system can acquire a hard inconsistent restriction, but the cost of selecting that restricted subspace matters. One cannot claim unconditional monotonicity of SAT LFD under restrictions.

### Consequence for existing certificates

The certified R35a instance has n=35, dim V=18, and refutation degree 5. It can be obtained by restricting both x and y in the unrestricted full field H=F_(2^35) to V. That restriction has codimension

 t=2(35-18)=34.

The inequality then reads 5<=max(d_LFD(F_full),34,2), which gives no lower bound on the full-field family. Thus the finite degree-five certificate does not contradict the possibility of a uniform small bound for the unrestricted inside-subfield case.

Similarly, adding ambient dimensions and then recovering a fixed hard instance by restriction preserves at most its already known constant lower bound. It does not amplify degree. A viable reduction from a growing hard family must control restriction codimension: its refutation degree must grow beyond that codimension, or it must furnish a lower-degree indicator/vanisher with a separate proof.

## 2. Why a hard restriction is not automatically an amplification gadget

The existing branching lemma is an upper bound: refutations of both one-bit branches at degree d give a refutation at degree d+1. Its converse is false. A system H independent of a fresh selector c has two branches identical to H; both have degree d but the unrestricted system still has degree d, not d+1.

Adding field-coordinate gadgets faces the further multiplication-tensor obstruction proved in `algebraic-route.md`: cross terms cannot all vanish between nontrivial subspaces of dimension greater than one. Therefore a branch-amplification construction needs both a genuine increasing lower-bound witness and a realizable field multiplication pattern. Two hard branch certificates alone supply neither.

## 3. The normal form is only one quadratic definitional bit after trace elimination

The earlier normal-form note gave an affine forward map and a quadratic inverse, with a conservative factor-two closure simulation. There is a sharper structural description, valid for arbitrary V, not only full-subfield V.

Let K=F_(2^n), z!=0, a6!=0, and

 c=sqrt(a6)/z, s=x+y,
 A(r)=r²+r,
 G(x,r)=x²+x(r²+r+c)+zr².

The original trace relation is Tr_K(s+c)=0. If it is inconsistent on s in V, the system has an immediate degree-two refutation and there is no hard-family candidate. Otherwise eliminate this affine relation and parameterize the remaining s-values by h affine Boolean coordinates t, where h=k-1 if trace is nonzero on V and h=k when it is identically zero and compatible.

The Artin–Schreier map A has kernel F2 and image ker Tr_K. Choose an F2-linear section on that image. There is then an affine-linear r_0(t) such that A(r_0)=s+c, and all preimages are

 r=r_0(t)+epsilon, epsilon in F2.

Substituting this into G gives the exact identity

 G(x,r_0+epsilon)=G_0(x,t)+z epsilon,            (1)

because A(r) is unchanged by epsilon and r² changes by epsilon. G_0 is quadratic in the x,t Boolean coordinates.

Choose an F2-linear functional lambda:K->F2 with lambda(z)=1 and set q(x,t)=lambda(G_0). Row-transform the full coordinate system G=0. It becomes

 epsilon+q(x,t)=0,
 coordinates of P(G_0)=0,

where P(u)=u+z lambda(u) projects onto ker lambda along F2 z. The second group is independent of epsilon.

The original pulled-back Semaev system is L_z(G_0)=0, where L_z(u)=u²+zu. Since ker L_z=F2 z, L_z restricted to ker lambda is an isomorphism onto its image. Hence the coordinates of L_z(G_0) and P(G_0) have exactly the same F2 span. After the original affine trace elimination, write this common system as F_red. The transformed system is therefore precisely

 G_red = F_red union {epsilon+q(x,t)}.          (2)

This is one Boolean quadratic definitional extension, not n unrelated quadratic substitutions. It adds no freedom on solutions and changes no solution count. The choice of section and output functional changes coordinates but not this structural fact.

## 4. Additive refutation-degree comparison

For an unsatisfiable F_red, the extension G_red obeys the conservative bounds

 d_ref(G_red) <= d_ref(F_red) <= d_ref(G_red)+2. (3)

The first inequality holds because every F_red derivation is also a G_red derivation. For the second, substitute epsilon=q in a degree-D Boolean derivation of G_red.

Every multilinear proof line has the form h_0+epsilon h_1. Substitution increases its degree by at most one. Initial F_red equations remain unchanged, while epsilon+q becomes zero. Multiplication by an ordinary variable requires degree at most D+1. Multiplication by epsilon can be simulated by multiplying the substituted preceding line by each monomial of q, of degree at most two; the safe intermediate-degree budget is D+2. Therefore a degree-D refutation of G_red becomes a degree-at-most-D+2 refutation of F_red. This deliberately keeps the extra intermediate-degree allowance: cancelling epsilon²=epsilon before substitution is not a justification for silently claiming D+1.

Affine coordinate changes and elimination of available linear constraints preserve refutation degree above the degree-two threshold, so this gives the same additive comparison for the corresponding nontrivial original and normal-form instances.

**Use.** Unbounded refutation degree for the actual restricted-domain normal-form family is equivalent, up to an additive constant, to unbounded refutation degree for the original unsatisfiable Semaev family. This is a tighter and more faithful target than the previous arbitrary factor-two substitution estimate.

**Limit.** It does not prove either side is unbounded. The normal form still has the restrictive field multiplication tensor and the r-domain tied to V through the Artin–Schreier preimage. Replacing G_red by an arbitrary quadratic system would abandon the Semaev family.

## 5. Why this still does not give SAT LFD invariance

For satisfiable F_red, a degree-e ideal element derived in G_red at degree max(C,e) only gets the direct substitution bound max(C,e)+2 in F_red. LFD requires max(C',e), not an additive overhead for every arbitrarily high output degree e. Thus (3), whose output is the constant 1, cannot simply be relabeled as a last-fall-degree comparison for SAT systems.

Likewise, the high Boolean degree of the explicit inverse s^(2^k-2), or of a particular rational-trace elimination formula, proves nothing about the minimum degree of all possible derivations. The one-bit description shows especially clearly why inspecting the inverse formula alone is misleading: most of the inverse coordinates are affine after trace elimination; only one Boolean quadratic definition remains. What matters is how that definition interacts with full closure, not the apparent field-expression exponent.

## 6. Concrete next obligation for a growing lower bound

A rigorous lower-bound route can now be stated without ambiguity:

1. Construct infinitely many compatible V,z,a6 with k=ceil(n/2) and unsatisfiable F_red.
2. Work with the exactly corresponding G_red family in (2), retaining its field-product and Artin–Schreier-domain constraints.
3. Construct growing-degree full-closure quotient certificates for G_red. By the first inequality in (3), these immediately lower-bound F_red; the additive reverse inequality confirms the normal form has not hidden an unbounded proof-degree gap.
4. If instead importing hard restrictions of another family, prove that their refutation degree exceeds the restriction-indicator degree, as required in Section 1.

No construction satisfying step 3 is obtained here. The progress is a precise admissible transfer and the elimination of two tempting but invalid shortcuts: high-codimension embedding of finite certificates, and treating explicit inversion degree as LFD.
