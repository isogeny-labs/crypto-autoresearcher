# No-wrap closure: odd-degree collapse and cap two through r=5

2026-11-09. Pure symbolic derivations; no solver, build, experiment, or cloud execution. This extends the corrected no-wrap-coefficient-progress.md. Its constant-coefficient affine elimination remains required.

After the already proved degree-two affine eliminations, use independent Boolean coordinates for x(T) of degree <=r and q(T) with q(0)=0 and degree <=d=floor(r/2). The reduced coefficient system is

 E(T)=A(x)+x A(q)+q²=0,       A(p)=p²+p.

Here y=x+1+A(q). Every affine consequence below can be lifted back at cap two: changing a quadratic by an affine substitution uses only linear multiples of an already known affine relation.

## Lemma 1: odd r collapses to r-1 at cap two

If r is odd, deg(x A(q))<=r+2d=2r-1, and deg q²<=r-1. The T^(2r) coefficient of E is therefore exactly x_r, from x². Thus x_r belongs to W2. Removing it leaves precisely the reduced presentation for r-1, since floor(r/2)=floor((r-1)/2). Consequently any cap-two derivation for r-1 lifts to r. This is a derivation-level induction step, not an argument merely comparing degrees at solutions.

## Lemma 2: the first affine consequence for even r

For r=2d>=2, before additional saturation, the affine part of the span of the reduced E coefficient rows is exactly the one-dimensional space generated by q(1)=sum_(j=1)^d q_j.

Indeed the quadratic coefficient attached to the independent monomial x_i q_j is T^(i+j)+T^(i+2j), for 0<=i<=r, 1<=j<=d. View these as edge vectors on vertices 1,...,2r. The edges for j=1 connect 1,...,r+2. Inductively, after j-1 all vertices through r+2j-2 are connected; edges for j attach any new vertices r+2j-1,r+2j to that component, because their lower endpoints r+j-1,r+j already lie in it. Thus the graph is connected and these quadratic coefficient vectors span all even-parity vectors on the positive coefficient indices. The only linear functional annihilating them is the sum of all positive coefficients. Applied to E, this is E(1)=q(1), since x(1),q(1) are Boolean and A(x)(1)=A(q)(1)=0. The constant coefficient is identically zero in the reduced presentation.

This lemma characterizes only the first affine extraction. Subsequent products of q(1) with variables and further affine extraction are indispensable. It is not a dimension-based lower bound.

## Lemma 3: r=4 has a complete cap-two derivation

Lemma 2 gives q_1+q_2=0. Set q=c(T+T²) and x=a+bT+dT²+eT³+fT^4. Then A(q)=c(T+T^4) and the nonzero coefficient rows of E are

 R1=b+ca,
 R2=b+d+c+cb,
 R3=e+cd,
 R4=d+f+c+ca+ce,
 R5=cb+cf,
 R6=e+cd=R3,
 R7=ce,
 R8=f+cf.

The term ce in R4 must be retained; it comes from eT³ times cT. It may be removed using R7, but is not absent from the raw coefficient.

The sum R2+R5+R8 gives the linear relation

 L=b+d+f+c.

Eliminate d=b+f+c. Multiplying L by c is a permitted cap-two step, and Boolean reduction c²=c gives

 R2'=f+cb,
 R3'=e+cb+cf+c.

Now R3'+R2'+R8=e+c is linear. Multiply this relation by c and add R7=ce to derive c. With c available, all quadratic multiples c times a variable are available at cap two. Then R1,R2,R3,R8 derive b,d,e,f. The original positive coordinates of y also vanish because y=x+1+A(q), while y_0=x_0+1 remains free. Hence the complete two-point affine ideal is derived within W2 and LFD<=2.

Together with the previously recorded r=1,2 derivations and Lemma 1, this proves LFD<=2 for every 1<=r<=5 in the no-wrap polynomial-subspace family, for every irreducible field modulus of degree n>4r. These are algebraic upper bounds, not computational measurements or assertions of exact LFD under every convention.

## Remaining even-degree obligation

Lemma 1 reduces a universal cap-two theorem to even r. Lemma 2 identifies the first affine extraction exactly. For even r>=6, after imposing q(1)=0 there are multiple independent q coordinates. The r=4 argument exploits one remaining coordinate c and c²=c; its cancellations have not been generalized to the mixed products among several q coordinates that arise through later affine substitutions.

A valid next induction must show that degree-two feedback derives an affine relation reducing the allowed q-space or the maximal degree of x, and that the resulting system falls into an inductive class. No such general reduction is proved here. Conversely, failure of the first affine extraction to do more than q(1)=0 is not an obstruction: r=4 explicitly demonstrates the additional information generated by saturation.
