# Explicit polynomial subspace: exact two-point family

2026-11-09. Written derivation, pending independent review. No execution.

Let r>=1, n>4r, K=F2[t]/(p(t)) for any irreducible p of degree n, and V=span_F2(1,t,...,t^r). Set z=a6=1 and a2=0. The target (1,0) is on the curve y²+xy=x³+1. The normalized m=2 Semaev equation is

    (xy)²+xy+(x+y+1)²=0.

Every term has polynomial degree at most4r<n. Thus equality in K is precisely equality in F2[t], without modular reduction. This defines a constructive family once any irreducible p is chosen; it does not require the earlier averaging selection of V.

## Short highest-degree proof

If x and y are both nonconstant of degrees a,b>0, (xy)² has degree 2(a+b), strictly greater than every other term in the equation, and cannot cancel. At least one variable is constant in F2. Substitution of0 forces the other1; substitution of1 forces the other0. Thus precisely the two constant pairs occur. The alternative descent below gives the same conclusion.

## Elementary square-root descent

Write P=xy and S=x+y+1. From P²+P=S², comparison of odd polynomial exponents shows P is a square, P=Q². Injectivity of squaring then gives Q²+Q=S. Thus

    xy=Q²,    x+y=Q²+Q+1.

Since gcd(Q,Q²+Q+1)=1, any common irreducible divisor of x and y would divide Q and Q²+Q+1, which is impossible. Hence gcd(x,y)=1. The polynomial ring is a unique factorization domain; coprime factors of a square are individually squares, so x=A²,y=B². (If either is zero, the displayed equation directly gives the other1.) Squaring is injective, so AB=Q. The equation

    (A+B)²=Q²+Q+1

makes Q itself a square, Q=C², by the same odd-coefficient argument. It follows that

    AB=C²,    A+B=C²+C+1.

Consequently (A,B) satisfies exactly the original Semaev polynomial identity. If a nonconstant solution pair existed, taking its polynomial square roots would strictly reduce the positive maximum degree; repeated descent is impossible. Every solution is constant. Checking four pairs in F2 gives precisely (0,1) and (1,0).

This establishes exactly two ordered solutions for all r>=1 and n>4r, conditional only on the explicitly stated irreducibility and presentation. The factor-base dimension k=r+1 is unbounded. The ideal of Boolean solutions is generated by x_i,y_i for i>=1 and x_0+y_0+1.

## Degree obligation remains separate

The UFD step is an argument about actual polynomial solutions, not a bounded-degree polynomial-calculus derivation of coefficient consequences. It does not prove a constant upper bound, a growing lower bound, or any specific cap for deriving x_i=y_i=0. In particular, logarithmically many conceptual square-root descents do not imply logarithmic LFD.
