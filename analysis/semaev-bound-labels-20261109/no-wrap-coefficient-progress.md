# No-wrap polynomial subspaces: coefficient closure at r=1,2

2026-11-09. Source-only algebra. No execution, searches, builds or cloud jobs. Read alongside polynomial-subspace-candidate.md; this note does not change that note.

Take K=F2[t]/P(t), deg P=n>4r, and V=span(1,t,...,t^r). The Semaev polynomial for z=a6=1 has degree at most4r in t, so its coordinate equations are precisely its polynomial coefficients. The only solutions are (x,y)=(0,1),(1,0): if both polynomial degrees a,b are positive, (xy)^2 has degree2(a+b), strictly larger than every other term. If either polynomial is constant, direct substitution gives the stated pairs. This family is off the diagonal dim V=ceil(n/2); earlier diagonal lower-bound theorems do not apply.

Write p=xy and s=x+y+1. With p_j,s_j their coefficients, the equations are

 f_j=p_j + [j even](p_(j/2)+s_(j/2))=0,

where Boolean squaring of a coefficient fixes it. In particular f0=s0, and all statements below concern j>0. Coefficient rows with j>2r first give p_j=0 for j>r (descending through the even indices). The remaining rows give p_odd=0 and p_(2j)=p_j+s_j. Thus for each positive odd d the sum of s_(2^i d) along its chain of indices at most r is zero. These are linear consequences in the span of the original coefficient equations, not high-degree elimination consequences.

First eliminate y_0=x_0+1 using the affine row s_0; Boolean reduction then gives p_0=x_0(x_0+1)=0. This step is available at cap two and is not asserted to be an initial row-span identity. After this substitution, there is a unique polynomial q with zero constant coefficient and degree at most floor(r/2) such that

             p=q²,       s=q²+q.                         (1)

Here q's coefficients are fixed linear forms in the coefficients of s, subject to the chain relations just described. Equation (1) is an equivalent coordinate presentation obtained by linear row operations and affine coordinate substitutions. It does not assert that q exists as an extra unconstrained variable. In particular it does not by itself make a high-degree polynomial substitution into the Boolean derivation.

## r=1: full cap-two derivation

Write x=a+bt, y=c+dt. Available coefficient rows are

 a+c+1, ad+bc, ad+bc+bd+b+d, bd.

Their span contains b+d. Multiplication of that linear by b is permitted at cap two and gives b+bd. Together with bd it yields b, and then d. Hence the affine ideal generated by a+c+1,b,d is derived at cap two, proving LFD<=2. No degree-three multiplication is required. (The n=3 modular-reduction control has a different system.)

## r=2: full cap-two derivation

Write x=a+bt+ct² and y=d+et+ft². The coefficient span contains

 a+d+1, p1, p3, p4, p2+s1, p2+s2,

where s1=b+e, s2=c+f and

 p1=ae+bd, p2=af+be+cd, p3=bf+ce, p4=cf.

The affine consequences permit d=a+1, e=b+s, f=c+s with s=s1=s2. This affine substitution preserves degree. All corrections to quadratic polynomials are products of known linears by linears, permitted at cap two. The resulting quadratics are

 p1=as+b,
 p3=bs+cs,
 p4=c+cs,
 p2=as+b+bs+c.

Their sum p1+p3+p4+p2 is zero. Since p1,p3,p4 are available as zero equations and p2+s is an original consequence, their sum derives the linear s. With s=0, p1 gives b and p4 gives c, using only cap-two products of s by variables. Then e=f=0 and d=a+1. Thus all affine generators of the two-point ideal are in W2, proving LFD<=2.

These are symbolic derivations, independently checkable coefficient by coefficient; no software test is claimed.

## Exact unresolved general step

The initial linear chain constraints are available for all r. What remains is to prove or disprove that their degree-two saturation together with the quadratic coefficient identities xy=q² forces every positive-degree coordinate of x and y to vanish, for every r. A successful induction must derive a new linear consequence using degree-two operations only; comparing the highest nonzero polynomial degrees at a solution is not such a derivation. It implicitly conditions on which coefficient is the highest nonzero one, and lifting all those branches can increase the degree.

Accordingly, the explicit family currently has an elementary infinite two-solution construction and verified cap-two upper bounds for r=1,2. No general uniform cap or growing lower bound follows from the degree comparison or the chain constraints alone.
