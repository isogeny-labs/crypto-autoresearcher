# Theorem dossier: first and last fall degree of the $m=2$ Semaev descent

## 0. Status, scope, conventions

This is a proof dossier plus computer-assisted results on explicit instances.
It is **not** an evidence record and does not promote, supersede or close any
`KN-*`, `EV-*`, `DEC-*`, `H-*` or `GOAL-*` record. Every computational claim is
about an explicit instance whose parameters are in
[`results/instances.json`](results/instances.json). Every instance was computed
by implementation A (`code/`). The instances that carry the main claims were
also recomputed by implementation B (`verify/`), which shares no code with A.
[`results/verification.md`](results/verification.md) lists exactly which ones.

**Claim tiers used below.**

- **Proved** means a complete pen-and-paper proof is given here, valid for
  every $n$ and every subspace $V$ the statement names.
- **Computer-verified (instance)** means an exact finite computation on one
  named instance. Nothing is extrapolated beyond the named instance. An
  instance marked *(B)* was reproduced by implementation B with identical
  results. An instance marked *(cert)* has a closure certificate
  (Proposition 1.5) that `verify/cert_check.py` accepted. That check proves
  $1\notin W_4$ without trusting the program that produced the certificate.
  Every other instance was computed by implementation A alone.
- **Observed** means a pattern seen on every instance computed, with no proof
  and no claim beyond those instances.

### 0.1 Setting

- $n\ge 3$, $K=\mathbb F_{2^n}$, $\operatorname{Tr}=\operatorname{Tr}_{K/\mathbb F_2}$, and $K_0=\ker\operatorname{Tr}$.
- $E: y^2+xy=x^3+a_2x^2+a_6$ with $a_6\ne 0$. Semaev's third summation polynomial for $E$ is
  $S_3(x_1,x_2,x_3)=(x_1x_2+x_1x_3+x_2x_3)^2+x_1x_2x_3+a_6$.
- The target is $z\in K^{\times}$. Semaev substitutes $R_X$ by a uniform $z$; the statements below hold for every $z\ne0$.
- $V\subset K$ is an $\mathbb F_2$-subspace of dimension $k$ with basis $v_1,\dots,v_k$. Semaev's diagonal for $m=2$ is $k=\lceil n/2\rceil$.
- $B=\mathbb F_2[a_1,\dots,a_k,b_1,\dots,b_k]/(a_j^2+a_j,\,b_j^2+b_j)$ is the Boolean ring in $N=2k$ variables.
  - Every element has a unique multilinear representative. $\deg$ means the degree of that representative, and $B_{\le d}$ is the set of elements of degree at most $d$.
- $B_K=B\otimes_{\mathbb F_2}K$. Monomials are idempotent and the characteristic is 2, so:
  1. $g\mapsto g^2$ on $B_K$ is the coefficientwise Frobenius. It is a degree-preserving ring endomorphism, and $g^{2^n}=g$.
  2. The coefficientwise trace $\operatorname{Tr}:B_K\to B$, $\operatorname{Tr}(g)=\sum_{j<n}g^{2^j}$, is $B$-linear and satisfies $\operatorname{Tr}(g^2)=\operatorname{Tr}(g)$.
  3. An $\mathbb F_2$-linear map $\Phi:K\to K$, applied coefficientwise, is $B$-linear and does not increase degree.
- $X_1=\sum_j a_jv_j$, $X_2=\sum_j b_jv_j$ and $s=X_1+X_2$ have degree 1. Put $S:=S_3(X_1,X_2,z)\in B_K$.
- **The system.** $F=\{\operatorname{Tr}(\gamma_iS)\}_{i\le n}$ for an $\mathbb F_2$-basis $(\gamma_i)$ of $K$.
  - This is the usual Weil descent: the coordinates of $S$ in the trace-dual basis.
  - $\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb F_2}F=\{\operatorname{Tr}(\gamma S):\gamma\in K\}$ does not depend on the choice of basis.
  - Boolean zeros of $F$ correspond to pairs $(X_1,X_2)\in V\times V$ with $S_3(X_1,X_2,z)=0$.
  - In $R=\mathbb F_2[a,b]$, the system *with field equations* is $\tilde F=\{\text{multilinear lifts of }F\}\cup\{a_j^2+a_j,b_j^2+b_j\}$. For $m=t=2$ this is exactly the Boolean system of Semaev's eq. (5): see [Semaev, ePrint 2015/310, §3], *"For t = 2 the system consists of only one equation $S_3(x_1,x_2,R_X)=0$"*.

### 0.2 Closures and fall degrees

- **Closure $W_d(G)$**, for $G\subset B$: the smallest $\mathbb F_2$-subspace $W\subseteq B_{\le d}$ such that
  1. $G\cap B_{\le d}\subseteq W$, and
  2. $x\,g\in W$ whenever $g\in W$, $\deg g\le d-1$ and $x$ is a variable.
- **Macaulay space** $M_d(G)=\operatorname{span}\{m\,g: g\in G,\ m \text{ a monomial},\ \deg m+\deg g\le d\}$. Always $M_d\subseteq W_d$.
- **Refutation degree.** If $G$ has no Boolean zero, $d_{\rm ref}(G)=\min\{d:1\in W_d(G)\}$.
- **Last fall degree** (Huang–Kosters–Yeo 2015; Caminata–Gorla 2023, Def. 1.5), for $\tilde F\subset R$:
  - $V_{\tilde F,d}$ is the smallest space that contains $\tilde F\cap R_{\le d}$ and is closed under $f\mapsto hf$ whenever $\deg(hf)\le d$.
  - The last fall degree is $d_{\tilde F}=\min\{d: f\in V_{\tilde F,\max(d,\deg f)}\ \forall f\in(\tilde F)\}$.
- **First fall degree** (Caminata–Gorla 2023, Def. 1.3) is $d_{\rm ff}(F)=\min\{d:\operatorname{Syz}(F^{\rm top})_d/\operatorname{Triv}(F^{\rm top})_d\ne0\}$.
  - It is computed in the graded ring $A=\mathbb F_2[x]/(x_i^2)$ of top forms.
  - $\operatorname{Triv}$ is generated by the Koszul syzygies $f_i^{\rm top}e_j-f_j^{\rm top}e_i$ and the field syzygies $f_i^{\rm top}e_i$.
- The Petit–Quisquater first fall degree, the one Semaev uses (§4.4), is the least $d$ for which there are $g_i$ with:
  - $\max_i(\deg g_i+\deg f_i)=d$,
  - $\deg\sum g_if_i<d$, and
  - $\sum g_if_i\ne 0$.
- **Semaev's $d_{F4}$** is "the maximal total degree of the polynomials occurring before a Gröbner basis is computed" by F4 on $\tilde F$ (§4.4).

## 1. General closure facts

**Proposition 1.1** (Boolean closure = polynomial closure). **Proved.**
Let $\pi:R\to B$ be the quotient map and $d\ge 2$. Then
$\pi(V_{\tilde F,d})=W_d(F)$ and $V_{\tilde F,d}=\pi^{-1}(W_d(F))\cap R_{\le d}$.
In particular $1\in V_{\tilde F,d}\iff 1\in W_d(F)$.

*Proof.* Let $J=(x_i^2+x_i)$.

*Step 1.* $J\cap R_{\le d}\subseteq V_{\tilde F,d}$. The field polynomials form a
Gröbner basis of $J$ for every degree-compatible order, and reduction by them
never raises degree. So every element of $J\cap R_{\le d}$ is a sum of
products $h\,(x_i^2+x_i)$ of degree $\le d$, and each such product lies in
$V_{\tilde F,d}$.

*Step 2* ($\supseteq$). Let $U=\pi(V_{\tilde F,d})$, so $U\supseteq F\cap B_{\le d}$.
Take $g\in U$ with $\deg g\le d-1$, and let $\tilde g$ be its multilinear lift.
Some preimage of $g$ lies in $V_{\tilde F,d}$, and it differs from $\tilde g$ by
an element of $J\cap R_{\le d}$. By Step 1, $\tilde g\in V_{\tilde F,d}$. Since
$\deg x\tilde g\le d$, we get $x\tilde g\in V_{\tilde F,d}$ and
$x\,g=\pi(x\tilde g)\in U$. So $U$ is closed and contains $W_d(F)$.

*Step 3* ($\subseteq$). Let $U'=\pi^{-1}(W_d(F))\cap R_{\le d}$, which contains
$\tilde F\cap R_{\le d}$. Let $f\in U'$ and let $h=x_{i_1}\cdots x_{i_r}$ be a monomial
with $\deg(hf)\le d$. Since $R$ is a domain, the $j$-th partial product has
degree $\deg f+j\le d-(r-j)$, which is at most $d-1$ for $j<r$. Each step
satisfies $\pi(xf')=x\,\pi(f')$ with $\deg\pi(f')\le\deg f'\le d-1$, so
$hf\in U'$, and by linearity $U'$ is closed. Hence
$U'\supseteq V_{\tilde F,d}$. $\square$

**Proposition 1.2** (unsatisfiable systems). **Proved.**
Suppose $F$ has no Boolean zero. Then:

1. $d_{\tilde F}=d_{\rm ref}(F)$.
2. For every degree-compatible order, the solving degree of $\tilde F$ equals $d_{\rm ref}(F)$. This is Caminata–Gorla Thm 3.1 with reduced Gröbner basis $\{1\}$.
3. Every F4 run on $\tilde F$, under any selection strategy, produces a polynomial of total degree $\ge d_{\rm ref}(F)$. That is, $d_{F4}\ge d_{\rm ref}(F)$.

*Proof.*

*Monotonicity.* $V_{\tilde F,d}\subseteq V_{\tilde F,d+1}$, because
$V_{\tilde F,d+1}\cap R_{\le d}$ contains $\tilde F\cap R_{\le d}$ and is closed
under the degree-$d$ operations.

*Part 1.* With field equations and no common zero in $\mathbb F_2^N$, we have
$(\tilde F)=R$. Let $d^{\ast}=\min\{d:1\in V_{\tilde F,d}\}$, which equals
$d_{\rm ref}(F)$ by Proposition 1.1.
- Taking $f=1$ in the definition of $d_{\tilde F}$ gives $d_{\tilde F}\ge d^{\ast}$.
- Conversely, take $f\in R$ and $e=\max(d^{\ast},\deg f)$. Then
  $1\in V_{\tilde F,d^{\ast}}\subseteq V_{\tilde F,e}$, and $f=f\cdot1$ with
  $\deg(f\cdot 1)\le e$. So $f\in V_{\tilde F,e}$, and $d_{\tilde F}\le d^{\ast}$.

*Part 3.* Each F4 step of degree $\le D$ multiplies earlier polynomials by
monomials, keeping total degree $\le D$, and then takes linear combinations.
By induction, every polynomial produced while all step degrees are $\le D$
lies in $V_{\tilde F,D}$. F4 returns the reduced Gröbner basis $\{1\}$, so
$1\in V_{\tilde F,d_{F4}}$ and $d_{F4}\ge d^{\ast}$. $\square$

**Lemma 1.3** (branching). **Proved.**
Let $d\ge\max\deg F$, let $x$ be a variable, and let $F_b=F|_{x=b}$ for
$b\in\mathbb F_2$ be the restricted system in the other variables. If
$1\in W_d(F_0)$ and $1\in W_d(F_1)$, then $1\in W_{d+1}(F)$.

Iterating: if all $2^t$ restrictions of $t$ variables satisfy $1\in W_d$,
then $1\in W_{d+t}(F)$. The same holds after any invertible affine change of
variables, since such a change preserves every $W_d$ and $M_d$.

*Proof.* Let $e_b=x+b+1$, so that $e_bx=e_bb$ and therefore
$e_b\,g=e_b\,(g|_{x=b})$ for every $g\in B$. Let
$U=\{g'\in B'_{\le d}: e_b g'\in W_{d+1}(F)\}$, where $B'$ is the ring without
$x$.

- **Base case.** For $f\in F$ we have $\deg f\le d$, so $f\in W_{d+1}$ and
  $xf\in W_{d+1}$. Hence $e_bf\in W_{d+1}$, and since $e_b f=e_b f|_{x=b}$ this
  gives $F_b\cap B'_{\le d}\subseteq U$.
- **Closure.** If $g'\in U$ with $\deg g'\le d-1$ and $y\ne x$, then
  $e_b\,yg'=y\,(e_bg')$, and $\deg(e_bg')\le d$. So $yg'\in U$.

Therefore $U\supseteq W_d(F_b)\ni1$, giving $e_0,e_1\in W_{d+1}(F)$ and
$1=e_0+e_1\in W_{d+1}(F)$. $\square$

**Proposition 1.4** (satisfiable systems). **Proved.**
Let $S\subseteq\mathbb F_2^N$ be the Boolean zero set of $F$ and let $d\ge2$. If
$\operatorname{codim}(W_d(F)\subseteq B_{\le d})>|S|$, then $d_{\tilde F}\ge d+1$,
and every F4 run on $\tilde F$ produces a polynomial of degree $\ge d+1$.

*Proof.* The ideal $(\tilde F)$ contains the field equations, so it is radical
and equals the vanishing ideal of $S$. Hence
$\pi((\tilde F)\cap R_{\le d})=I(S)_{\le d}:=\{g\in B_{\le d}:g|_S=0\}$. Its
codimension in $B_{\le d}$ is the rank of the evaluation map
$B_{\le d}\to\mathbb F_2^S$, which is at most $|S|$.

So the hypothesis gives some $f\in(\tilde F)$ with $\deg f\le d$ and
$f\notin V_{\tilde F,d}$ (Proposition 1.1). By monotonicity, $f\notin V_{\tilde
F,\max(e,\deg f)}$ for every $e\le d$, so $d_{\tilde F}\ge d+1$.

If every F4 step had degree $\le d$, the reduced Gröbner basis $G$ it returns
would lie in $V_{\tilde F,d}$. Then $f$ would have a standard representation
$f=\sum h_ig_i$ with $g_i\in G$ and $\deg(h_ig_i)\le\deg f\le d$, for a
degree-compatible order, putting $f\in V_{\tilde F,d}$. This is a
contradiction. $\square$

**Proposition 1.4a** (saturation at the exact solution count gives an upper
bound). **Proved.** Let $S\ne\varnothing$ be the Boolean zero set of $F$,
$s=|S|$, and $d\ge\max(2,1+\lfloor\log_2s\rfloor)$. If

$$\operatorname{codim}(W_d(F)\subseteq B_{\le d})=s,$$

then $d_{\tilde F}\le d$. If also $d\ge3$ and
$\operatorname{codim}W_{d-1}(F)>s$, then $d_{\tilde F}=d$.

*Proof.* Let $I=(\tilde F)$ in $R$, the vanishing ideal of $S$ with the
field equations. Fix a degree-compatible monomial order and let $T$ be its
standard monomials for $I$. The quotient $R/I$ is the algebra of functions
on $S$, so $|T|=s$. The field equations make every element of $T$
squarefree. Because $T$ is closed under divisors, a monomial of degree $r$
in $T$ brings all its $2^r$ squarefree divisors into $T$. Consequently
every standard monomial has degree at most $r_0=\lfloor\log_2s\rfloor$.
Their evaluations form a basis of the functions on $S$, so evaluation from
$B_{\le d}$ has rank $s$. Since $W_d(F)$ vanishes on $S$, its stated
codimension forces $W_d(F)=I(S)\cap B_{\le d}$. Proposition 1.1 then gives
$V_{\tilde F,d}=I\cap R_{\le d}$.

Every minimal leading monomial of the reduced Gröbner basis of $I$ has
degree at most $\max(2,r_0+1)$: a squarefree minimal nonstandard monomial
has all proper divisors in $T$, and a non-squarefree minimal one is a
square $x_i^2$ because the field equations are in $I$. Degree compatibility
makes each corresponding basis polynomial have at most that degree, hence
at most $d$. All basis polynomials therefore lie in $V_{\tilde F,d}$.
For any $f\in I$, division by this basis yields a standard representation
whose summands have degree at most $\deg f$. Thus each summand belongs to
$V_{\tilde F,\max(d,\deg f)}$, and so does $f$. This proves the upper
bound by the definition of last fall degree. The additional strict
codimension at $d-1$ gives the opposite bound by Proposition 1.4. $\square$

For a named system with exactly six Boolean solutions, $r_0=2$. In
particular, an **exact** W5 codimension of six and a checked W4 codimension
greater than six prove last fall degree exactly five. An exact W5
codimension of at least seven proves last fall degree at least six by
Proposition 1.4. A checked six-dimensional quotient map alone only proves
$\operatorname{codim}W_5\ge6$; the upper-bound direction requires equality
with the true W5 quotient.

**Proposition 1.5** (dual certificate for $1\notin W_d$). **Proved.**
Let $d\ge2$ and let $\pi:B_{\le d}\to Z$ be an $\mathbb F_2$-linear map onto a
vector space $Z$. Put $U=\ker\pi$. Suppose:

- **(i)** $\pi(f)=0$ for every $f\in F$ with $\deg f\le d$;
- **(ii)** $\pi(xu)=0$ for every variable $x$ and every $u\in U$ with $\deg u\le d-1$.

Then $W_d(F)\subseteq U$. Hence $\operatorname{codim}(W_d(F)\subseteq B_{\le d})\ge\dim Z$,
and if $\pi(1)\ne0$ then $1\notin W_d(F)$.

Conversely, if $1\notin W_d(F)$, the quotient map $B_{\le d}\to B_{\le d}/W_d(F)$
satisfies (i), (ii) and $\pi(1)\ne0$ with $U=W_d(F)$. So a certificate exists
exactly when the claim is true.

*Proof.* (i) and (ii) say that $U$ has both properties in the definition of
$W_d(F)$ (§0.2). The intersection of all subspaces with those two properties
has them too, so it is the smallest one, and it lies in $U$. For the converse,
$W_d(F)$ has both properties by definition. $\square$

For (ii), only a basis of $U\cap B_{\le d-1}$ needs to be tested, against the
$N$ variables. A certificate can be checked without knowing how it was found.
`verify/cert_check.py` does this with its own descent of $S_3$, and
`results/verification.md` lists the instances whose certificates it accepted.

**Equivalent factorization check.** Write $A=\pi|_{B_{\le d-1}}$ and, for
each variable $x_i$, let $B_i(g)=\pi(x_i g)$ on $B_{\le d-1}$. Condition (ii)
is exactly $\ker A\subseteq\ker B_i$. Equivalently, there is a unique linear
map $T_i:\operatorname{im}A\to Z$ with $B_i=T_i A$. To check this without
storing a basis of $\ker A$, eliminate the paired columns
$(A(m),B_i(m))$ for low-degree monomials $m$: when a column reduces to zero
in its first component, its second component must also reduce to zero.
Indeed, the same elimination operations express a relation among the
original columns, so a nonzero second remainder is precisely a witness that
$\ker A\not\subseteq\ker B_i$. Conversely, if all second remainders vanish,
every relation among the $A(m)$ has zero image under $B_i$, which defines
$T_i$. This changes the verification representation, not the certificate
criterion or its mathematical conclusion. The small exact implementation is
`../semaev-degree-five-quotient-lift/check_quotient_closure_factorized.py`.
It is separate from the source-hash-pinned checker in the frozen n47 sliced
D4 protocol. The alternative complete checker
`../semaev-degree-five-quotient-lift/check_sliced_certificate_factorized.py`
also verifies the monomial universe, quotient rank, generators and image of
one while reading certificate rows on demand by default (whole-file mapping
is optional). It passes
small retained-certificate and tamper tests, including agreement at degree
five on a retained n=6 affine-sliced map and acceptance of the retained full
n=6 D5 map in its `--full-original` mode. Its time and memory use on an
actual frontier-size D5 map have not yet been measured, so it is a candidate
verifier for a separately frozen D5 protocol, not an accepted D5 result. The
source-derived size screen for the n47 slice is
`../semaev-degree-five-quotient-lift/N47-D5-CHECKER-ENVELOPE.md`.

**Remark 1.5a** (why a scalar closure certificate cannot refute an
unsatisfiable system). Let $d\ge\max(2,\max_{f\in F}\deg f)$ and suppose
$\pi:B_{\le d}\to\mathbb F_2$ satisfies Proposition 1.5 with
$\pi(1)=1$. Put $c_i=\pi(x_i)$. For any $u\in B_{\le d-1}$, condition (ii)
gives $\pi(x_i u)=c_i\pi(u)$: if $\pi(u)=0$ apply (ii) to $u$, and if
$\pi(u)=1$ apply it to $u+1$. Induction on monomial degree now gives
$\pi(m)=m(c)$ for every monomial of degree at most $d$. Thus (i) says
$f(c)=0$ for every generator: $c$ is a Boolean solution. Conversely,
evaluation at any Boolean solution is such a scalar map. For an unsatisfiable
instance, a degree-$d$ nonmembership certificate with nonzero image of $1$
therefore needs a quotient codomain of dimension at least two. A scalar
functional that merely annihilates $W_d(F)$ need not satisfy the closure
condition and is not, by itself, a Proposition 1.5 certificate.

**Lemma 1.6** (lifting a checked kernel). **Proved.** Let
$d\ge\max_{f\in F}\deg f$, and let $U\subseteq B_{\le d}$ contain $F$ and be
closed under multiplication by each variable on $U\cap B_{\le d-1}$.
Let $C_{d+1}(U)$ be the smallest subspace of $B_{\le d+1}$ containing $U$
and closed under variable multiplication on its degree-at-most-$d$ part.
Then

$$W_{d+1}(F)\subseteq C_{d+1}(U).$$

Equality holds whenever $U\subseteq W_{d+1}(F)$; in particular it holds
when $U=W_d(F)$. Thus $1\notin C_{d+1}(U)$ proves
$1\notin W_{d+1}(F)$, but $1\in C_{d+1}(U)$ alone does not prove
$1\in W_{d+1}(F)$. A checked quotient map with kernel $U$ establishes
$W_d(F)\subseteq U$, not the additional equality.

*Proof.* Since $F\subseteq U\subseteq C_{d+1}(U)$, the definition of
$W_{d+1}(F)$ gives the first inclusion. If
$U\subseteq W_{d+1}(F)$, that latter space is closed under every allowed
degree-$(d+1)$ multiplication and contains $U$, so minimality of
$C_{d+1}(U)$ gives the reverse inclusion. Finally,
$W_d(F)\subseteq W_{d+1}(F)$ by monotonicity. $\square$

**Lemma 1.7** (solutions constrain an exact-seed lift). **Proved.** Let
$S\ne\varnothing$ be the Boolean zero set of $F$, and write $I(S)$ for
its vanishing ideal in $B$. If $U\subseteq I(S)\cap B_{\le d}$, then
$C_{d+1}(U)\subseteq I(S)\cap B_{\le d+1}$ and $1\notin C_{d+1}(U)$.
Moreover, if $|S|=s$ and $d+1\ge s-1$, then

$$\operatorname{codim}(C_{d+1}(U)\subseteq B_{\le d+1})\ge s.$$

In particular, the conclusion holds for the exact seed $U=W_d(F)$.
A checked quotient kernel known only to contain $W_d(F)$ need not lie in
$I(S)$, so its provisional lift can kill $1$ even when solutions exist.

*Proof.* The vanishing ideal contains $U$ and is closed under every
variable multiplication, giving the first inclusion by minimality.
The constant one does not vanish on nonempty $S$. For the codimension,
fix $p\in S$. For each $q\in S\setminus\{p\}$, select a coordinate on
which $p$ and $q$ differ and an affine coordinate factor that is one at
$p$ and zero at $q$. Their product has degree at most $s-1$ and restricts
to the indicator of $p$ on $S$. These $s$ indicators show that evaluation
$B_{\le d+1}\to\mathbb F_2^S$ is onto when $d+1\ge s-1$. Since
$C_{d+1}(U)$ lies in its kernel, its codimension is at least $s$.
Finally, all generators vanish on $S$, and their allowed closure steps
still vanish there, so $W_d(F)\subseteq I(S)$. $\square$

**Proposition 1.8** (exactness of pivot-marked direct closure). **Proved.**
Assume $d\ge\max_{f\in F}\deg f$. Order the monomials of $B_{\le d}$ by
decreasing degree, and let $M_e$ be
the span of all Boolean products $mf$ with $f\in F$ and
$\deg m+\deg f\le e$. Begin with $A=M_d$. Pre-mark the leading columns
of an echelon basis of $M_{d-1}$. Repeatedly choose every unmarked
leading column in the degree-at-most-$(d-1)$ part of an echelon basis of
$A$, mark it, multiply its basis row by every variable, adjoin the
products to $A$, and re-echelonize. Batching and arbitrary row operations
that preserve row space do not affect the conclusion. If this process
terminates with no unmarked low-degree leading column, then $A=W_d(F)$.
If it stops early because $1\in A$, then $1\in W_d(F)$.

*Proof.* Every row of $M_d$ is in $W_d(F)$, and each newly adjoined row is
a variable multiple of a row already in $A\cap B_{\le d-1}$. Hence
$A\subseteq W_d(F)$ throughout. A leading column of a row space remains
a leading column when the row space grows: its old witness with all earlier
coordinates zero remains in the larger space. Thus a marked column cannot
disappear. For each marked low-degree pivot $p$, retain a row $r_p$ with
leading column $p$. If $p$ was pre-marked, take $r_p\in M_{d-1}$, so
$x_i r_p\in M_d\subseteq A$ for every variable $x_i$. Otherwise its
products were adjoined, so $x_i r_p\in A$ at termination.

Let $s_p$ be the final echelon row with low-degree pivot $p$. The difference
$s_p+r_p$ is in $A$ and has leading column strictly after $p$. Since both
rows have degree at most $d-1$, it is a combination of final low-degree
echelon rows with pivots after $p$. Descending induction on the marked
low-degree pivots now gives $x_i s_p\in A$ for every $i$: it holds for
$r_p$, and the products of all later rows are in $A$ by induction. Every
element of $A\cap B_{\le d-1}$ is a combination of these rows, so $A$ is
closed under all allowed variable multiplications. It contains $F$;
minimality of $W_d(F)$ gives $W_d(F)\subseteq A$. The early-stop claim
follows from the invariant $A\subseteq W_d(F)$. $\square$

`code/lfdclose2.c` implements this pivot-marked algorithm. Its `mult`
array marks leading columns, rather than immutable row identities; the
descending-induction argument is what justifies that choice. The source
audit and retained n47 D3 rank are recorded in
`../semaev-degree-five-quotient-lift/N47-D3-EXACT-CLOSURE-AUDIT.md`.

**Proposition 1.9** (exactness of a completed quotient lift). **Proved.**
Let $U\subseteq B_{\le d}$ be the kernel of an onto map
$\beta:B_{\le d}\to Q$, and suppose each coordinate of $Q$ has a monomial
representative $b_q$ with $\beta(b_q)=e_q$. Among degree-$(d+1)$ monomials,
retain as *standard* those with no degree-$d$ divisor other than such
unit representatives. For each other high monomial $t=xm$, choose a
non-representative divisor $m$ and recursively rewrite
$t$ as $\sum_q\beta(m)_q\,xb_q$, using an acyclic order. This defines an
onto linear map
$\phi:B_{\le d+1}\to P=\mathbb F_2^{H}\oplus Q$, where $H$ is the
standard high-monomial set, $\phi|_{B_{\le d}}=\beta$, and the selected
standard monomials and $b_q$ map to the basis of $P$.

Let $u_m=m+\sum_q\beta(m)_q b_q$ for every low monomial $m$. Start a
relation space $A\subseteq P$ with all $\phi(x_i u_m)$; then repeatedly
adjoin $T_i(v)=\phi(x_i\sum_q v_q b_q)$ for every variable $x_i$ and a
basis of $A\cap Q$ until the rank stabilizes. If this process completes,
then

$$\phi^{-1}(A)=C_{d+1}(U).$$

Consequently, if $U=W_d(F)$, a completed quotient export
$B_{\le d+1}\to P/A$ has kernel exactly $W_{d+1}(F)$.

*Proof.* The $u_m$ span $U$. Each low monomial is congruent modulo $U$ to
its $b_q$ expression. Each selected high rewrite differs from the original
monomial by $x_i u_m\in C_{d+1}(U)$. Acyclic substitution therefore shows
that every polynomial is congruent modulo $C_{d+1}(U)$ to its standard
representative in $P$. In particular, $\ker\phi\subseteq C_{d+1}(U)$.

Put $C=C_{d+1}(U)$. The initial relation space lies in $\phi(C)$. If
$v\in\phi(C)\cap Q$, take $c\in C$ with $\phi(c)=v$. Its low representative
$\sum_q v_q b_q$ differs from $c$ by an element of $\ker\phi\subseteq C$,
so that representative belongs to $C$; hence $T_i(v)\in\phi(C)$. Thus
the completed $A$ lies in $\phi(C)$.

Conversely, $K=\phi^{-1}(A)$ contains $U$. For $g\in K\cap B_{\le d}$,
put $v=\beta(g)\in A\cap Q$. The difference
$g+\sum_q v_q b_q$ lies in $U$, so $\phi(x_i g)$ differs from $T_i(v)$
by an initial relation in $A$. Therefore $x_i g\in K$ for every $i$;
minimality gives $C\subseteq K$. Since $A\subseteq\phi(C)$ and
$\ker\phi\subseteq C$, the reverse inclusion $K\subseteq C$ follows.
The final claim is Lemma 1.6. $\square$

**Proposition 1.10** (dimension floor for an unsatisfiable dual

$1\notin W_d(F)$, the exact quotient $B_{\le d}/W_d(F)$ has dimension

*Proof.* Put $S_r=\pi(B_{\le r})$ for $0\le r\le d$, and let
$q=\dim Z$. We have $\dim S_0=1$ and $S_d=Z$. If
$S_{r+1}=S_r$ for some $r\le d-2$, every degree-$(r+2)$ monomial

$v\in B_{\le r}$ with $\pi(v)=\pi(m)$. Then
$m+v\in\ker\pi\cap B_{\le d-1}$, so the closure condition gives
$\pi(x_i m)=\pi(x_i v)\in S_{r+1}=S_r$. Repeating this
argument shows $S_d=S_r=Z$. Thus the filtration grows strictly at
every step before reaching $Z$, and $S_{q-1}=Z$ whenever $q\le d$.

Suppose $q\le d-1$. Then $S_{d-2}=Z$. The closure condition makes
$T_i(\pi(u))=\pi(x_i u)$ a well-defined linear map on
$Z=S_{d-1}$. On the spanning set $S_{d-2}=Z$, the Boolean identities
$x_i^2=x_i$ and $x_ix_j=x_jx_i$ give
$T_i^2=T_i$ and $T_iT_j=T_jT_i$. These commuting idempotents have a
joint eigenspace decomposition indexed by Boolean assignments $c$:
the orthogonal projectors $\prod_i T_i^{c_i}(I+T_i)^{1-c_i}$ sum
to the identity.
The vector $\pi(1)$ generates $Z$ under the $T_i$, because every
monomial image is obtained by successive actions. For each $f\in F$,
$T_f\pi(1)=\pi(f)=0$; commutativity and cyclicity imply $T_f=0$
on all of $Z$. Any nonzero joint eigenspace therefore has
$f(c)=0$ for every $f\in F$, a Boolean solution, contradiction.
Hence $q\ge d$. The quotient-map consequence follows from the
converse direction of Proposition 1.5. $\square$

## 2. Structure of the $m=2$ system

Fix $\vartheta\in K$ with $\operatorname{Tr}\vartheta=1$; for odd $n$ take $\vartheta=1$.
The map $L(y)=y^2+y$ is $\mathbb F_2$-linear, with kernel $\mathbb F_2$ and
image $K_0$. Fix an $\mathbb F_2$-linear $G:K\to K$ with
$G(c)^2+G(c)=c+\operatorname{Tr}(c)\vartheta$. For odd $n$ the half-trace
$H(c)=\sum_{i=0}^{(n-1)/2}c^{4^i}$ is such a $G$. Put

$$u=\frac{X_1X_2}{z},\qquad \alpha=\frac{a_6}{z^2},\qquad w=u+G(s^2)+G(\alpha)\in B_K,$$
$$\ell_0=\operatorname{Tr}(s)+\operatorname{Tr}(\alpha)=\sum_j\operatorname{Tr}(v_j)(a_j+b_j)+\operatorname{Tr}(\alpha)\in B .$$

**Theorem 2.1** (exact reformulation). **Proved.**

(a) $S=z^2\,(w^2+w+\ell_0\,\vartheta)$.

(b) $\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb F_2}F=\{\operatorname{Tr}(\delta w):\delta\in K_0\}+\mathbb F_2\,\ell_0$.

(c) $\ell_0=\operatorname{Tr}(S/z^2)\in\operatorname{span}F$ holds for every $n$, every $V$ and every $z$.

*Proof.*

(a) Squaring is a ring endomorphism of $B_K$, so
$S=(X_1X_2+zs)^2+zX_1X_2+a_6=z^2(u^2+u+s^2+\alpha)$. Let
$h=G(s^2+\alpha)$. Coefficientwise,
$h^2+h=s^2+\alpha+\operatorname{Tr}(s^2+\alpha)\vartheta$, and
$\operatorname{Tr}(s^2)=\operatorname{Tr}(s)$. Hence
$w^2+w=u^2+u+s^2+\alpha+\ell_0\vartheta$.

(b) Put $\gamma'=\gamma z^2$. Using $\operatorname{Tr}(\gamma'w^2)=\operatorname{Tr}(\gamma'^{1/2}w)$ and $B$-linearity,
$\operatorname{Tr}(\gamma S)=\operatorname{Tr}\big((\gamma'^{1/2}+\gamma')w\big)+\operatorname{Tr}(\gamma'\vartheta)\,\ell_0$.
The map $\gamma'\mapsto(\gamma'^{1/2}+\gamma',\ \operatorname{Tr}(\gamma'\vartheta))$ sends $K$ to $K_0\times\mathbb F_2$ and is injective:
- its first coordinate vanishes only on $\gamma'\in\mathbb F_2$;
- $\operatorname{Tr}(1\cdot\vartheta)=1$.

So it is bijective.

(c) Take $\gamma'=1$. $\square$

**Corollary 2.2** (the first fall degree is $2$ for every $n$). **Proved.**
Suppose every $f_i\in F$ has degree exactly 2. This holds whenever, for each
$i$, some coefficient $\operatorname{Tr}(\gamma_i(v_j^2v_{j'}^2+zv_jv_{j'}))$ is
nonzero; it held for every instance computed here. Then:
- $d_{\rm ff}(F)=2$ in the sense of Caminata–Gorla, for every $n\ge3$ and every $V$;
- if moreover $\ell_0\ne0$, the Petit–Quisquater first fall degree is also $2$.

*Proof.* By Theorem 2.1(c), $\sum_ic_if_i=\ell_0$ for a nonzero vector
$(c_i)$, so $\sum_ic_if_i^{\rm top}=0$ is a syzygy of degree 2. Every
generator has degree 2, so the Koszul and field syzygies have degree 4 and
$\operatorname{Triv}_2=0$. No syzygy has degree below 2. $\square$

*Remark.* Semaev reports the first fall degree of $S_3(x_1,x_2,z)$ as "always
4" in experiments (§4.5). Under the two definitions above the value is 2,
because of the trace relation $\ell_0=\operatorname{Tr}(S/z^2)$. Removing it by
Gaussian elimination still leaves falls in degree 3 (Lemma 2.3). Either way
the first fall degree is bounded independently of $n$.

**Prior-art boundary.** Kosters–Yeo, *Notes on summation polynomials*
(arXiv:1503.08001, Corollary 4.11 and Remark 4.12), already derive a linear
trace combination and first fall degree 2 for the ordinary-curve $S_3$
Weil-descent setting. Kousidis–Wiemers, *On the first fall degree of summation
polynomials* (2019, Remark 3.3), expressly cite that result for $m=2$.
Accordingly, the value 2 itself must not be presented as a new discovery.
The proof here fixes this dossier's exact Boolean presentation and supports
the subsequent closure and LFD statements. The first-fall statement for the
specific chained presentation must be compared separately with the splitting
systems in Kosters–Yeo §5 and Huang–Kosters–Yeo §5.2 before any novelty
claim. See `PRIOR-ART-DEFINITION-GATE.md` for source links and definitions.

**Lemma 2.3** (explicit degree-3 falls). **Proved** for every $n$ and every $V$.

Choose a basis $\theta_1=1,\theta_2,\dots,\theta_n$ of $K$, let $(\theta_i^{\ast})$
be its trace-dual basis, and put $\omega=\operatorname{Tr}(\theta_1^{\ast}w)\in B$.
Equivalently, $\omega=\operatorname{Tr}(\mu w)$ for any $\mu$ with
$\operatorname{Tr}\mu=1$. Let
$V^\perp=\{\lambda\in K:\operatorname{Tr}(\lambda v)=0\ \forall v\in V\}$, of
dimension $n-k$. Then:

(a) $w-\omega=\sum_{i\ge2}\operatorname{Tr}(\theta_i^{\ast}w)\,\theta_i$ with $\theta_i^{\ast}\in K_0$, so $w-\omega\in K\cdot\operatorname{span}F$.

(b) For $\lambda\in V^\perp$ and $i\in\{1,2\}$, $q_{i,\lambda}:=\operatorname{Tr}(\lambda X_iw)$ lies in $M_3(F)$ and has degree $\le2$.

(c) $q_s:=\operatorname{Tr}(sw)+\operatorname{Tr}(\alpha)\,\omega$ lies in $W_3(F)$ and has degree $\le 2$.

(d) Let $\mu_j$ satisfy $\operatorname{Tr}(\mu_jv_{j'})=\delta_{jj'}$. Then
$\omega\,a_j+\operatorname{Tr}(\mu_jX_1w)\in M_3(F)$, and similarly for $b_j$
with $X_2$. So modulo the degree-3 part of the ideal, the cubic $\omega a_j$
equals a quadratic.

*Proof.*

(a) This is the coefficient expansion of $w$, using
$\operatorname{Tr}(\theta_i^{\ast})=\operatorname{Tr}(\theta_i^{\ast}\theta_1)=\delta_{i1}$ and Theorem 2.1(b).

(b) Expanding,
$\operatorname{Tr}(\lambda X_i(w-\omega))=\sum_{i'\ge2}\sum_j\operatorname{Tr}(\lambda\theta_{i'}v_j)\,x_{ij}\,\operatorname{Tr}(\theta_{i'}^{\ast}w)$,
a combination of variable times element of $\operatorname{span}F$, hence in
$M_3$. Since $\omega\in B$,
$\operatorname{Tr}(\lambda X_i\omega)=\omega\sum_jx_{ij}\operatorname{Tr}(\lambda v_j)=0$.
For the degree: $X_1w=X_1^2X_2/z+X_1G(s^2)+X_1G(\alpha)$, and $X_1^2$ and
$G(s^2)$ are linear.

(c) As in (b), $\operatorname{Tr}(s(w-\omega))\in M_3$. Also
$\operatorname{Tr}(s\omega)=\omega(\ell_0+\operatorname{Tr}\alpha)$. Since
$\ell_0\in W_3$ has degree 1, multiplying it one variable at a time by the
monomials of $\omega$ stays within degree $\le3$, so $\omega\ell_0\in W_3$.
For the degree: $sw=(X_1^2X_2+X_1X_2^2)/z+sG(s^2+\alpha)$ has degree $\le2$.

(d) $\operatorname{Tr}(\mu_jX_1(w-\omega))\in M_3$ and
$\operatorname{Tr}(\mu_jX_1\omega)=\omega a_j$. $\square$

**Theorem 2.5** (general upper bound). **Proved** for every $n$, every $V$ and every $z$.
If $F$ has no Boolean zero, then $1\in M_{k+2}(F)$. Hence
$d_{\tilde F}=d_{\rm ref}(F)\le k+2$, which is $\lceil n/2\rceil+2$ on Semaev's diagonal.

*Proof.* Change variables to $(a,c)$ with $c_j=a_j+b_j$; this preserves every
$M_d$ and $W_d$. Then $X_2=X_1+s$ with $s=\sum c_jv_j$, and

$$S=X_1^4+X_1^2s^2+z^2s^2+zX_1^2+zX_1s+a_6 .$$

Every term is at most linear in $a$ and at most linear in $c$, since
$X_1^2,X_1^4,s,s^2$ are linear. So each
$f_i=\sum_ja_jL_{ij}(c)+M_i(c)$ with $L_{ij},M_i$ affine.

For $\sigma\in\mathbb F_2^k$, the affine system $\{f_i(a,\sigma)\}$ in $a$ is
inconsistent, because $F$ has no zero. So there is
$\lambda^\sigma\in\mathbb F_2^n$ with $\sum_i\lambda^\sigma_if_i(a,\sigma)=1$
identically. Put $e_\sigma=\prod_j(c_j+\sigma_j+1)$; then
$e_\sigma g=e_\sigma g(a,\sigma)$ and $\sum_\sigma e_\sigma=1$. Hence

$$1=\sum_\sigma\sum_i\lambda_i^\sigma\,e_\sigma f_i .$$

Each $e_\sigma f_i$ is a sum of products $m\,f_i$ with $m$ a monomial in $c$
of degree $\le k$, so it lies in $M_{k+2}$. $\square$

The $k+2$ bound cannot be lowered merely from the displayed bilinear
presentation. In $\mathbb F_2[a,b,c]/(a^2-a,b^2-b,c^2-c)$, take
$g_0=1+ac$ and $g_1=1+(c+1)b$. At $c=0$, $g_0=1$; at $c=1$,
$g_1=1$, so the system is unsatisfiable. Both generators and their sum have
degree exactly two. Consequently $W_2(\{g_0,g_1\})$ is their two-dimensional
span and does not contain $1$. Yet
$(c+1)g_0+cg_1=1$, and the allowed degree-three multiplications put this
identity in $W_3$. Its last fall degree is therefore exactly three, attaining
$k+2$ for the single $c$ variable. This is a sharpness control for general
bilinear systems, not a Semaev instance; improving the Semaev bound requires
using its additional field structure.

**Proposition 2.5a** (upper bound from affine constraints on the sum).
**Proved.** Suppose the unsatisfiable $m=2$ system has $r$ affine-linear
polynomials in $c=a+b$ in $\operatorname{span}F$ whose linear parts are
linearly independent.
Then

$$1\in W_{k-r+2}(F),\qquad d_{\tilde F}\le k-r+2.$$

In particular, if $\operatorname{Tr}|_V\ne0$, Theorem 2.1(c) supplies
$\ell_0=\operatorname{Tr}(s)+\operatorname{Tr}(\alpha)$ and
$d_{\tilde F}\le k+1$. This applies to every unsatisfiable instance with
$V\not\subset K_0$. For odd $n$, a polynomial-basis subspace containing $1$
meets that condition because $\operatorname{Tr}(1)=1$; for even $n$, it must
be checked on the actual basis. The conclusion concerns the closure $W_d$; it does not assert
$1\in M_{k-r+2}$.

*Proof.* Make an invertible affine change among the $c$ variables so that
the $r$ given polynomials are the coordinate variables $y_1,\ldots,y_r$.
Write the other $k-r$ coordinates as $q$. Each $f_i(a,y,q)$ still has degree
at most two and is affine in $a$ after fixing $(y,q)$, by the expression in
Theorem 2.5. For each $\tau\in\mathbb F_2^{k-r}$ the affine system
$\{f_i(a,0,\tau)\}$ is inconsistent, so choose constants
$\lambda_i^\tau\in\mathbb F_2$ with
$\sum_i\lambda_i^\tau f_i(a,0,\tau)=1$ identically in $a$.

Let $e_\tau(q)$ be the Boolean indicator of $q=\tau$, and set
$Q=\sum_{\tau,i}e_\tau(q)\lambda_i^\tau f_i(a,y,q)$.
Each summand belongs to $W_D(F)$ for $D=k-r+2$: expand the indicator into
monomials and multiply a generator by at most $k-r$ variables. At $y=0$,
the indicator identity gives $Q(a,0,q)=1$. Therefore every monomial of
$Q+1$ contains some $y_j$, and we can partition its monomials to write
$Q+1=\sum_{j=1}^r y_jH_j$ with $\deg H_j\le D-1$.
Each $y_j$ lies in $\operatorname{span}F\subseteq W_D(F)$. Multiplication by
the monomials of $H_j$, one variable at a time, stays inside degree $D$;
hence $y_jH_j\in W_D(F)$. Thus $1\in W_D(F)$, and Proposition 1.2 gives the
last-fall-degree bound. The trace specialization follows from Theorem
2.1(c). $\square$

**Proposition 2.5b** (exact slicing by an affine equation). **Proved.**
Suppose $y=b_j+L(a,b_{\ne j})$ is a nonconstant affine polynomial in
$\operatorname{span}F$, where $L$ is affine and does not involve $b_j$.
Let $G=F|_{b_j=L}$ be the system in the remaining $2k-1$ Boolean variables.
For every $d\ge2$,

$$W_d(G)=W_d(F)\cap B'_{\le d},\qquad
 1\in W_d(G)\iff1\in W_d(F),$$

where $B'$ is the Boolean ring without $b_j$, embedded in $B$. In particular,
an independently checked degree-five nonmembership certificate for $G$
proves $1\notin W_5(F)$. When $F$ is unsatisfiable, so is $G$, and this gives
$d_{\tilde F}\ge6$ by Proposition 1.2. For $V\not\subset K_0$, a coordinate
$b_j$ with $\operatorname{Tr}(v_j)=1$ can be chosen using $y=\ell_0$.

*Proof.* Let $\rho:B\to B'$ substitute $b_j=L$. Since $\rho$ does not
increase degree and sends every old variable to an affine polynomial,
restriction sends the generators and every allowed closure step of $W_d(F)$
into $W_d(G)$. Hence $\rho(W_d(F))\subseteq W_d(G)$.

Conversely, for each quadratic or lower-degree generator $f_i$, multilinearity
in $b_j$ gives $f_i+\rho(f_i)=y h_i$ for an affine $h_i\in B'$. The polynomial
$y$ is in $\operatorname{span}F$ and has degree one, so $yh_i\in W_d(F)$ for
$d\ge2$. Thus every generator of $G$ lies in $W_d(F)$. Its closure steps
multiply only by the remaining variables and are also allowed in $W_d(F)$.
Therefore $W_d(G)\subseteq W_d(F)\cap B'_{\le d}$. Applying $\rho$ to an
element of the latter intersection proves the opposite inclusion. $\square$

The same restriction transfers a dual certificate: if
$\pi_G:B'_{\le d}\to Z$ satisfies Proposition 1.5 for $G$, then
$\pi_F=\pi_G\circ\rho$ is onto, annihilates $F$, and satisfies the closure
condition for $F$. Indeed $\rho(x)$ is affine for every original variable
$x$, so its product with a low-degree member of $\ker\pi_G$ is annihilated
by the checked closure condition. Also $\pi_F(1)=\pi_G(1)$. This describes
a certificate transfer, not a completed reduced-instance computation.

Conversely, a dual certificate for $F$ restricts to one for $G$ **with the
same quotient dimension**. Let $\pi_F:B_{\le d}\to Z$ satisfy Proposition 1.5
and set $\pi_G=\pi_F|_{B'_{\le d}}$. For every monomial $m$ containing $b_j$,
$m+\rho(m)=y(m/b_j)$ in the Boolean ring. Its degree is at most $d$, and
$y\in W_d(F)$, so the closure rule puts $m+\rho(m)$ in
$W_d(F)\subseteq\ker\pi_F$. The equality is trivial for monomials omitting
$b_j$; by linearity $\pi_F=\pi_G\circ\rho$. Since $\pi_F$ is onto, $\pi_G$
is onto as well. It annihilates each generator $\rho(f)$ of $G$, because
$\pi_G(\rho(f))=\pi_F(f)=0$. The low-degree kernel closure under the retained
variables is inherited from $\pi_F$, and $\pi_G(1)=\pi_F(1)$. Thus a checked
original degree-four map can be projected without losing codimension when an
affine relation is in $W_4(F)$; checking the projected bytes independently
remains necessary for a computational certificate claim.

**Corollary 2.5c** (slicing from a later affine fall). **Proved.** Fix
$d\ge2$. The equality and nonmembership transfer of Proposition 2.5b still
hold if its affine polynomial $y$ belongs to $W_d(F)$ rather than to
$\operatorname{span}F$. In particular, an affine $y\in W_s(F)$ permits exact
slicing at every degree $d\ge\max(2,s)$.

*Proof.* The restriction map $\rho$ still sends $W_d(F)$ into $W_d(G)$.
Conversely, $f_i+\rho(f_i)=y h_i$ with $h_i$ affine in the retained variables.
Since $y\in W_d(F)$ has degree one, the closure rule puts $y h_i$ in
$W_d(F)$; hence every generator of $G$ belongs to $W_d(F)$. Closure under
the retained variables gives $W_d(G)\subseteq W_d(F)\cap B'_{\le d}$, and
applying $\rho$ gives the reverse inclusion. Monotonicity of $W_d$ proves
the final assertion. $\square$

**Proposition 2.5d** (quadratic-rank formula). **Proved.** Let
$P=\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb F_2}\{uv:u,v\in V\}\subseteq K$ and $z\ne0$.
The rank of the quadratic coefficient matrix of the $n$ descended $m=2$
equations is

$$\dim_{\mathbb F_2}P-\mathbf 1_{z\in P}.$$

Consequently, the space of equation combinations whose quadratic part
vanishes has dimension $n-\dim P+\mathbf 1_{z\in P}$. Its members are affine
polynomials in $c=a+b$ or zero. More precisely, identify coordinate-equation
combinations with trace functionals $u\mapsto\operatorname{Tr}(\mu u)$ and put
$A=L_z(P)^\perp$. The linear rank of the resulting affine constraints is

$$r=\operatorname{rank}\left(A\longrightarrow\mathbb F_2^k,\quad
\mu\longmapsto
(\operatorname{Tr}(\mu z^2v_j^2))_{j=0}^{k-1}\right).$$

Equivalently, with $Q=L_z(P)$ and $U=z^2V^2$,
$r=\dim(Q+U)-\dim Q$, since the field trace pairing is nondegenerate.

For every unsatisfiable such system, Proposition 2.5a therefore gives the
computable upper bound $d_{\tilde F}\le k-r+2$. The constant terms depend on
$a_6$, but do not affect $r$.

*Proof.* In the invertible coordinates $(a,c)$, $c=a+b$, the expression in
Theorem 2.5 shows that the only quadratic terms are
$X_1^2s^2+zX_1s$. The coefficient of $a_ic_j$ is therefore
$(v_iv_j)^2+zv_iv_j=L_z(v_iv_j)$, where $L_z(u)=u^2+zu$ is an
$\mathbb F_2$-linear map on $K$. The $n$ coordinate equations have quadratic
coefficient rank $\dim L_z(P)$, independently of the chosen field basis.
Since $\ker L_z=\{0,z\}$, rank-nullity on $P$ gives the formula. An
invertible Boolean coordinate change from $(a,b)$ to $(a,c)$ preserves the
degree filtration and induces an isomorphism on $B_{\le2}/B_{\le1}$, so this
is also the rank in the original Boolean coordinates. In fact, the full
expression in Theorem 2.5 is
$S=L_z(X_1^2+X_1s)+z^2s^2+a_6$.
Both $X_1^2$ and $X_1s$ have coefficients in $P$, so every $\mu\in A$
annihilates the first term. Its remaining equation combination is exactly
$\operatorname{Tr}(\mu z^2s^2)+\operatorname{Tr}(\mu a_6)$, whose coefficient
of $c_j$ is the displayed trace. A basis of the image supplies $r$
independent affine linear parts in $c$. The kernel of that image map is
$(Q+U)^\perp$, while its domain is $Q^\perp$, giving the equivalent
dimension formula. Proposition 2.5a applies.
$\square$

**Proposition 2.5e** (one-way certificate transfer under arbitrary affine
restriction). **Proved.** Let $\rho:B\to B'$ set any chosen Boolean variables
to affine forms in the retained variables, and let $G=\rho(F)$. For every
$d\ge2$, $\rho(W_d(F))\subseteq W_d(G)$. Thus a checked certificate
$1\notin W_d(G)$ proves $1\notin W_d(F)$, even when the substituted affine
equations are **not** consequences of $F$. If $F$ has no Boolean zero, this
gives $d_{\tilde F}\ge d+1$. More generally, an onto quotient certificate
$\pi_G$ for $G$ lifts to $\pi_G\circ\rho$ for $F$ with the same codimension
lower bound; it yields the same last-fall-degree conclusion whenever that
bound exceeds the exact solution count of $F$.

*Proof.* Affine substitution does not increase Boolean degree and sends
each generator of $F$ to a generator of $G$. It also sends an allowed
product $x_i g$ to $\rho(x_i)\rho(g)$, where $\rho(x_i)$ is affine. The
constant part is already in $W_d(G)$, and each variable part is obtained by
an allowed closure step; applying this argument inductively proves the
inclusion. Since $\rho(1)=1$, nonmembership transfers back to $F$. For the
dual statement, $\rho$ is onto, so $\pi_G\circ\rho$ is onto and annihilates
the original generators. If $g$ has degree below $d$ and lies in its kernel,
then $\rho(g)$ has degree below $d$ and lies in $\ker\pi_G$; the affine
expansion of $\rho(x_i)$ shows that $\pi_G(\rho(x_i g))=0$. The solution-count
claims follow from Propositions 1.2 and 1.4. $\square$

**Proposition 2.5f** (one-step closure preserves an internal affine slice).
**Proved.** Let $U\subseteq B_{\le d}$ be a linear space closed under all
degree-$d$ variable multiplications, and suppose the affine polynomial
$e=x+L(y)$ lies in $U$, where $x$ is one Boolean variable and $L$ uses only
the retained variables $y$. Let $\rho:B\to B'$ set $x=L(y)$ and put
$U'=\rho(U)\subseteq B'_{\le d}$. Then restriction induces an isomorphism

$$B_{\le d+1}/C_{d+1}(U)\ \cong\
  B'_{\le d+1}/C_{d+1}(U').$$

In particular, $1\in C_{d+1}(U)$ if and only if
$1\in C_{d+1}(U')$. The statement applies to the kernel of any checked
degree-$d$ quotient that kills $e$; exactness $U=W_d(F)$ is not required.

*Proof.* Every $f\in B_{\le d}$ has a unique form $f=f_0(y)+xf_1(y)$,
with $\deg f_1\le d-1$, and $f+\rho(f)=ef_1$. Since $e\in U$ has degree
one, degree-$d$ closure puts $ef_1\in U$. Thus the canonical lift of
every member of $U'$ belongs to $U$, and
$\ker\rho\cap B_{\le d}\subseteq U$. The same multiplication argument in
degree $d+1$ gives
$\ker\rho\cap B_{\le d+1}\subseteq C_{d+1}(U)$.
Affine substitution sends each generator and allowed multiplication of
$C_{d+1}(U)$ into $C_{d+1}(U')$, so
$\rho(C_{d+1}(U))\subseteq C_{d+1}(U')$. Conversely, canonical lifts of
$U'$ lie in $U$, and multiplication by a retained variable commutes with
lifting, so $C_{d+1}(U')\subseteq C_{d+1}(U)$ under the natural inclusion
$B'\subseteq B$. These inclusions and the kernel inclusion prove the
quotient isomorphism and the assertion about one. $\square$

For the named n50 polynomial03 target, the retained affine relation used

slice maps have the same kernel as recorded by the conversion source, this

**Proposition 2.6** (a general lower bound: $d_{\tilde F}\ge3$). **Proved** for every $n$.
Write $T_a=\sum_j\operatorname{Tr}(v_j)a_j$ and $T_b=\sum_j\operatorname{Tr}(v_j)b_j$
(the linear parts of $\operatorname{Tr}X_1$ and $\operatorname{Tr}X_2$). Let
$(V\!\cdot\!V)^\perp=\{\mu\in K:\operatorname{Tr}(\mu xy)=0\ \forall x,y\in V\}$. Assume:

- **(G1)** $z^{-1}K_0\cap(V\!\cdot\!V)^\perp=0$. This holds whenever the products $xy$ ($x,y\in V$) span $K$.
- **(G2)** Needed only if $V\not\subset K_0$: no $\mu\in z^{-1}K_0$ satisfies $\operatorname{Tr}(\mu xy)=\operatorname{Tr}(x)\operatorname{Tr}(y)$ for all $x,y\in V$.

Assume also $\ell_0\ne1$. Then $W_2(F)=\operatorname{span}F+\ell_0B_{\le1}$,
and its degree-$\le1$ part is $\mathbb F_2\ell_0$. Consequently, if $F$ has no
Boolean zero, then $1\notin W_2(F)$ and $d_{\tilde F}\ge3=d_{\rm ff}+1$.

The excluded case $\ell_0=1$ happens exactly when $V\subset K_0$ and
$\operatorname{Tr}\alpha=1$. The system is then trivially unsatisfiable, with
$1\in\operatorname{span}F$.

*Proof.* By Theorem 2.1(b) every element of $\operatorname{span}F$ is
$\operatorname{Tr}(\delta w)+e\ell_0$ for some $\delta\in K_0$ and
$e\in\mathbb F_2$. Its degree-2 part is the bilinear form
$\beta_\delta=\operatorname{Tr}((\delta/z)X_1X_2)$, because $G(s^2)$ is linear.

*Case $V\subset K_0$.* Then $T_a=T_b=0$, so $\ell_0=\operatorname{Tr}\alpha$
is a constant, which must be $0$. Then $\ell_0B_{\le1}=0$, and an element
$\operatorname{Tr}(\delta w)$ has degree $\le1$ only if $\beta_\delta=0$, that
is $\delta=0$ by (G1). So the degree-$\le1$ part of $W_2=\operatorname{span}F$
is $0=\mathbb F_2\ell_0$.

*Case $V\not\subset K_0$.* Then $T_a\ne0$ and $T_b\ne0$. In the graded ring
$A$ we have $\ell\cdot\ell'=0$ for linear $\ell,\ell'$ iff they are linearly
dependent. Hence the degree-2 part of $\ell_0L$, for an affine $L$, is
$(T_a+T_b)L^{\rm top}$. Its $aa$- and $bb$-components vanish only when
$L^{\rm top}\in\{0,T_a,T_b,T_a+T_b\}$, which leaves
$(\ell_0L)^{\rm top}\in\{0,T_aT_b\}$.

Now suppose $g=\operatorname{Tr}(\delta w)+e\ell_0+\ell_0L$ has degree $\le1$.
Then $\beta_\delta\in\{0,T_aT_b\}$.

- $\beta_\delta=T_aT_b\ne0$ is excluded by (G2).
- $\beta_\delta=0$ forces $\delta=0$ by (G1). Then $(\ell_0L)^{\rm top}=0$, so
  $L^{\rm top}\in\{0,\ell_0^{\rm top}\}$ and $\ell_0L\in\mathbb F_2\ell_0$,
  using $\ell_0^2=\ell_0$.

So the degree-$\le1$ part of $\operatorname{span}F+\ell_0B_{\le1}$ is
$\mathbb F_2\ell_0$. Multiplying it by variables adds nothing new, so the
closure stops at $W_2=\operatorname{span}F+\ell_0B_{\le1}$, and
$1\in W_2\iff1\in\mathbb F_2\ell_0\iff\ell_0=1$. $\square$

`verify/g1g2_check.py` checks the hypotheses. They hold on every random-$V$
and every trace-zero-$V$ instance computed.

For the polynomial basis $V=\langle1,t,\dots,t^{k-1}\rangle$ with $n=2k-1$,
(G2) fails for about half of the targets. In that case
$\operatorname{Tr}(xy\mu)=\operatorname{Tr}(x)\operatorname{Tr}(y)$ is solved by
the dual-basis element of $1$, whenever it lies in $z^{-1}K_0$. A second
linear equation then appears at degree 2. For those instances
$1\notin W_2$ is computer-verified instead (§3).

Proposition 2.6 is sharp: $d_{\tilde F}=3$ is attained at $n=17,19$ (§3).

**Corollary 2.7** (the whole chained system (5) has first fall degree 2). **Proved** for every $t\ge2$.
In Semaev's system (5) for any $t\ge2$, the last equation is
$S_3(y,x_t,R_X)=0$, where $y=x_1\in V$ if $t=2$ and $y=u_{t-2}\in K$ (free) if
$t\ge3$. Its descent contains the affine-linear consequence

$$\operatorname{Tr}\!\big(S_3(Y,X_t,R_X)/R_X^2\big)=\operatorname{Tr}(Y+X_t)+\operatorname{Tr}(a_6/R_X^2).$$

Here $Y$ and $X_t$ are the linear forms of $y$ and $x_t$ in the Boolean
variables. Suppose the descended coordinates of this last equation all have
degree 2, which holds on every instance computed. Then the descended system
(5) has $d_{\rm ff}=2$ in the Caminata–Gorla sense. It is also 2 in the
Petit–Quisquater sense when the form above is nonzero.

*Proof.* The proof of Theorem 2.1(a) and (c) uses only that $X_1$ and $X_2$
are linear forms in $B_K$, never that they range over $V$. So it applies
verbatim to $(Y,X_t)$. The resulting combination is a degree-2 syzygy of the
top forms that involves only quadratic generators. Every trivial syzygy has
degree $\ge4$: Koszul syzygies have degree $\deg f_i+\deg f_j$, and field
syzygies $f^{\rm top}e_f$ have degree $2\deg f$. $\square$

The C19a instance ($t=3$) shows this directly: $\dim M_2\cap B_{\le d}=(0,1,19)$,
one affine-linear form already in the span of the equations.

This contradicts the value "4" that Semaev states for the first fall degree
(§4.5 of the paper). His argument there concerns only the cubic equations
$S_3(u_i,u_{i+1},x_{i+2})$, which do have a fall at degree 4, and the
experiments he cites for $S_3(x_1,x_2,z)$. Under the definition he quotes,
the trace relation makes the first fall degree of the system 2.

| 19 | 7 | random | 2 | **no, 0 of 2** | $d_{F4}\ge5$: counterexample |
| 21 | 7 | random | 2 | **no, 0 of 2** (8 rounds each) | $d_{F4}\ge5$: counterexample |
| 17 | 6 | polynomial | 2 | yes, 2 of 2 (5 rounds) | $d_{\tilde F}\le4$, consistent with Semaev Tables 1–2 |
| 19 | 7 | polynomial | 2 | yes, 2 of 2 (6 rounds) | $d_{\tilde F}\le4$, consistent with Semaev Table 2 |
| 21 | 7 | polynomial | 2 | yes, 2 of 2 (5 rounds) | $d_{\tilde F}\le4$, consistent with Semaev Table 2 |

At $m=t=3$ the subspace decides the outcome at $n=19$ and $n=21$. All four
polynomial-$V$ instances there are refuted at degree 4, and all four random-$V$
instances are not. Their Macaulay spaces $M_4$ have the same dimension and
differ only in $\dim M_4\cap B_{\le3}$: $2344$ against $2338$ at $n=19$, and
$2710$ against $2702$ at $n=21$. Yet their closures diverge.

**Proposition 3.7** (general upper bound for the chained system). **Proved** for every $t\ge2$, $n$, $V$ and $z\ne0$.
Let $F$ be the Weil descent of Semaev's system (5) for $t$:

$$S_3(x_1,x_2,u_1)=0,\qquad S_3(u_{j-1},x_{j+1},u_j)=0\quad(2\le j\le t-1),$$

with the convention $u_{t-1}:=z$. Here $x_1,\dots,x_t\in V$, and
$u_1,\dots,u_{t-2}\in K$ are free. If $F$ has no Boolean zero, then
$1\in W_{(t-1)k+t}(F)$, so

$$d_{\tilde F}\le(t-1)k+t .$$

For $t=2$ this is the bound $k+2$ of Theorem 2.5. For $t=3$ it is $2k+3$.
Each auxiliary element $u_j$ has $n$ Boolean coordinates but adds only 1 to
the bound.

*Proof.* (a) *One link.* For $x,y\in K$ put $\Lambda_{x,y}(u)=(x+y)^2u^2+xy\,u$.
This map is $\mathbb F_2$-linear in $u$, and

$$S_3(u,x,y)=\Lambda_{x,y}(u)+x^2y^2+a_6 .$$

Its kernel is $\{0,\,xy/(x+y)^2\}$ if $x\ne y$, it is $\{0\}$ if $x=y\ne0$, and
it is all of $K$ if $x=y=0$. In the last case $S_3(u,0,0)=a_6\ne0$ for every
$u$. So for fixed $x,y$ the $n$ coordinate functions of $S_3(u,x,y)$ are affine
in the coordinates of $u$. Either their linear parts have rank $\ge n-1$, or
they are constants, not all zero. Since $S_3$ is symmetric, the same holds in
each argument.

(b) *Fixing $x_2,\dots,x_t$.* Let $\sigma$ assign the $(t-1)k$ coordinates of
$x_2,\dots,x_t$, and let $F_\sigma$ be the restricted system. The degree-3
monomials of $F$ come only from the terms $x_1x_2u_1$ and $u_{j-1}x_{j+1}u_j$,
and each of these contains a fixed coordinate. So $F_\sigma$ has degree
$\le2$. We show $1\in W_t(F_\sigma)$.

Two facts are used.
- Restricting a variable maps $W_d(G)$ into $W_d$ of the restricted system.
  Restriction is a ring map that does not raise degree and sends each
  variable to a variable or a constant, so it respects both closure rules.
- An invertible affine change of variables preserves $W_d$ (Lemma 1.3).

Say $u_i$ is *pinned* at a node if $W_2$ of that node contains $n$ affine
forms in the coordinates of $u_i$ whose only common zero is one point
$\gamma_i\in\mathbb F_2^n$. Then $W_2$ also contains each form
$u_{i,l}+\gamma_{i,l}$. Put $\gamma_{t-1}=z$, so $u_{t-1}$ is pinned at the
root.

Build a decision tree on $F_\sigma$, pinning $u_{t-2},\dots,u_1$ in turn. At a
node where $u_{j+1}$ is pinned, take the coordinates $g$ of the link
$S_3(u_j,x_{j+2},u_{j+1})$. They have degree $\le2$, so $g\in W_2$. In the
Boolean ring,

$$g-g|_{u_{j+1}=\gamma_{j+1}}=\sum_i(u_{j+1,i}+\gamma_{j+1,i})\,h_i,\qquad\deg h_i\le1 .$$

This holds because $g$ is multilinear of degree $\le2$. Each pinning form
$u_{j+1,i}+\gamma_{j+1,i}$ is a degree-1 element of $W_2$, and the closure
rule multiplies it by variables. So the coordinates of
$S_3(u_j,x_{j+2},\gamma_{j+1})$ lie in $W_2$. For $j=t-2$ there is nothing to
substitute, since $u_{t-1}=z$.
By (a) they are affine in $u_j$. Gaussian elimination now gives one of three
outcomes:
- $1\in W_2$, and the node is a leaf;
- $n$ independent forms, and $u_j$ is pinned;
- $n-1$ independent forms. The node then splits on a complementary linear
  form $\varepsilon_j$ in the coordinates of $u_j$, and $u_j$ is pinned in
  both children.

At a node where $u_1$ is pinned, the same substitution puts the coordinates of
$S_3(x_1,x_2,\gamma_1)$ in $W_2$. They are affine in the coordinates of $x_1$.
A common Boolean zero of them would give a zero of $F$, built from it, $\sigma$
and the pinned values $\gamma_1,\dots,\gamma_{t-2}$, each of which satisfies
its link by construction. $F$ has no zero, so the affine forms are
inconsistent and $1\in W_2$.

So every leaf has $1\in W_2$, the tree has depth $\le t-2$, and every node has
degree $\le2$. Lemma 1.3, applied after an affine change of variables that
makes $\varepsilon_j$ a variable, gives $1\in W_{2+(t-2)}(F_\sigma)=W_t(F_\sigma)$.

(c) *Lifting.* $F$ has degree $\le3\le t$ for $t\ge3$, and degree 2 for $t=2$.
By (b), $1\in W_t(F_\sigma)$ for all $2^{(t-1)k}$ assignments $\sigma$. Lemma 1.3,
iterated over the coordinates of $x_2,\dots,x_t$, gives
$1\in W_{t+(t-1)k}(F)$. $\square$

For $t=3$ the plain indicator argument of Theorem 2.5, which fixes all $3k$
$x$-coordinates, gives only $1\in M_{3k+3}(F)$.

Part (b) was checked on a small instance, `gen3.py 13 5 1 77 rand unsat`
($n=13$, $k=5$, random $V$, unsatisfiable), with `verify/check_prop37.py`.
All $2^{10}$ restrictions of $x_2,x_3$ have degree $\le2$, and each has
$1\in W_2$; 608 of them already have $1\in W_1$. So the split on
$\varepsilon_1$ was never needed there. That is **observed** on this
instance, not proved. It would not improve the bound anyway: step (c) splits
systems of degree 3, so Lemma 1.3 needs $d\ge3$ there.

For C19a this gives $5\le d_{\tilde F}\le17$. Branching on one variable does
not close the gap: both restrictions $x_{1,1}=0$ and $x_{1,1}=1$ (variable
index 19) also have $1\notin W_4$, after 9 rounds each. So C19a's exact last
fall degree is not determined here.

## 4. A heuristic model of the growth (not a proof)

Lemma 2.3 and Observation 2.4 say that for random $V$ the closure's first
falls add exactly $2n$ quadratics to the original $n$. That gives $3n$
quadratic equations in $N=n+1$ variables, including $\ell_0$ and its
multiples. If the system behaves like a *semi-regular* Boolean system after
these falls (Bardet–Faugère–Salvy), its degree of regularity is the index of
the first non-positive coefficient of $(1+z)^{N}/(1+z^2)^{3n}$. This is
**model A**.

| step | model A predicts first $n$ | observed |
|---|---|---|
| $3\to4$ | 21 | random $V$: at $n=15$ ($n=11,13$ give 3); polynomial $V$: at $n=21$ (2 of 4 instances) |
| $4\to5$ | 37 | random $V$: at $n=35$; polynomial $V$: at $n=45$ (P43a refuted at degree 4, P45a not) |
| $5\to6$ | 55 | not reached |
| $6\to7$ | 75 | — |

Asymptotically model A gives $d\approx n/22$ in this range, and a slope of
$\approx0.037$ for $n\to\infty$. So it predicts linear growth of the last
fall degree, with $d\approx 9$ near $n=200$.

The plain semi-regular model with only the original $n$ equations is
**model B**. It predicts degree 5 already at $n=15$, which the data rule out.
So it is precisely the explicit first falls that make $S_3$ "easier than
random", and only by an $n$-independent amount.

Model A is a heuristic. For random $V$ it predicts the $4\to5$ step within
one step in $n$. It places the $3\to4$ step too late: random-$V$ systems
already need degree 4 at $n=15$. It also misses the other families:

- for polynomial $V$, with $5n-1$ quadratics, it predicts $3\to4$ only at
  $n=31$, and $4\to5$ at $n=59$. Both observed steps come earlier: $3\to4$ at
  $n=21$ and $4\to5$ at $n=45$;
- for trace-zero $V$, with $2n-1$ quadratics, it predicts $4\to5$ at $n=25$
  against the observed $n=33$.

These deviations come from structure beyond the counted quadratics, such as
the cubic identities of Lemma 2.3(d). No statement in §§1–3 depends on this
section.

## 5. What is and is not established

- **Growth versus constancy.** On the instances computed, the last fall degree
  rises $3\to4\to5$ as $n$ grows ($n\le 21$, $23\le n\le 33$, $n\ge35$ for
  random $V$), while the first fall degree stays at $2$. This is a rigorous
  separation *at the named instances*. It is **not** a proof that
  $d_{\tilde F}\to\infty$. The only proved general bounds are
  $3\le d_{\tilde F}$ on the instances listed and $d_{\tilde F}\le k+2$ in
  general.
- **The polynomial subspace.** Semaev's own $m=t=2$ data point ($n=40$, Table
  2) uses his default polynomial subspace. Polynomial subspaces have $2n-1$
  extra degree-3 falls (Observation 2.4), and our polynomial-$V$ instances are
  refuted at degree 4 through $n=43$, including his $n=40$ cell. The single
  instance P45a at $n=45$ is not refuted at degree 4. This is one instance per
  $n$ at $n\ge41$. It places the polynomial family's $4\to5$ step near
  $n=45$; it does not show that every polynomial-$V$ instance at $n\ge45$
  needs degree 5.
- **$m\ge3$.** Semaev's asymptotic algorithm uses $m\approx\sqrt{n/\ln n}$ and
  the chained systems with auxiliary variables. Here they are computed only at
  $m=t=3$ and $n\le21$ (Theorem 3.5 and the table after Corollary 3.6). The
  proved general bound for system (5) is $d_{\tilde F}\le(t-1)k+t$
  (Proposition 3.7). With $k=\lceil n/m\rceil$ and $t=m$ this is about $n$,
  far above the observed values. The counterexamples falsify the assumption
  *as stated*. They do not by themselves bound the complexity of the
  algorithm as $m$ grows.
- **No cost claim.** At $m=2$ root-finding decides each decomposition in
  $2^k$ field operations, and Pollard rho dominates the DLP. Nothing here
  speeds up, or slows down, an ECDLP attack.
