# The graded binary bilinear multiplication complex

This is a structural lemma for the characteristic-two lower-bound problem,
not a last-fall-degree bound. It identifies the exact low-degree kernel for
**one** quadratic coordinate combination. The full Semaev m=2 system has
many coordinate equations and additional cross-equation syzygies; those must
be controlled before this lemma can support a closure separator.

Let `A` be the associated graded algebra of the Boolean ring in `N`
variables over `F2`. Thus

    A = F2[x_1,...,x_N]/(x_1^2,...,x_N^2).

Let `q` be a bilinear form in two disjoint variable blocks, with matrix
rank `r` over `F2`. Multiplication `d(h)=q*h` has degree two and `d^2=0`:
every positive-degree element of `A` has square zero in characteristic two.
The resulting graded complex has

    H^j(A,d) = 0                         for j<r,
    dim H^j(A,d) = 2^r * binom(N-2r,j-r) for every j,

with an out-of-range binomial interpreted as zero. In particular, for
`j<r`, every homogeneous relation `q*h=0` of degree `j` is the obvious
relation `h=q*t` for some `t` of degree `j-2`. The convention is that no
such nonzero `h` exists when `j<2`.

**Proof.** Independent invertible linear changes in the two blocks put
`q` into `u_1*v_1+...+u_r*v_r`. Such changes are valid in `A`: the square
of every linear form is the sum of the squares of its variables and is
zero. Separate the `r` paired two-variable algebras from the `N-2r`
unused variables. In one pair, multiplication by `u*v` sends `1` to `u*v`
and sends `u`, `v`, and `u*v` to zero. Its complex is the direct sum of a
contractible pair `1 -> u*v` and the two degree-one homology classes `u,v`.
The total multiplication by `q` is the tensor-product differential (the
signs disappear over `F2`). Over a field, tensoring the contractible
summands remains contractible, while choosing one of `u_i,v_i` from each
pair yields `2^r` homology classes, all in degree `r`. Every unused
variable has zero differential and contributes the ordinary square-zero
algebra on `N-2r` variables. Taking its degree-`j-r` component gives the
formula. Vanishing of homology below `r` is exactly the stated equality
of kernel and image. QED.

For the Semaev m=2 presentation, Proposition 2.5d of
`../semaev-m2-last-fall-degree/theorem-dossier.md` writes the quadratic
part of the coordinate combination indexed by `mu in K` as the bilinear
form with entries

    M_mu[i,j] = Tr(mu * ( (v_i*v_j)^2 + z*v_i*v_j )).

The lemma applies to each nonzero `M_mu` with its **own** rank. It says the
prime-field proof's raw injectivity of top-form multiplication cannot hold
in binary degree two and higher: `q*q=0`. It also gives a stronger usable
replacement than that obstruction alone: below rank `r`, every single-form
kernel element is generated by this explicit square-zero relation.

The next proof obligation is joint rather than single-form. One must compute
the low-degree syzygies of the entire space `{q_mu : mu in K}` and show that
all resulting lower-degree falls are already represented inside the full
iterated closure. Only then could a dimension gap plus an exact solution
count or UNSAT bridge yield a binary LFD lower bound. No such joint claim is
made here.

For the frozen `fresh-n47-rand-00` plan,
`rank_binary_top_forms.py` evaluated the matrix formula exactly over its
named `GF(2^47)` representation. The 47 basis trace functionals `mu=2^i`
have rank histogram `22:4, 23:21, 24:22`; selected combinations give
rank `24` for `mu=1`, `22` for `mu=2`, `24` for `mu=3`, and `23` for
`mu=z`. The input SHA-256 and machine-readable output are in
`validation/n47-rand00-top-form-ranks-001.json`. Thus the single-form
complex is exact below at least degree 22 for **each of these 47 basis
forms**. This does not bound the ranks of all `2^47-1` combinations or
control relations between different forms; in particular it is not an
n47 D5 closure result.

## A parameter-uniform joint degree-one lower bound

For every `n>=3`, nonzero `z in K`, and nonzero `k`-dimensional `V`, the
degree-one-multiplier kernel of **all n quadratic coordinate top forms**
has dimension at least

    2*k + 2*(n-k) = 2*n.

More precisely, it contains a direct sum of `2k` variable multiples of a
single trace relation and two `n-k`-dimensional families indexed by
`V^perp`. This is a theorem about top-form syzygies, not an LFD bound.

**Proof.** Work in the invertible variables `(a,c=a+b)` and set
`X=sum a_i*v_i`, `s=sum c_i*v_i`. The quadratic top form indexed by
`gamma in K` is

    q_gamma = Tr(T(gamma)*X*s/z),
    T(gamma)=sqrt(gamma*z^2)+gamma*z^2.

Indeed the quadratic part of `S3` is `(X*s)^2+z*X*s`, and moving the
square through the trace gives this formula. `T` maps onto `K_0`: its
image has trace zero, and its kernel is exactly `{0,z^(-2)}`, since
`sqrt(y)+y=0` iff `y in F2`. Choose an `F2`-linear right inverse
`R:K_0 -> K`.

The coefficient-valued linear form `z^(-2)*ell(a,c)` gives a top syzygy
for every homogeneous linear Boolean form `ell`; these `2k` vectors are
independent. For `lambda in V^perp`, both `lambda*X` and `lambda*s` have
trace zero coefficientwise. Therefore the linear multipliers
`R(lambda*X)` and `R(lambda*s)` are defined. Their products with the top
equations sum respectively to

    Tr(lambda*X^2*s/z),   Tr(lambda*X*s^2/z).

Frobenius squares `X^2` and `s^2` have Boolean degree one, so both
expressions have degree at most two and their degree-three parts vanish.
Thus both are degree-one top syzygies. Modulo the `2k` trace multiples,
applying `T` to a linear combination gives
`lambda_1*X+lambda_2*s`. The `a` and `c` variables are disjoint and `V`
contains a nonzero element, so this polynomial vanishes only when
`lambda_1=lambda_2=0`. Each `V^perp` family has dimension `n-k`, and the
three families form the asserted direct sum. QED.

This lower bound can be strict: it makes no assertion that other
cross-equation syzygies are absent. When it is sharp, as on the named n47
input below, the displayed trace and annihilator families classify the
entire first joint layer. The remaining degree-two-and-higher multiplier
layers are the obstacle to a growing binary LFD lower bound.

## Exact joint linear-multiplier syzygies on the named n47 input

`joint_binary_top_syzygies.py` now computes the **whole** degree-one
multiplier map for the same named input. Its domain consists of 47 top
equations times each of 48 Boolean variables, so has dimension 2,256; its
image in squarefree cubics has rank 2,162. The kernel therefore has
dimension 94. The source independently reconstructs all 576 bilinear
coefficients by four-point interpolation of the original S3 expression,
checks the product-span rank 47 and the target's membership in that span,
and verifies the predicted quadratic-equation rank 46.

The kernel is **explicitly exhausted** at this degree. One trace relation
between the 47 top equations gives 48 variable multiples. The annihilator
`V^perp` has dimension 23 and supplies 23 `X1`-weighted and 23
`X2`-weighted syzygies of Lemma 2.3 in the theorem dossier. To construct
them, the script solves the linear map

    T(gamma)=sqrt(gamma*z^2)+gamma*z^2

for each coefficient of `lambda*X1` or `lambda*X2`. In the trace-dual
identity, the top equation indexed by `gamma` is
`Tr(T(gamma)*X1*s/z)`, where `s=X1+X2`. The weighted cubic tops reduce to
`Tr(lambda*X1^2*s/z)` and
`Tr(lambda*(X1^2*s+X1*s^2)/z)`, respectively; both have Boolean degree at
most two, so their cubic parts vanish. The script checks each resulting
vector against the complete 17,296-column cubic map and finds the 94
exhibited vectors independent. Since this equals the computed kernel
dimension, they span it. The exact input hash, ranks and control counts are
in `validation/n47-rand00-joint-top-syzygies-001.json`.

This closes the **linear-multiplier top syzygy classification for this one
input**. It does not classify degree-two or higher multipliers, the iterated
bounded closure, or the ideal's solution count. The next proof calculation
should lift these 94 syzygies through the lower-degree equation terms and
test whether their falls generate all low-degree closure mutations. The
uniform theorem above controls only the first joint layer; a dimension
match at degree one alone is not an LFD lower bound.

## Symmetric quadratic syzygies at the next layer

There is a universal, explicitly harmless subspace of the joint
degree-two-multiplier kernel. This reduces the part of that kernel that
needs to be classified for a degree-four closure argument.

Let `g_1,...,g_r` be Boolean equations of degree at most two whose
homogeneous quadratic top forms `q_1,...,q_r` are independent in the
square-zero associated graded algebra `A`. Write `g_i=q_i+ell_i` in the
Boolean ring, where `deg ell_i <= 1`. The map

    Phi_2 : A_2 tensor span(g_i) -> A_4,
    (h_i)_i |-> sum_i h_i*q_i

has a canonical `r*(r+1)/2`-dimensional subspace in its kernel. For each
`i`, use the coefficient vector with `q_i` in position `i`; for each
`i<j`, use `q_j` in position `i` and `q_i` in position `j`. These vectors
are independent because the `q_i` are independent. Their images vanish:
`q_i^2=0` in `A`, while `q_i*q_j+q_j*q_i=0` in characteristic two.

**Their lower-degree lifts add nothing beyond the degree-three Macaulay
space.** In the Boolean ring, where every element is idempotent,

    q_i*g_i = (g_i+ell_i)*g_i = g_i+ell_i*g_i,
    q_j*g_i+q_i*g_j = ell_j*g_i+ell_i*g_j.

Every right-hand side belongs to `M_3(g_1,...,g_r)`: it is an input
equation or a degree-at-most-one multiple of one. Thus the symmetric
subspace produces no new low-degree fall when degree-two multipliers are
admitted at cap four. This statement is parameter-uniform for Boolean
quadratic systems; it does **not** classify the rest of `ker Phi_2` or
make the degree-four closure exact.

For the named n47 Semaev input, the independently checked quadratic-top
rank is 46. Choosing 46 independent combinations gives a symmetric
subspace of dimension `46*47/2 = 1081` inside the quadratic-multiplier
kernel. The omitted 47th combination has zero quadratic top and is the
affine trace equation. Its 1128 independent squarefree quadratic
multipliers occupy the separate trace-equation coordinate, so the full
47-equation top map has kernel dimension **at least 2209** before other
syzygies are considered. Its multiples are lower-degree rows, not additional
members of the 1081-dimensional symmetric subspace. The next useful
rank calculation should quotient out these 1081 known relations before
seeking genuinely new quadratic syzygies and their degree-three lifts.

The source-only `--quadratic-generated` mode in
`joint_binary_top_syzygies.py` builds the variable multiples of the
already classified linear syzygies and the symmetric family in the
53,016-coordinate n47 quadratic-multiplier domain. It computes their
individual and combined ranks, checks the symmetric rank against
`r(r+1)/2+r*s`, and stops if the domain exceeds 60,000 coordinates.
The flag has not yet been used for a frontier run. Its named-input plan
and `experiment.protocol/v1` record are frozen in
`n47-rand00-quadratic-generated-plan-001.json` and
`n47-rand00-quadratic-generated-protocol-002.json` (which supersedes the
unrun V1 protocol with a pre-data linear-rank control); the cost admission
gate remains open. The existing degree-one output is unchanged when the
flag is absent.

The rank of the **linear-syzygy variable lifts** can in fact be obtained
without the generated-rank run. Let `k>=2`, let
`P=span_F2{v_i*v_j: 1<=i,j<=k}` in `K`, and put `delta=n-dim(P)`.
For the explicit `2n`-dimensional degree-one family constructed above,
the span of all its variable multiples in the degree-two-multiplier
domain has exact dimension

    binom(2k,2) + 4*k*(n-k) - 3*delta.                 (1)

This is a formula for the displayed family, not for all degree-two
syzygies. **Proof.** Modulo the trace-relation equation coordinate,
apply `T` to the multiplier coefficients. The two `V^perp` families
become the coefficient-valued linear forms `lambda*X` and `lambda*s`.
Their variable multiples split by monomial type into `aa`, `ac` and
`cc` blocks in the disjoint `(a,c)` coordinates. In the `aa` block, a
putative dependence is specified by `lambda_i in V^perp` and has
coefficient `lambda_i*v_j+lambda_j*v_i` on `a_i*a_j`. Because all
`v_i` are nonzero field elements and `k>=2`, these coefficients all
vanish exactly when `lambda_i=rho*v_i` for one `rho in K`. The condition
`lambda_i in V^perp` for every `i` is equivalent to
`Tr(rho*v_i*v_j)=0` for all `i,j`, or `rho in P^perp`. Thus the `aa`
kernel has dimension `delta`; the `cc` block is identical.

In the `ac` block, the two families contribute coefficients
`eta_j*v_i+mu_i*v_j` on `a_i*c_j`, with `eta_j,mu_i in V^perp`.
Vanishing for every `i,j` again forces
`eta_j=rho*v_j` and `mu_i=rho*v_i` for one `rho in P^perp`; its kernel
also has dimension `delta`. The nontrace families have domain
dimension `4k(n-k)` and therefore image dimension
`4k(n-k)-3delta`. The trace-relation family's variable multiples
span exactly the independent trace coordinate tensored with all
squarefree quadratics, of dimension `binom(2k,2)`. Adding this direct
coordinate proves (1). QED.

For the named n47 input, the independently checked product span has
dimension 47, so `delta=0`. Formula (1) gives **3336** exactly:
`binom(48,2)=1128` trace lifts plus `4*24*23=2208` annihilator lifts.
The generated-rank run now needs to determine only how the symmetric
family intersects this proved 3336-dimensional span; the full quadratic
kernel remains a separate calculation.
The symmetric family has rank 1127, of which the 46 trace-coordinate
cross terms already lie in the trace-lift span. Consequently the combined
generated rank is constrained to the exact interval **3336 through 4417**.

## A diagonal bound on the remaining overlap

For odd `n`, full product span `P=K`, and nonzero `z`, the overlap between
the 1081-dimensional independent-top symmetric family and the lifted
linear families has a sharper upper bound. Put `K_0=ker Tr` and let
`pi(t)=t+Tr(t)` be the projection from `K` to `K_0` along `F2*1`.
The trace pairing restricted to `K_0` is nondegenerate because
`Tr(1)=1`. A symmetric quadratic syzygy on the independent `K_0`
top-equation coordinates can be written uniquely as a self-adjoint
`F2`-linear map `L:K_0->K_0`; after applying `T` to its equation
coefficients, its bilinear polynomial is

    F_L(X,s) = L(pi(X*s/z)),       X,s in V.

If this syzygy is also a variable lift of the displayed annihilator
families, the absence of `aa` and `cc` terms and the injectivity proved
above force it to have the form `X*A(s)+s*B(X)`, for linear
`A,B:V->V^perp`. Since `F_L` is symmetric in `X,s`, the difference
under swapping them is
`X*(A+B)(s)+s*(A+B)(X)=0`. The same pairwise-ratio argument used in
(1) gives `(A+B)(v)=rho*v` with `rho in P^perp`. Full product span
forces `rho=0`, so `A=B`. Setting `s=X` now yields

    L(pi(X^2/z)) = X*A(X)+X*A(X) = 0.

Let `m=dim pi(V^[2]/z)`, where `V^[2]={v^2:v in V}`. Self-adjoint
operators on the `(n-1)`-dimensional nondegenerate trace space that kill
an `m`-dimensional subspace correspond to symmetric bilinear forms on
its quotient. Their dimension is `(n-1-m)*(n-m)/2`. This is an upper bound
on the overlap; additional product-factorization constraints may make
the actual overlap smaller.

For the named n47 input, `rank_projected_square_subspace.py` independently
checks the field inverse, trace projection, basis squares and exact rank
`m=24` from the frozen plan. Its machine-readable result is
`validation/n47-rand00-projected-square-rank-001.json`, including the
source and plan hashes. Thus the overlap has dimension **at most 253**,
and the combined generated rank lies in the narrower interval
**4164 through 4417**. This is a rigorous bound on a specified
top-syzygy subspace, not a claim about the full D4 kernel.

More generally, if an `m`-equation quadratic-top map has rank `r` and
kernel dimension `s=m-r`, its degree-two-multiplier kernel has a canonical
subspace of dimension

    s*binom(N,2) + r*(r+1)/2.

To see the dimension without double counting, choose a basis of the
equation space consisting of `r` independent quadratic tops and `s`
zero-top equations. The latter contribute the direct coordinate space
`ker(q) tensor A_2`, of dimension `s*binom(N,2)`. Project the symmetric
syzygies to the `r` independent equation coordinates; their images are
the independent diagonal and off-diagonal vectors above, of dimension
`r*(r+1)/2`. The coordinate spaces intersect trivially after this
projection. If each zero-top equation is actually linear, as the n47
trace relation is, its quadratic multiples already lie in the
degree-three closure. Thus all 2209 displayed n47 vectors have known
lower-degree lifts; an additional kernel vector is necessary for any
new degree-four mutation beyond those lifts.
