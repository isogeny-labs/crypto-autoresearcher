## 1. The actual polynomial/Boolean bridge

Let k be a field, P=k[b_1,...,b_N], J=(b_i^2-b_i), and B=P/J.
Supply **multilinear lifts** F as the input generators and include the explicit
bit equations in the ordinary polynomial system Ftilde. Use strict quotient
closure W_D: include eligible inputs; close under linear combinations and
b_i w when deg(w)+1<=D. Degree means squarefree degree. For D>=2,

    image(V_(Ftilde,D)) = W_D,
    V_(Ftilde,D) = {p in P_<=D : NF(p) in W_D}.

This is a written derivation, not yet a generic Lean theorem.

First, ordinary reduction by b_i^2-b_i never increases degree. For every p,

    p-NF(p) = sum_i q_i (b_i^2-b_i),
    deg(q_i (b_i^2-b_i)) <= deg(p).

Reduce one repeated variable at a time. A monomial c*m*b_i^a, a>=2,
contributes c*m*b_i^(a-2) to q_i and replaces its term by
c*m*b_i^(a-1). This proves the identity and degree cap by induction.
`normalization.py` constructs these exact cofactors; its separate expansion
checker verifies the formal-polynomial identity, not just evaluation equality.

For the first inclusion, take a degree-D polynomial derivation, project its
rows to B, and split every multiplier into monomials. Each monomial product
is simulated one variable at a time. In the polynomial domain, degrees of
nonzero factors add; NF never increases degree, so every intermediate
quotient multiplication fits the original cap. The zero case is immediate.
Thus the projection of every derivation is in W_D.

Conversely, lift a quotient derivation inductively. Linear combinations lift
because normal form is coefficient-linear. For a permitted variable product,
the multilinear lift w has degree <=D-1 and is available in V_D by induction.
Form b_i*w at cap D, then subtract its explicit degree-D field-equation repair.
This obtains the multilinear lift of the quotient product in V_D. Every
p of degree <=D with the same normal form differs by an element of
J intersect P_<=D, itself derivable from the bit equations at cap D.
This proves both displayed equalities. At D=0,1 the result follows directly
from the affine spans: no nonzero degree-two field equation is eligible.

**Raw input qualification.** If inputs are raw polynomials G, replacing them
by NF(G) preserves closure only at caps where each raw input and its repair
are available. A sufficient condition is D>=max(2,max deg(G)). This is not an
unconditional transfer of small exact LFD values between raw and normalized
presentations. F_p coefficients with Boolean variables remain F_p
coefficients; this proof does not describe GF(2) carry circuits.

The existing binary dossier Proposition 1.1 and prime digit frozen convention
already contain versions of this argument. This change makes the shared
hypotheses explicit and supplies bounded per-normalization repair witnesses.
It does not alter those frozen sources or settle their formalization gaps.

## 2. Two-sided exact closure certificates

The certificate represents a candidate space U by referenced primal rows.
Every row has a derivation from the **entire original generator list**, using
only input, scalar-addition and strict-budget variable multiplication.
Induction gives U subset W_D. A seed-kernel rule is forbidden.

A separate checker performs descending-degree RREF on U. Its rows with
pivot degree below D form a basis of U intersect B_<=D-1: linear combinations
cannot cancel distinct pivot columns without using zero coefficients. Testing
every variable product of this low-degree basis therefore tests the entire
required closure, not just products of low-degree literal inputs. Together
with inclusion of every eligible generator this proves W_D subset U.
Hence **both checks** prove U=W_D. The reverse inclusion was the missing
obligation in the 2026-10-19 dual-certificate dimension correction.

In the Boolean algebra, the ideal generated by F is the vanishing ideal of
its Boolean zero set Z. Indeed, Boolean assignment indicators partition 1;
on each assignment outside Z choose a nonzero input value to invert. This
constructs the indicator from an input times a scalar. Consequently its
codimension on B_<=D equals the rank of the evaluation matrix on Z.
If codim(W_D) is larger than this rank, a kernel vector of evaluation outside
W_D is an explicit missing ideal consequence. This is a stronger finite
criterion than comparing with |Z|. The checker returns such a polynomial.
No later-fall cutoff is inferred from a merely stabilized low-degree profile.

For UNSAT, an upper refutation at D plus a separator at D-1 gives exact LFD D.
For SAT, nonmembership of 1 says nothing; use the missing ideal consequence.
The finite checker is capped at N<=12 and p<=65537 and exhaustively enumerates
Boolean assignments. It is a correctness control, not a large-instance solver.

## 3. Lean work completed at its stated scope

`ClosureCertificates.lean` uses the existing ordinary polynomial definition
`V` and proves closed-space containment, exactness from two inclusions,
separator soundness, ideal membership of derivations, the full last-fall
bound from a refutation, exact last fall from adjacent certificates, and the
SAT missing-consequence lower bound. The exact-pair lemma derives the unit
ideal from the upper derivation; it does not assume one as a circular premise.

The same module proves the exact polynomial partition of assignment
indicators over any field and the final assembly of localized affine
contradictions. Its localization theorem **explicitly assumes** the localized
rows are derivable. It does not prove the support-aware construction, the
binary S3 descent, Boolean-normal-form semantics, or the 3*ell+1 theorem.
Those remain necessary to formalize the original binary upper bound.
The module is not a Lean verification of the Python parser or arithmetic.

## 4. Growing lower-bound work: the precise remaining task

For a named UNSAT input, certify 1 outside W_D; for SAT, construct a missing
ideal consequence using the evaluation-kernel criterion above. For a family
statement, construct these separators for **arbitrarily growing** parameters,
with their closure checked symbolically and a specified input distribution
or explicit deterministic family. A next finite degree does not prove this.

The candidate route is a family of linear maps pi_D with (a) every eligible
input in the kernel, (b) low-degree kernel closed under every variable, and
(c) either pi_D(1)!=0 with independently established UNSAT, or a specified
ideal polynomial g with pi_D(g)!=0. A plain-Macaulay annihilator does not
suffice: the mutant fixture in the tests has a low-degree combination of two
quadratic inputs whose variable product escapes their original span.
The previously proposed shortcut through a top-form or Hilbert-series count
therefore leaves condition (b) unproved. No parameter-uniform construction
of pi_D is established here. This is a proof gap, not evidence of impossibility.

Keep the existing prime digit law 4+floor(s/2) restricted to its measured
widths. Its missing instrument controls and a proof of later-fall completion
must be restored before extrapolation. Likewise, the ordinary membership
bound B+1<=d_F depends on the degree-B input; substituting B=2^ell does not
prove a lower bound for a fixed-degree coordinate encoding.
