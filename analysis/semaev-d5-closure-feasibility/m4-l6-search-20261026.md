# m=4, l=6 (m*l = 24): an unsatisfiable instance, and why its degree-4 closure did not finish here

Write-once record, 2026-10-26. Follows `crosscheck-hky-par-20261020.md` (unchanged). Observations are measured; derived numbers are labelled. Raw files and hashes: `raw-20261026/`. Observations only: no claim about the degree of any instance is made.

## Why n = 17
The m=4 cells use N = 2n + 4l variables. A degree-4 refutation needs an **unsatisfiable** system, and the exact ordered solution count of a draw is about 2^(m*l - n) (measured counts below). Unsatisfiable draws therefore thin out roughly by a factor of two per unit of m*l - n.
- At (n, l) = (13, 5), surplus 7: one unsatisfiable draw in 19 (the earlier record, draw 18).
- (n, l) = (13, 6), surplus 11: a first attempt (`hky_par newcells --cells 4:13:6`) ran 31 CPU-minutes in the single-threaded draw phase with no result and was stopped. Not a measurement of anything but cost.
- Measured exact (ordered) solution counts, seed 20261016: (16, 6): 276, 240, 216 at 92 s per count; (15, 6): 288, 1290, 180 at 47 s per count. Unsatisfiable draws there are not findable (derived: about 1 in 20,000 at n = 16).

## Search method (scratch tool, not pushed to crypto)
`hky_sys findunsat` (diff in `raw-20261026/hky_sys-vs-hky_par.diff`, 119 lines against `examples/hky_par.rs` at crypto 981f80710b7e) replays the `newcells` draw stream (`cell_seed`, `ladder_sample`) and decides each draw with an **early-exit** version of `chained_solution_count`'s search, which returns at the first solution. Draws are split over workers by `draw % workers`; each worker replays the same generator stream.
- Validation against the exact counter: at (13, 5) draws 0-18 all agree, and draw 18 is the unsatisfiable one found by the earlier run. At (16, 6) draws 0-2 and (17, 6) draw 0 the early-exit result is "has solution" with exact counts 276 / 240 / 216 / 48. Early-exit cost: median < 0.05 s at (13, 5); 1.5-4.1 s at (16, 6); 5 s at (17, 6) versus 92-190 s for the exact count.
- Four workers, `--cell 4:17:6 --seed 20261016`: worker 1 found **draw 57** with no solution, after 198 s, and the exact counter then returned **0 solutions** for it. Draw 57 is not shown to be the lowest-index unsatisfiable draw (the other workers were stopped at their own draws).

## Derived: what the closure needs (extrapolation; 2.5x more columns than the largest completed cell)
- Columns for N = 58 at degree 4: 456,838 (exact). Row-triangular storage at full rank: columns^2 / 16 bytes = 13.0 GB. The one completed degree-4 m=4 run (N = 46, 179,447 columns) peaked at 3.26 GB = 1.62 x its triangular figure (2.0 GB). Applying that factor gives about 21 GB; the factor at this size is unknown.
- Time: 613 s at N = 46 on 4 threads. Scaling by (columns ratio)^2 to ^3 gives 66 min to 2.8 h. Not validated.
- Planning figure for a host: 32 GB RAM, 4+ cores. An unvalidated choice, not a bound.
- Why no smaller cell works: memory limits N to about 54 (n = 15) here, where the unsatisfiable-draw rate is about e^-21 per draw by the solution-count estimate; n = 17 gives a findable rate but N = 58. This is the two-sided constraint on an m = 4, l = 6 cell in this container.
