# Linearization excess and elite binary factor bases: a transfer check

**Preparatory source assessment, 26 October 2026.** Question: can a target-blind choice of a binary factor-base subspace lower the *charged* point-decomposition cost in the chained three- and four-summand systems used by the current descent studies? This report reviews existing measurements and a published product-space theorem. It contains no new solver run, protocol approval, factor-base ranking, or end-to-end speed claim.

## Sources and scope

- **Retrieved primary literature:** [Bachoc–Serra–Zémor, *An analogue of Vosper's theorem for extension fields*](https://arxiv.org/abs/1501.00602), Theorem 3, and [Faugère–Perret–Petit–Renault, *Improving the Complexity of Index Calculus Algorithms in Elliptic Curves over Binary Fields*](https://people.maths.ox.ac.uk/petit/files/Eurocrypt2012.pdf), Sections 1–2. The former classifies minimum product spans in prime-degree finite-field extensions when both subspaces have dimension at least two and the product span has dimension at most `n-2`: they have geometric-progression bases. It controls product-space dimension under those hypotheses, not Gröbner cost, relation rank, or a composite-degree field. The latter already couples the binary factor base `x(P)∈V` to a structured polynomial-system solver; its complexity claim depends on a stated linear-independence heuristic. Compact product-space design is an established mechanism, not a novelty claim here.
- **Internal PDP measurement:** [cryptanalysis `pdp-degree-heuristics/README.md`](https://github.com/aburan28/cryptanalysis/blob/e7cf096b4e2f9020e1326e4ed0b5ac0e96cc99f9/experiments/pdp-degree-heuristics/README.md), commit `e7cf096b4e2f9020e1326e4ed0b5ac0e96cc99f9`, Git blob `2d4852c9fc078ad3569d6304f3254020ebb2019c`; its [generated paired table](https://github.com/aburan28/cryptanalysis/blob/e7cf096b4e2f9020e1326e4ed0b5ac0e96cc99f9/experiments/pdp-degree-heuristics/REPORT.md), blob `ee932334e025cf891fdb7152d2e15fdbf4db39e7`, and [table generator](https://github.com/aburan28/cryptanalysis/blob/e7cf096b4e2f9020e1326e4ed0b5ac0e96cc99f9/experiments/pdp-degree-heuristics/heuristics.py), blob `1cf19f0e27586c1119f3786e621cf9d9f8b2a023`. The study uses `y²+xy=x³+1` over binary fields, exact matched target workloads, and enumerated subspace bases. Its experimental identities and subgroup orders must be taken from each raw row before a new matched run.
- **Internal chained-system measurement:** [crypto closure-scale results](https://github.com/aburan28/crypto/blob/d51b908a77574e8d7ac7aa2cb3332961578cade8/research/dreg_closure_scale_20261016/RESULTS.md), commit `d51b908a77574e8d7ac7aa2cb3332961578cade8`, Git blob `b473577e9c66b595ce654779b9ce862d2c51b600`, and the [specialisation preregistration](https://github.com/aburan28/crypto/blob/d51b908a77574e8d7ac7aa2cb3332961578cade8/research/dreg_closure_specialise_20261017/PREREGISTRATION.md), blob `60d94eac8d3c90f6e3ae886d0692a8c061ee7c85`. The generator uses randomly sampled `ℓ`-dimensional subspaces; the frozen draws retain each basis and target coordinate. The specialisation result's null control failed, so it cannot decide whether maximum summand dimension or total summand dimension drives the closure rise.
- **Internal conservation control:** [KN-FIND-007](../../../knowledge/findings/KN-FIND-007.md) proves equal-size bases have the same mean multiset decomposition count over a finite group. It does not equate actual coverage, rank, solver work, or bases with different realized usable-point counts.

This source search covers those two primary papers and three internal study lines. It does not claim an exhaustive literature review; a frozen protocol must still inspect the exact system-builder version and all cited raw rows it executes.
The chained closure study is an unsatisfiable-system diagnostic on the Koblitz-form equation `y²+xy=x³+1` over the stated binary fields, not a subgroup DLP run. It has no single prime-subgroup target identity; the exact field polynomial, curve UID and subgroup order must be bound for a later ECDLP comparison rather than inferred from `n` alone.

## Observations and the missing bridge

| Source-reported object | What is established at its tested scale | What remains open |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Two-summand PDP over prime-degree binary fields | For `e = n - ℓ - dim(V²)`, the fitted **solving degree** is `2 + [e < -3.5] + [e < -8.5]` over 656 bases; mean absolute error 0.06 degrees. The homogeneous **degree of regularity** is `ℓ+1` for that formulation. | The fit is not a law for chained `m=3,4`, and a lower input degree or smaller `dim(V²)` alone is not a whole-problem gain. |
| Symmetric three-summand PDP in the same study | Its input dimension is `sum_{k=1}^3 dim(V^k)`. The source's paired MXL table reports random/structured geometric-mean operation ratios of `11.7` per attempted query and `6.6` per derived relation, with mean solving-degree difference `+0.24` across 18 eligible `(n,ℓ)` cells. Its direct-formulation table reports `1.46`, `1.01`, and `+0.25` across a different set of 31 cells. The structured pool is prefix plus geometric bases. | These are source-reported PDP-stage observations on smaller fields, not a matched comparison of the two encodings on an in-range ECDLP run. Some relation ratios exclude zero-yield cells. Construction, rank, descent and full-cost comparison are absent. |
| Chained S3 closure study in crypto | With random subspaces, degree-4 closure refuted all four checked unsatisfiable draws in each of `(n,ℓ)=(16,6),(18,6)`, and none of four in each of `(19,7),(21,7)`. All 40 source draws saturated at degree 4; one recorded satisfiable control was not refuted. See the [source-reported counts in the figure](figure.svg) and the source's `reports/run-20261016T1705Z/`. | `n` and `ℓ` both change across these cells. No top-`e` versus median-`e` paired arm was measured. The prime-extension theorem applies to `n=19`, not automatically to `n=21`. |

The first two rows concern PDP systems; the third rebuilds a chained `m=3,4` system from each saved `v_basis` and `x_R`. The present [lead](../../../knowledge/leads/ecdlp/ic-engine/LEAD-ICE-linearization-excess-fb-prior.yaml) proposes selecting bases by `e`, but its transfer from the two-summand fit to the chained solver remains an experimental question. The existing random-subspace closure cells offer a reference workload, not a selected elite base. The symmetric `m=3` source table makes **formulation a predeclared variable** in the next comparison: measure direct, symmetric and the current chained route on the same bases and targets rather than transferring a ratio between their different historical cell sets.

## Discriminating boundary for Coordinator design

Before a new measurement, bind one exact curve/subgroup, field representation, existing chained-system builder, frozen public targets and solver binary. A target-blind candidate scan can record `ℓ`, `dim(V²)`, higher product dimensions, `e`, point-lift and subgroup-projection counts, realized usable points, factor-log columns and construction cost for every attempted base. Predeclare selection from that scan; compare top-`e`, median-`e` and an independent random control at the same `(curve,m,ℓ)` with held-out matched targets. Match or stratify by **realized usable-point count and cofactor class** before attributing a relation effect to `e`; equal `ℓ` alone does not make those quantities equal. Preserve duplicate and failed candidates.

Measure each compatible formulation's input monomials and density, first-fall and solving degrees, closure work, splitting checks, wall/CPU/RSS, censored calls and independently checked planted/negative controls. Report source-stage cost per ordinary query separately from construction, actual subgroup relation yield, novel independent rank, linear algebra and target work. If the candidate wins only at the PDP stage, record exactly that. A later fully charged `T=1` and declared `T>1` comparison against the same-curve IC baseline and matched rho is required for an end-to-end claim.
