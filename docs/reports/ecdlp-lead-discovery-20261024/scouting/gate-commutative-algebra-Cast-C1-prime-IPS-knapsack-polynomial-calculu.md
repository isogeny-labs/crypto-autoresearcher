### What C1 does that the forecloses phrases do not cover (the strict test)

`LEAD-ICP-digit-presentation-solving-degree` forecloses: "base-B digit presentation as Weil descent over a prime field"; "bounded last fall degree for prime-field Semaev systems"; "first fall degree of the digit-presented decomposition system"; "Groebner staircase or semigroup presentation of the decomposition ring"; "carry-circuit encoding of prime-field decomposition"; "Yokoyama semi-normality object". C1's invariant is the *polynomial-calculus refutation degree* (a proof-system degree of the system, quantified over solvers), obtained analytically by block restriction. That is not first fall degree, not last fall degree, not a staircase and not a carry circuit, so none of the phrases names the *method*. It does fall inside the first, second and third phrases' *topic*: the conclusion (degree grows with `s`; no `p`-independent bound for PC-simulable solvers) is the contrapositive-side of "bounded last fall degree for prime-field Semaev systems". Hence same lead, sharper step: `advances_lead`.

`LEAD-ICE-prime-field-semaev-solving-degree` forecloses "prime-field Semaev Groebner solving degree law", "semi-regular null for prime-field decomposition", etc. C1 is not a measured law on an interval/random x-coordinate base; it is a lower-bound theorem for the digit-presented system. The closed lead's obstruction is not answered and is not contested; C1 strengthens it in one sub-class.

No standing obstruction is escaped by C1 (see N4): SO-ALGFB conclusion binds, SO-TABLE and SO-GEN are untouched because C1 is non-enumerative and non-generic but yields no oracle.

Pair: **(R1: the abscissa `x_i` written in `s` Boolean digits, `x_i = sum_j 2^j b_ij`, `b_ij^2 = b_ij`; Sigma = translation by factor-base elements, entering only through `S_{m+1}(x_1..x_m, x_R) = 0` (direct) or an `S_3` chain with `F_p` internal nodes (chained))**. Note the ledger's own IDEA-20260806-e4f96e places the *PC degree invariant* in Class III (coordinate-dependent: a function of the presentation, "does NOT propagate under a change of encoding"); both labels are consistent (Class I names the attack mechanism, Class III names the invariant), but the record should say which one it means.

Is it a recorded rigidity result in new notation? No, for four reasons.

- IDEA-20260806-c5d183 (checked from its index claim and `docs/object-frame-ideation.md` Sec. 1): a *lossy projection of a group element* that propagates deterministically under the *full translation action* of a prime-order group is constant or injective. C1 tracks no projection of group elements and makes no propagation claim; Sigma is the factor-base sub-action, which is the escape the document names (Class I). C1 is a degree lower bound on a polynomial system, not a statement about what a projection can preserve.
- IDEA-20260901-863e36 (block systems of `<Sigma>`) and KN-FIND-ffe1df Theorem C (an exactly sum-compatible bucket index is a surjective homomorphism onto a group of order dividing `N`) classify *labels on group elements*. The digit map is a representation of the *coordinate* `x`, not a bucket index of `P`; it is lossless (a bijection `[0,2^s) <-> {0,1}^s`), so the "projection that loses nothing is a change of coordinates" clause applies: C1 changes the presentation of the same variety and asks what a proof system pays for it. That is why the PC degree is encoding-sensitive and why C1 is *not* a rigidity statement.
- KN-OPEN-020 (read in full): the universal no-go for algebraically described factor bases is open; the open class is high-degree, implicit-membership or target-dependent descriptions; "Required next result: ... a formal complexity class for algebraic descriptions plus a lower bound for its implicit membership/source solver". The digit description is the implicit class (quadratic auxiliary constraints, degree-`B` membership polynomial after projection, so SO-ALGFB's premise 'target-independent algebraic locus of degree d' is not met, but its conclusion 'mean yield `binom(B+m-1,m)/N`, conserved' still binds). C1 would supply a lower bound for the implicit-membership solver in one sub-class (digit/box) and one model (PC-simulable solvers, unsat draws). That is a partial answer to the "required next result" of KN-OPEN-020, not a restatement of a recorded rigidity result and not an escape of it. The factor-base-escape clause of the constraint block ("say which KN-OPEN-020 open class it belongs to ... or declare itself inside the scoped-out class and stop") is satisfied by naming the implicit class and stating that no oracle is produced.
- Notation test: the same statement in the program's older vocabulary is "solver-independent degree floor for the digit-presented Semaev system", i.e. the Outcome 1 branch of H-PCD-03be5a specialised to E3-like encodings plus the digit lane's H-WIL floor. So C1 *is* a recorded program in older notation at the level of its object (PC degree of a Semaev presentation) and is new only in its method (block restriction + IPS) and in the sub-class it targets.

## Heuristics (numbered, conditional; none is a result)

- **H1.** IPS Lemma 4.2 holds over `F_{p^d}` for `p > k+1` (inclusion matrix full row rank mod `p` by Wilson/Ghorbani Cor. 2) and with coefficients `c_i = +-2^j` invertible. Supported by retrieved secondary statements; the exact Wilson formula is recalled. This is the candidate's own step beyond IPS99 (which is over the reals). FSTW16 Cor. 5.4 is a characteristic-`p` floor in a weaker proof system (static degree of the function `1/(sum x_i - beta)`, unit coefficients), so it narrows but does not remove what is new: the characteristic-`p` PC version of Lemma 4.2 is not covered by anything I retrieved.
- **H2.** Block restriction preserves the direction of the bound: a PC refutation of an unsatisfiable system restricts to one of no larger degree on the restricted system, and a restriction of an unsatisfiable system is unsatisfiable. (My own check of the direction; the candidate's L1-A.) What I did not check: that the restricted axiom `P*(L - r_1)` meets the hypothesis of Lemma 4.2 (`deg ML(P*) < ceil(s/2)`), which, on my reading of the lemma, needs roughly `s > 2^m - 2` when `deg P* = 2^{m-1} - 1` (the induction in IPS99 may tolerate more, so this may be too conservative); the candidate says the `s < 2^{m-1}` corner is handled by its A1 (leading coefficient `+-1`) and a computed table. That corner is exactly where a defect would hide, On that reading the planned toy ladder (`m = 2, 3`, `s <= 8`) splits as follows: for `m = 2` the hypothesis needs `s >= 3`, so `s = 3..8` tests the claimed regime and `s = 2` is a corner cell; for `m = 3` it needs `s >= 7`, so only `s = 7, 8` test the claimed regime and `s <= 6` are corner cells. Those cells (`m = 2, s = 3..8`; `m = 3, s = 7..8`) are the only digit-system cells that test the claimed regime; the checker should report them separately from corner cells. The P-256 `m = 4` target (`s = 65`) is far outside the corner.
- **H3.** PC refutations exist only for unsatisfiable systems. The operational question (decomposable targets, satisfiable systems) is covered only by the candidate's L1-D `log2 t` loss, which I did not examine. A floor on refutation degree is not a floor on the cost of finding a solution of a satisfiable system.
- **H4.** Arithmetic recomputation of the candidate's P-256 `m = 4` prediction (a prediction, not an outcome): with `s = 65`, `n = m s = 260`, `D = ceil(65/2)+1 = 34`, the number of multilinear monomials of degree at most 34 in 260 variables is `2^141.9` (`s = 66` gives `2^142.7`). The `2^62.9` per-attempt budget and the `+79.1`-bit margin were not re-derived.
- **H5.** Conditional implication, not claimed by the candidate: because the bound depends on the digit blocks and on `P` having an extension-field root and no root in the box, not on the curve, any curve model, special `j` or isogenous presentation that keeps the digit presentation inherits the floor (relevance to `LEAD-STR-special-j-ic-semaev`, `LEAD-ICE-curve-presentation-invariance`). Conditional on H1-H3.

## What is new, what is not
