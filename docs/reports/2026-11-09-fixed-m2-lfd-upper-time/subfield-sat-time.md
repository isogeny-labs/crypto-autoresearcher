# Full LFD and exact enumeration for quadratic-extension subfield bases

Date: 2026-11-09. Derived proof, awaiting independent review. This note extends Lemma A of `algebraic-route.md` from refutations to satisfiable ideals. No experiments, compilation, or solver execution occurred.

## Statement

Let H=F_(2^k), K=F_(2^(2k)), V=H, and z in K\H. Let F be the full Boolean coordinate descent of S3(x,y,z)=0 with x,y in H, and include Boolean field equations. Then:

1. The system has at most **four ordered Boolean solutions** (x,y).
2. Its last fall degree, under the dossier's definition with Boolean field equations, is **at most 3**, whether satisfiable or unsatisfiable.
3. Given valid field arithmetic and a promised subfield basis, all solutions can be enumerated deterministically using O(k^3) elementary bit operations with ordinary dense linear algebra and schoolbook field arithmetic, plus O(k) output bits. This complexity excludes certifying arbitrary external representation promises; details appear below.

No lower bound or unboundedness conclusion follows from this statement. It excludes this full-subfield family from an unbounded fixed-m=2 construction, including its satisfiable members.

## 1. At most two affine fibres, each of dimension at most one

Write z²=pz+q, p,q in H nonzero, and a6=A+Bz. As in the companion note, use s=x+y and obtain the equivalent H-valued equations

E1=x²+s x+p s²+B=0,
L(s)=p²s⁴+q s²+A+B²=0.

Every coordinate of L belongs to span F: L=E0+E1² and coefficientwise Frobenius of an H-valued Boolean polynomial is an F2-linear operation on its coordinate polynomials. L is affine-linear in the s coordinates. Its homogeneous kernel is {0,sqrt(q)/p}, so either L is inconsistent or its solutions have the form

s=s0+c h, c in F2, h=sqrt(q)/p nonzero.

The inconsistent case is already refuted by a linear combination of original equations. Otherwise eliminate the k-1 affine constraints on s, without increasing the derivation-degree budget beyond 2. The remaining system is quadratic in x and c, and each restriction c=b is precisely the affine F2-linear system

x²+s_b x=p s_b²+B.

The kernel of x↦x²+s_b x is {0} when s_b=0 and {0,s_b} otherwise. Hence each consistent fibre is an affine F2-subspace of dimension zero or one. There are two possible fibres, and therefore at most four ordered solutions. The map (x,c)↦(x,y=x+s_c) is injective on them.

This statement is about zeros of the descended summation polynomial, not a claim that every zero is an admissible nondegenerate elliptic-curve relation under a separate relation filter.

## 2. Conditional affine consequences are in W3

Let G be the system after eliminating the known affine s constraints. Its generators have degree at most two. Put e_b=c+b+1; then e_b g=e_b(g|_(c=b)) in the Boolean ring. Thus every coordinate of e_b g|_b lies in W3(G): it is a generator multiplied by a linear selector.

If the b-fibre is nonempty, every affine-linear polynomial ell that vanishes on it is a constant linear combination of its affine-linear equations. Therefore e_b ell lies in W3(G).

If the b-fibre is empty, its affine system is inconsistent, so its equations linearly combine to 1, and e_b belongs to W3(G).

These statements retain the complete original equations. No equation is discarded to infer a lower bound.

## 3. All affine-hull constraints are available in W3

Let S be the full solution set of G and suppose S is nonempty. For any affine-linear polynomial ell vanishing on S, its restriction ell|_b vanishes on each nonempty fibre. The preceding section therefore supplies e_b ell|_b in W3; an empty fibre supplies e_b, and multiplying by ell|_b remains within W3 because the polynomial degree is at most two. Consequently

ell=e_0 ell|_0+e_1 ell|_1 belongs to W3(G).

Hence all equations cutting out the affine hull A=aff(S) are in W3(G). If both fibres are nonempty, their dimensions are at most one and their selector values differ, so

dim A <= 1+1+1=3.

If only one fibre is nonempty, its affine hull is that fibre and has dimension at most one.

Choose an invertible affine Boolean coordinate change making A a coordinate affine subspace. Such a change preserves degree and W_D. Write the eliminated affine-hull coordinates as u=0, and the surviving coordinates as v. Every u_i is in W3. For D>=3, every polynomial of degree at most D divisible by u_i can be derived within W_D by multiplying the available degree-one u_i one variable at a time. Thus replacing any degree-at-most-D polynomial by its restriction to A changes it by an element of W_D.

## 4. All vanishing polynomials on A are generated by degree-three derivations

If both fibres are nonempty, choose the selector c as one of the affine coordinates on A. There are at most two further coordinates t because dim A<=3. On each c=b slice, the actual solution set remains an affine subspace, defined by affine-linear equations in t.

Let h(c,t) be any multilinear polynomial on A vanishing on S. It has degree at most three simply because A has at most three Boolean coordinates. Each h_b(t)=h(b,t) has degree at most two and vanishes on an affine subspace. After an affine coordinate change in the at-most-two t variables, that affine subspace is defined by coordinates t_i=0. Thus h_b is a sum of products of its affine-linear defining equations with multipliers of degree at most one. Reversing the affine coordinate change preserves this degree bound.

Section 2 supplies each gated affine defining equation e_b ell in W3. Multiplying by a degree-at-most-one multiplier stays within degree three. Therefore e_b h_b lies in W3 and

h=e_0h_0+e_1h_1 belongs to W3.

When only one fibre is nonempty, the entire solution set is affine and all its affine generators are already in W3; the same conclusion is easier. The empty set was treated by the degree-three refutation argument.

## 5. From this to the LFD bound

Let I be the polynomial vanishing ideal of the Boolean solution set, including field equations. We have derived all affine-hull constraints in W3. After eliminating them, at most three Boolean variables remain and every multilinear polynomial in their vanishing ideal is in W3 by Section 4.

For any f in I, set D=max(3,deg f). Boolean reduction differs from f by field-equation multiples of degree at most D. Elimination of the affine-hull coordinates differs by affine-hull-constraint multiples of degree at most D. The resulting multilinear polynomial has at most three variables, vanishes on S, and belongs to W3, hence W_D. Both differences also belong to the corresponding polynomial closure V_(F,D). Thus f belongs to V_(F,max(3,deg f)) for every ideal element f.

This proves d_LFD<=3 under exactly the dossier's LFD definition. The argument does not merely bound a particular solving strategy and does not confuse the number of solutions with LFD.

## 6. Exact algorithm and bit-operation accounting

### Representation promises

Assume the input supplies:

- a valid degree-2k binary field representation K, supporting schoolbook multiplication/reduction in O(k²) bit operations;
- an F2 basis h_1,...,h_k of the actual subfield H, with that subfield property promised;
- z outside H and a6 in K.

Alternatively K may be supplied directly as a quadratic extension of an explicitly represented H, which simplifies the basis conversion. Input validation such as proving irreducibility of an arbitrary supplied modulus or proving that an arbitrary proposed V is a subfield is separate from the following solver bound.

### Steps

1. Compute z'=z^(2^k) by k squarings. Set p=z+z' and q=zz'. Cost O(k³) bits under the schoolbook bound. The subfield and nonzero properties follow from the promised representation and z outside H.
2. Form the 2k-by-2k binary matrix with columns h_i and z h_i. It is invertible because z is outside H. Forming its columns costs O(k³); Gaussian elimination costs O(k³) bits. Use it to recover A,B from a6 and to convert H elements to coordinates.
3. Form the k-by-k affine system for L(s)=0. Its columns are p²h_i⁴+qh_i², converted to H coordinates. There are O(k) field operations, each O(k²), and O(k³) dense linear algebra. Reject if inconsistent; otherwise produce the particular s0 and the one nonzero kernel vector h. No enumeration of 2^k elements is needed.
4. For each of at most two s-values, form and solve the binary linear system x²+s x=p s²+B. Construct its matrix from h_i²+s h_i. Again O(k³) total per branch. Its nullity is at most one, so enumerate zero, one or two x-values, then output (x,x+s).

All loop counts are bounded by a constant number of k-by-k or 2k-by-2k linear systems and O(k) field operations. Total solver cost is O(k³) elementary bit operations and O(k²) working bits with naive linear algebra, excluding the separately identified representation-validation promises. Output is at most four pairs of 2k-bit ambient-field elements, hence O(k) bits.

If the basis is *not* promised to be a subfield, the above O(k³) statement must not silently include its validation. One explicit conservative validation route builds the 2k-by-2k Frobenius matrix, raises it to the k-th power using binary matrix exponentiation, and verifies all supplied basis vectors are fixed and independent. This takes O(k³ log k) bit operations using naive matrix multiplication, in addition to validating the ambient field representation. The fixed space has dimension k in a valid F_(2^(2k)), so k independent fixed vectors certify the exact desired subfield. Faster representation-specific validation may exist, but is not asserted here.

## 7. Boundaries and next review

The theorem covers the full subfield V=H in a quadratic extension and targets z outside H. It does not cover arbitrary k-dimensional subspaces of K, or near-subfield subspaces after perturbing even a few basis vectors. Those perturbations may be a useful separate question: an upper bound in the number of perturbations would further delimit potential hard families, but no such bound is proved in this note.

Independent review should check the exact closure lifting in Sections 2–5 and the representation promises in the operation count. No claim is made that the displayed O(k³) algorithm has been implemented, benchmarked, or integrated into the dataset generator.

## 8. Independently checked shorter Gröbner-basis proof

The parallel audit route gives a shorter replacement for Sections 3–5. After the affine s-elimination, every quadratic vanisher f belongs to W3: on each nonempty affine fibre, f_b is a combination of affine defining equations with cofactors of degree at most one; multiply by e_b and use the degree-three gated derivations from Section 2. On an empty fibre, e_b is available and e_b f_b has degree at most three. Summing the two gates gives f.

For a nonempty Boolean zero set S with |S|<=4, any reduced Gröbner basis under a degree-compatible order has degree at most two. Indeed, the ideal contains every x_i²+x_i, so any minimal initial-ideal generator of degree at least three must be squarefree. A squarefree minimal generator of degree three has seven proper divisors, all standard monomials. A generator of even larger degree has still more. Either case contradicts the fact that the number of standard monomials is dim(R/I(S))=|S|<=4. Thus the minimal initial generators have degree at most two; the degree-compatible reduced basis polynomials also have degree at most two.

All these basis polynomials are quadratic vanishers and hence lie in W3. A degree-compatible standard representation of any ideal element f then uses products of total degree at most deg f, so it derives within max(3,deg f). The empty-set case uses the existing degree-three refutation. This proves the same d_LFD<=3 conclusion. The affine constraints eliminated at the start lift without increasing this bound as detailed above.

This shorter argument was independently scrutinized in the algebraic route after being suggested by the parallel audit route; no new computational check was used.
