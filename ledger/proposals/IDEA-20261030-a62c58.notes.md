## Nilpotent interval membership and rational ordinates

For k>=1 work in Q=Fp[X]/(U). Introduce k field coefficients for each e_j, j=0,...,s-1, and impose

    e_j^2=e_j,
    n=X-t-sum_j 2^j e_j,
    n^k=0.

Soundness: evaluate at any geometric root alpha of U. Idempotence forces each e_j(alpha) to0 or1; nilpotence forces alpha=t+sum2^j e_j(alpha) in Fp. The B_len<p condition gives a unique integer bit label even if I_t wraps modulo p. Therefore U splits over Fp and all its roots lie in I_t. Completeness: factor U=product_a(X-a)^m_a with all a in I_t. Standard CRT decomposes Q into local factors Fp[X]/((X-a)^m_a). Choose the jth bit of the unique label a-t mod p in each factor. The CRT element is idempotent; n restricts to X-a and n^k vanishes because m_a<=k. In each nonzero local factor the only idempotents are0 and1, so the e_j are unique for fixed U. This is a proposed algebraic uniqueness statement, not a claim of efficient SAT propagation. It also covers s=0,B_len=1 with no e_j and n=X-t.

Interval coordinates alone do not imply rational points. Impose T^k=0 in Q, where T=f*(f^((p-1)/2)-1). At a rational root alpha this vanishes exactly when f(alpha)=0 or f(alpha) is a nonzero square.
