## Candidate structural change

For nonsingular short Weierstrass E/F_p, p>3, write f=X^3+aX+b, and let M define the desired coordinate membership. First obtain S=gcd(M,X^p-X), then H=gcd(S,f)*gcd(S,f^((p-1)/2)-1), all monic. The two factors are coprime. H is squarefree and split, and its roots are precisely rational member coordinates with at least one F_p point. Modular exponentiation in the relevant quotient avoids constructing degree-p polynomials explicitly. Its actual degree-dependent preprocessing cost must be charged. This prevents a hidden error: rational x alone does not ensure rational y when A and B have a common root.

For five affine summands and finite target R=(r,s), consider h=A(X)+B(X)y with A monic degree3, deg B<=1 and h(-R)=A(r)-sB(r)=0. Set N=A^2-fB^2=(X-r)U, with U monic degree5. Replace the distinct-root requirement U|M by U|H^5. The candidate condition allows repeated member coordinates, while still confining every residual norm root to the rational factor-base support. Multiplicity can be at most5 in U. This changes the modeled domain; it must not be silently compared to the earlier distinct-x experiment.

Do not recover solely with y=-A/B. Instead recover the zero divisor of h, including multiplicities at both signs over each root of N, and subtract exactly one copy of the known point -R. For y!=0 use x-x0 as local parameter and Hasse/formal-series coefficients of y; for y=0 use y as local parameter and solve x locally using f'(x0)!=0. Truncation through pole order6 bounds the work per root. Use Hasse jets rather than factorial-normalized ordinary derivatives, including p=5. This is intended to cover B(x0)=0, B identically0, tangent/repeated points, inverse pairs, two-torsion and target-coordinate overlap without enumerating a separate exceptional point-tuple chart. Verify every reconstructed multiset by exact group addition.

Proposed completeness argument to audit: sum(P_i)=R makes sum[P_i]+[-R]-6[O] principal. Its function has exact pole6 because all six support occurrences are affine, even when repeated. A basis of L(6O) is 1,x,y,x^2,xy,x^3; the pole6 coefficient can therefore be normalized to1. Norm multiplicities and local recovery must be proved carefully, especially at ramified two-torsion fibers. Conversely a recovered rational effective degree5 divisor after subtracting -R gives the desired sum by the principal-divisor group-law criterion. This is a reparameterization of relations, not scalar recovery.

Identity summands require a finite union of lower-pole strata: for k finite summands and finite nonzero R use exact pole k+1 and L((k+1)O); k=0 is impossible, and k=1..5 are the relevant strata. For R=O use pole k, with the all-identity case explicit and no nonconstant pole1 chart. Count identity multiplicities and permutations according to a declared unordered-multiset or ordered-tuple projection. Do not claim full chart coverage until all these branches have independently checked soundness/completeness and accounting.

## Explicit lower-stratum normalization and compact support check

Further unreviewed derivation, 2026-10-30. Let k be the number of affine summands. For finite R use d=k+1 and impose h(-R)=0; for R=O use d=k and impose no finite target zero. For d>=2, normalize the unique highest-pole basis coefficient: if d is even, A is monic of degree d/2 and 2 deg B+3<d; if d is odd, B is monic of degree (d-3)/2 and 2 deg A<d. Let c=1 in the even case and c=-1 in the odd case. Then c*(A^2-f B^2) is monic of degree d. For finite R define U=c*N/(X-r), degree k; for identity target define U=c*N, degree k. The proposed support condition is U | H^k. Recover the signed zero divisor before subtracting the finite target zero, and then pad with exactly5-k identity summands. k=0 has only the all-identity solution when R=O; d=1 contributes no chart. This supplies candidate equations for every stratum rather than appealing to an unspecified exceptional fallback.

The support test need not materialize H^k or a large quotient: reduce H modulo the degree-k monic U, then compute its kth power modulo U and require zero. This is equivalent to U dividing H^k over F_p, including repeated roots. It still charges construction/evaluation of H and all polynomial remainders, and gives no evidence that partial coefficient assignments make the test inexpensive. A SAT encoding may introduce remainder variables; those variables must be certified and projected away with identical complete point-model sets. No low-degree claim follows from the small degree in X: total degree in coefficient-bit variables and elimination work are different quantities.


## Dense-H-free quotient-algebra formulation (2026-10-30 15:10 UTC)

New unreviewed structural derivation. The dense H preprocessing may be avoidable, not merely chargeable. For each k>=1 and monic degree-k U over F_p, set F=X^3+aX+b, L=X^p-X and T=F*(F^((p-1)/2)-1). Proposed equivalent support test:

    M^k = 0 mod U,
    L^k = 0 mod U,
    T^k = 0 mod U.

Proof obligation, with direct derivation: U | L^k implies that every irreducible factor of U is linear over F_p, since L is squarefree and splits exactly over F_p. Each root alpha of U is a root of M and T by the other two divisibilities. T(alpha)=0 iff F(alpha)=0 or F(alpha) is a nonzero quadratic residue, by Euler's criterion. Thus precisely the rational curve-member support used to define H is obtained. Conversely, when every root of U lies in that support, its multiplicity is at most deg(U)=k; every one of M,L,T vanishes at each root, so its kth power is divisible by U. This proves the algebraic equivalence on full field assignments if the derivation is correct; it is still unreviewed and says nothing about partial-assignment propagation. M must be nonzero; an empty support yields no positive-k solution.

All three residues can be computed directly in the quotient algebra F_p[X]/(U). Compute X^p, F^((p-1)/2), and, for M=X^d-c, X^d by repeated squaring, then the kth powers. No dense degree-p or degree-d polynomial and no dense H is necessary. For fixed k<=5 a ring multiplication/reduction has bounded-size arithmetic circuits, and exponentiation uses O(log p + log d) ring multiplications. This is a circuit-size observation in field operations, conditional on compact evaluation of M; an arbitrary dense M still requires its input/evaluation cost. Bit-level modular multiplication and range enforcement are not free.

A multiplication/reduction can be lifted with intermediate convolution and monic-division variables so that each arithmetic gate equation has total degree at most2. Every intermediate value is deterministic once the field coefficients of A,B,U are given. Lower equation degree does not prove lower degree of regularity, solving degree, or SAT complexity; the added variables/Boolean carries may worsen these quantities. The construction supplies a concrete alternative to enumerating the prior p^3 coefficient prefixes and then computing one-variable gcd proofs. Whether certified partial coefficient-bit assignments prune before full assignments is the falsifiable next question.

Dimension/accounting caution: five-affine finite-target functions have four free F_p coefficients (five normalized A/B coefficients minus the target linear equation). This is not an intrinsic dimension reduction from the relation variety. Replacing ordered tuples by their unique normalized divisor function removes permutation multiplicity, only a constant for fixed k. Factorization/recovery, signed jets, chart selector variables, permutations, certificate production/checking, setup and failed searches all remain in the eventual comparison. There is still no unknown-scalar recovery algorithm or general ECDLP complexity improvement.

## Uniform-prime interval membership through quotient idempotents

New unreviewed derivation, 2026-10-30. This addresses the binomial support dependence on divisors of p-1, not the missing scalar-recovery/complexity bridge. Fix a power-of-two interval length B=2^s<p. In the finite commutative algebra Q=F_p[X]/(U), introduce elements e_0,...,e_(s-1), each represented by k field coefficients, satisfying e_j^2=e_j. Define n=X-sum_j 2^j e_j in Q and require n^k=0. Proposed equivalence: these equations have witnesses iff every root of U belongs to the canonical interval {0,...,B-1}, with arbitrary root multiplicity up to k.

Soundness derivation: evaluate at any root alpha of U in an algebraic closure. Each e_j(alpha) satisfies z^2-z=0, hence is0 or1. Nilpotence forces n(alpha)=0. Therefore alpha=sum_j2^j e_j(alpha) is a member of the canonical interval inside F_p (B<p prevents wrap and ensures distinct bit labels). This also proves splitting over F_p without a separate X^p-X exponentiation.

Completeness derivation: if U=product_(a in support)(X-a)^m_a with a in the interval, the pairwise-coprime local factors give Q isomorphic to the product of F_p[X]/((X-a)^m_a). Choose the jth bit of a as the constant0 or1 in each local factor and use CRT to obtain e_j in Q. Then n restricts to X-a on that factor, so n^k=0 since m_a<=k. In a local ring the only idempotents are0 and1; since the bit label of each interval element is unique, these e_j are unique for a fixed U. The nilpotent n is then determined as well. This is an algebraic uniqueness claim awaiting independent review, not measured SAT behavior.

Repeated roots are essential: requiring n=0 would incorrectly force away nontrivial nilpotent components. k=1 reduces exactly to ordinary coordinate bits. Distinct-root U has n=0 automatically. These supply explicit prospective positive/negative controls. Quotient multiplication remains commutative automatically; no unknown matrix-commutation constraints are needed. The prime field p>3, monic U, k>=1 and B<p are preconditions.

Combine this support encoding with the existing rational-ordinate condition [f*(f^((p-1)/2)-1)]^k=0 mod U and the normalized divisor-function equations. Thus the chart can represent interval factor bases uniformly in p without constructing product_(a=0)^(B-1)(X-a) or enumerating the coordinate interval during constraint construction. Recovery/certificates and useful relation density remain separate. Actual coordinate interval support is not guaranteed large after curve filtering or useful for every target; no distribution theorem is being assumed.

Size/cost boundary: there are k*s new field coefficients for the e_j, plus quotient-multiplication/reduction intermediates. k is at most5 here. Each e_j coefficient is an F_p element requiring canonical bit encoding; this can mean O(k*s*log p) Boolean variables before arithmetic gates, substantially more than assigning bits to ordered points. Field arithmetic circuits can be quadratically lifted, but that proves neither low regularity nor easy SAT. This must be compared to direct ordered-point bit encodings with complete multiset/ordered projections and all setup/check/reconstruction costs. Idempotents perform symmetric coordinate selection; they do not eliminate the relation variety's dimension or establish an asymptotic attack. Novelty unverified; finite-algebra/CRT/symmetric-encoding prior art must be checked before a novelty label.

## Explicit highest-part regularity obstruction (unreviewed symbolic derivation)

The repeated-root interval fixture exposes a precise metric trap. Take p=5, U=(X-1)^2, t=X-1 in Q, t^2=0, k=2, B=2, and e=a+b*t. The two coefficient equations of e^2-e=0 are g1=a^2-a and g2=(2a-1)*b. The two coefficients of (X-e)^2=0 are g3=(1-a)^2 and g4=2*(1-a)*(1-b). These four equations have unique solution(a,b)=(1,0): g3-g1=1-a, and then g2=b. This is an exact algebraic derivation, not an executed experimental result.

Yet their homogeneous highest-degree parts generate J=(a^2,2ab)=(a^2,ab). For every D>=1, b^D is not in J. Thus the degree defined as the first D at which the ideal of highest parts contains the entire degree-D component is not finite for this raw presentation. Quadratic generators and a unique affine solution do not establish a finite bound for that invariant. Conversely, this example is easy by low-degree linear combinations, so the obstruction is not a lower bound on SAT time or actual solving degree. Row reduction/preprocessing changes the presentation and may change the highest-part invariant; every such preprocessing operation and the resulting generator list must be explicit. Do not confuse this invariant with Castelnuovo-Mumford regularity, which is a different definition.
