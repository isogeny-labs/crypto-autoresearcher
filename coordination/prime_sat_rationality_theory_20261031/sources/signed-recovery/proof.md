# Signed recovery and localization of rationality checks

Status: UNREVIEWED mathematical derivation; unimplemented; zero trials. Coordinator assigned this five-file scratch report scope before writing. This extends the supplied-idempotent projector draft; it is not an accepted complete chart theorem or a measured improvement.

## Input contract

Let p>3 be prime, E: y^2=f(x)=x^3+ax+b nonsingular, h=A(x)+B(x)y nonzero with coefficients in F_p and exact pole order d>=2 at O. Let N=A^2-fB^2, with degree d, leading coefficient c in {1,-1} under the selected normalized chart. The different parities of 2deg A and 2deg B+3 prevent leading cancellation. For a finite rational target R=(r,s_R), d=k+1 and cN=(X-r)U, with h(-R)=A(r)-s_R B(r)=0. For R=O, d=k and U=cN. U is monic and k<=5. A supplied and independently verified factorization of U has distinct rational coordinates alpha and positive integer multiplicities m_U(alpha); in the intended interval construction it is recovered from checked idempotents.

Construct the norm factor list by adding one to m_U(r) for finite R, inserting r if absent, or using the U list unchanged for R=O. This step retains r even outside the factor-base interval. Check the full coefficient identity for N. Write its multiplicity at alpha as m. No generic factorization of N is needed after this verified U factorization.

Conventions: v_alpha(0)=infinity; h nonzero ensures at least one valuation is finite. Polynomial valuations are computed by repeated exact division by X-alpha, never by factorial-normalized derivatives. Branch points are roots of f; nonsingularity gives f'(alpha)!=0 there.

## Local parameter justification

At P=(alpha,beta) with beta!=0 over the algebraic closure, y+beta is a unit and

 (y-beta)(y+beta)=f(x)-f(alpha)=(x-alpha)F(x).

The maximal ideal (x-alpha,y-beta) is generated locally by x-alpha; smoothness gives this as a uniformizer. Thus a polynomial in x of root multiplicity a has order a at each sign.

At P=(alpha,0), write f(x)=(x-alpha)F(x) with F(alpha)!=0. The equation gives x-alpha=y^2/F(x), so the maximal ideal is generated by y, ord_P(y)=1 and ord_P(x-alpha)=2. These are direct local-ring arguments, not characteristic-zero Taylor expansions.

## Nonbranch roots: remove the common part once

Put a_alpha=v_alpha(A), b_alpha=v_alpha(B), g=min(a_alpha,b_alpha). Divide both A and B by (X-alpha)^g, obtaining A_0,B_0 with at least one nonzero value at alpha. Put q=m-2g. The norm identity gives q>=0.

If q>0 and f(alpha)!=0, then A_0(alpha)^2=f(alpha)B_0(alpha)^2. B_0(alpha) cannot be zero, since that would also make A_0(alpha)=0. Therefore

 beta=-A_0(alpha)/B_0(alpha) is in F_p, beta^2=f(alpha), beta!=0.

At (alpha,beta), h_0=A_0+B_0 y vanishes; at (alpha,-beta) it has value 2A_0(alpha)!=0. In the local norm product h_0 times its involution, the second factor is a unit at the first point. Consequently h_0 has order exactly q at the selected sign and zero at the opposite sign. The output multiplicities are g+q at beta and g at -beta, omitting any zero entry. No square-root routine or local-series expansion is required in this case.

If q=0, both residual values are nonzero at the two geometric signs. The multiplicities are g at each sign. Because alpha is a norm root, g>0. The signed divisor is rational-point supported exactly when f(alpha) is a nonzero square. Given a checked square root beta, output both signs with multiplicity g. If alpha=r for a finite target, use the already supplied nonzero target ordinate s_R as that root. Otherwise this is the only nonbranch case that still needs a square-root witness or a separately specified square-root procedure. Choice of root sign does not change the multiset.

These statements cover B=0: then g=v_alpha(A), m=2g, q=0. They also cover A=0 away from branch points. Neither case divides by a zero polynomial or zero residual B value.

## Branch roots (two-torsion)

For f(alpha)=0, P=(alpha,0) is rational. Orders of the two terms of h are 2a_alpha and 2b_alpha+1. Finite orders have different parity and cannot cancel, hence

 ord_P(h)=min(2a_alpha,2b_alpha+1).

The polynomial terms A^2 and fB^2 have exactly these different root orders in X, so this minimum also equals m=v_alpha(N). Output P with multiplicity m. Infinite valuations are handled by the other finite term. This includes characteristic five, repeated roots, A=0 and B=0; no division by multiplicities is used.

## Complete extraction and its independent certificate

Apply these cases to every verified norm root. The resulting geometric zero divisor has degree sum m=d, because nonbranch contributions sum to 2g+q=m and each branch contribution is m. Reject a nonsquare balanced pair. For finite R remove exactly one occurrence of (r,-s_R); verify its presence before subtraction. The residual degree must be k. Its coordinates must have precisely the U factor multiplicities, lie in the declared interval/base, satisfy the curve equation, and obey any separately declared subgroup restriction. Check exact elliptic-curve addition of all residual points gives R. Pad by 5-k copies of O only when identities belong to the output predicate; expand into distinct permutations only when ordered output is requested.

Independent checking uses the polynomial identities, exact local divisibilities/nondivisibilities, q and the sign formula or square-root equation, followed by the signed multiplicity subtraction and group check. Merely checking h(P)=0 would not verify multiplicity. This procedure recovers the divisor for a supplied chart h; it does not by itself prove that the chart enumeration reaches every relation. The Riemann-Roch normalization/descent correspondence remains a separate proof obligation. k=0 and nonexistent pole-order-one charts stay explicit special cases.

## New conditional simplification: rationality checks on the gcd support

Let D be the monic gcd(A,B), degree g_D, and write A=D A_*, B=D B_* with gcd(A_*,B_*)=1. Adopt gcd(A,0)=monic(A) and the symmetric convention. All roots of D are rational because D^2 divides N and the norm factor list is verified to split over F_p.

At a nonbranch root outside D, q=m>0, so the ratio formula already gives a rational ordinate. Branch roots are rational automatically. At a root of D, if the reduced norm also vanishes, the same ratio formula again supplies a square root. Only the remaining balanced roots need a separate rationality test. In particular the following simpler condition is necessary and sufficient for rational-point support of the entire zero divisor:

 for every root alpha of D, f(alpha)=0 or f(alpha) is a square in F_p.

Necessity follows because the common factor contributes both geometric signs (or the branch point); sufficiency follows from the local cases above. For a finite rational target, removing -R cannot eliminate a nonsquare common-root pair: such a root cannot equal r. Thus the same condition characterizes rational support of the residual divisor.

Define T(X)=f(X)*(f(X)^((p-1)/2)-1). By Euler's criterion, T(alpha)=0 exactly for zero or square f(alpha). For g_D>=1 the condition is equivalent to

 T^g_D = 0 in F_p[X]/(D).

Each root multiplicity in D is at most g_D, so vanishing at each root is equivalent to this nilpotence test, including repeated roots. If D=1 the condition is vacuous. Thus the full T^k mod U test can, conditional on verified split norm and exact gcd, be localized to the common factor. This is an algebraic equivalence, NOT a proven SAT propagation or regularity reduction.

In the five-affine finite-target chart, deg A=3 and deg B<=1. When B!=0, g_D<=1. When B=0, g_D=3 and the balanced-pair chart must be retained. In the identity-target five-affine chart, deg B=1 and deg A<=2, so g_D<=1. Lower-pole strata remain necessary. Do not turn the gcd-one case into a completeness restriction.

To make gcd stratification part of an encoding, introduce a monic D of each allowed degree, coefficient identities A=D A_*, B=D B_*, and a checked Bezout identity S A_*+T_* B_*=1. This establishes exact gcd, but creates auxiliary variables and possibly nonunique certificates. Degree bounds, canonicalization/projection, coefficient-generation cost, and all gcd-degree strata must be specified before claiming an equivalent solver or lower degree. For completed h, ordinary Euclidean gcd is an exact cheap postprocessing operation; it is not free inside partial-assignment search.

## Cost boundary and sources

For fixed d<=6, norm-factor verification and repeated polynomial division are bounded-degree field arithmetic; a conservative O(d^3) field-operation allowance covers the local arithmetic after U recovery, excluding square-root construction, source search, ordered output, and separately charged group/field bit costs. At most floor(d/2) balanced nonbranch coordinates can request square roots because each consumes at least two zero-divisor degrees. Known target ordinates and ratio cases need none. Supplied-root verification is one square per coordinate. No runtime measurements or uniform deterministic polylogarithmic square-root construction are asserted.
