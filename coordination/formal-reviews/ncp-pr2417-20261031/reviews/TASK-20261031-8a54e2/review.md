## Independent calculation of the two images

Write A for the free associative integer algebra, C for its commutator ideal, v for the negation generator, t for one chosen translation generator, and w=(vt)^2-1.

1. The unique algebra homomorphism a:A→Z sending every free generator to zero satisfies a(xy-yx)=a(x)a(y)-a(y)a(x)=0. Its kernel is a two-sided ideal, so C⊆ker(a). Since a(1)=1, **a(w)=(0·0)^2-1=-1**, which is nonzero in Z. Therefore w∉C. This is the zero-generator augmentation of a free algebra. The usual group-algebra augmentation sending basis group elements to 1 would give zero on this witness and would not separate it from C.
2. Put N(x)=-x and T_s(x)=x+s in ZMod n. With the pinned permutation product, NT_s(x)=-(x+s)=-x-s. Applying it again gives `-((-x-s)+s)=x`. Thus `(NT_s)^2=id`. If δ_g denotes the basis element of the group algebra, then **evalRho(w)=δ_(NT_s)^2-δ_id=δ_id-δ_id=0**. The first equality uses the actual basis monoid homomorphism, rather than merely checking the orbit of one point.

This computation uses commutativity of the additive group ZMod n. Although the supporting `affineGroup` constructors accept an arbitrary additive group, the companion theorem specializes to ZMod n; no general nonabelian additive-group extension is being asserted. No primality, oddness, invertibility of s, positive modulus, finite-generation or surjectivity hypothesis is needed for this witness.

At B=1,n=2,s=1: N is the identity permutation `(0,1)`, T is `(1,0)`, NT is `(1,0)`, and `(NT)^2=(0,1)`. Thus the group-algebra image is zero while the free-algebra augmentation is -1. At s=0 both generator images are identity and the same conclusion holds. For n=1 the action is again trivial. For n=0, ZMod 0 is Z and the symbolic identity still applies. B=0 supplies no `i : Fin 0`, so the companion theorem has no selected translation instance; the original v^2-1 theorem remains available.

## Historical full-presentation boundary at n=2

Let J be the historical Theorem 2 ideal: translation commutators, v^2-1, (vt_i)^2-1, and all lattice binomials t^u-t^v. With B=1,n=2,s=1, the permutation evaluation kills **v-1**, because negation is identity.

Define a different integer-valued algebra map χ by **χ(t)=1, χ(v)=-1**. It kills every displayed generator of J: v^2-1 and (vt)^2-1 map to zero, translation commutators vanish, and every lattice binomial maps to `1^u-1^v=0`. This last argument covers all nonnegative exponents, not only the 25 small parity-compatible pairs recorded in the control output. But **χ(v-1)=-2**, so v-1∉J. Therefore the n=2 extension of `ker(evalRho)=J` is false. The corresponding group presentation retains a nontrivial negation generator; mapping it to the sign group gives the same separation.

The exact producer note already adds the odd-order boundary and explicitly distinguishes the written full equalities from its Lean targets. This control confirms that boundary; it is not a defect in the universally quantified companion witness.

## Separate historical target defect: group algebra versus point operators

The source note, §1 (E2), claims an inclusion of Z[Aff(G)] in End(Z[E(F_p)]). A group embedding into permutations does not imply that its linear extension is injective.

For G=Z/3, let T(x)=x+1 and N(x)=-x, and define

```text
q = (δ_id + δ_T + δ_(T^2)) - (δ_N + δ_(TN) + δ_(T^2N)).
```

All six affine permutations are distinct, so q≠0 in the integer group algebra. For each x∈G, the three translations send x to all of G once, and the three translated reflections also send x to all of G once. Therefore q sends every basis point to zero. The exact control retains all six nonzero coefficients and the **3×3 zero operator matrix**.

This case can occur within the historical prime-field setup: the nonsingular curve **E/F5: y^2=x^3+4x+2** has exactly `(3,1)`, `(3,4)` and the point at infinity, so E(F5)=G has order 3. The raw control checks this point enumeration and `4a^3+27b^2=4 mod 5≠0`; it does not implement or formally verify the elliptic-curve group law. The usual elliptic-curve group theorem identifies this three-element group with Z/3. The group-algebra counterexample itself depends only on the explicit Z/3 permutations.

Hence the historical faithful-action bridge **breaks** even with odd prime subgroup order. A correct bridge is an algebra homomorphism to operators with its own kernel; injectivity cannot be supplied by group-action faithfulness. The companion declarations land in the group algebra and retain their stated witness interpretation. No full-kernel theorem for the operator target is reviewed or inferred here.

## Obligations beyond this witness

To formalize the historical full equalities at their intended group-algebra target, the following additional objects and implications are needed individually:

1. Define the free group, its evaluation, the lattice L and the normal closure, with a precise relation between chronological word action and homomorphic composition. Reversal/inversion must be accounted for explicitly when comparing equalities.
2. Prove the dihedral presentation quotient is Z^B⋊C2, including existence and uniqueness of normal forms and the exact kernel L×{+1} of the map to affine **permutations**. The sign action must be faithful in the stated odd-order setting.
3. Define the historical ideal J in A. Its translation-commutator generators differ from C's all-element commutators. Prove J⊆ker(evalRho), including every lattice binomial and every torsion relation.
4. Prove the converse inclusion: translations become units in A/J via t_i^n=1, negation conjugates them to their inverses, and every monomial reduces to a translation class times v^e. Then prove the induced quotient map is injective, for example by a correctly constructed basis or the finite-rank argument.
5. Supply the target cardinality and generation hypotheses used by that rank argument: odd n for distinct signs; B≥1 and a generating translation if invoking s_1 and surjectivity onto all 2n affine permutations. Empty/non-generating families need an image-based argument. The companion makes none of these assumptions.
6. When claiming an elliptic-curve interpretation, supply the cyclic-group identification and its compatibility with actual translations and negation. If the intended target is End(Z[E(F_p)]), replace the false injective-algebra bridge with the actual representation map and characterize its additional kernel.

The existing witness neither defines J nor gives either full-presentation equality. These are proof obligations, not mathematical impossibility assertions about those equalities at the correct target and hypotheses.
