# Curve25519 index calculus: an atlas of coordinates, summation-polynomial symmetry and factor bases

Status 2026-11-04. Written for the owner's question: which coordinates,
point representations, equations and factor bases can speed up index
calculus on Curve25519, and what each one measured.

**Class:** a synthesis of measured studies plus the theory that bounds them.
Every number has a source path. Nothing here is a leaderboard row. Rho
remains ahead on every track, and the distance remaining is stated in
each table.

## 0. How to read this

- **Curves.** The Curve25519 family miniatures built by Curve25519's own
  rule (`curve25519_family family`): p the largest prime < 2^k with
  p ≡ 5 (mod 8), smallest A ≡ 2 (mod 4) with #E = 8r, r prime, twist order
  4r′. k = 20: p = 1,048,573, A = 926, r = 131,203, √r = 362.2. Also
  k = 16 (A = 1058), 18 (78), 22 (43186), 24 (318).
- **Units.** Counted only. GAE = group additions equivalent. S = (counted
  GAE + w × unpriced native operations)/√r, priced at w = 0, 0.1 and 1 GAE
  per native operation (AGENTS.md §5a). F4 work is counted in
  multiply-adds and is native (unpriced at w = 0). No wall-clock claim
  appears anywhere in this atlas.
- **Two tracks.**
  - *Generic track*: a cofactor-projected factor base and a pair table
    (`prime-projected-abscissa` + `mitm`). This is the lab's baseline,
    `proj-a`.
  - *Algebraic track*: a factor base with an algebraic description
    (an x-interval, a tower) and Semaev's summation polynomial solved by
    F4 (`pkm-semaev`). Coordinates and symmetries only matter here.
- **Cost model** (validated in §6): F4 work per solve
  E = columns × r × mpt / covered, where columns = base size, covered =
  exact number of subgroup points that are a sum of two base points, and
  mpt = F4 multiply-adds per target, which depends on (form, t, G) and not
  on the seed or the curve.

## 1. The family's torsion, and what it rules in and out

E(F_p) ≅ Z/8 × Z/r is cyclic for every family curve (the family rule
requires it). Consequences:

| fact | consequence |
|:--|:--|
| E[2](F_p) = Z/2, generated by T₀ = (0, 0) in Montgomery form | exactly one translation acts on the x-line: u ↦ 1/u. This is the only pairwise symmetry available to summation polynomials over F_p. |
| rational points of order 4 and 8 exist (u = ±1 have order 4) | translation by them is not a function of x. Any coordinate on which T₄ acts is invariant under 2T₄ = T₀, so it lives on E/⟨T₀⟩, where T₄ has become a 2-torsion point: the same symmetry on an isogenous curve, not a new one (`research/notes/index-calculus/RESEARCH_EXOTIC_COORDINATES.md` §1.3). |
| E[2](F_p) ≠ Z/2 × Z/2 | Jacobi intersection and Huff models do not exist over F_p; the full FGHR symmetry group (which needs E[2] rational, or an extension field) is not available. |
| no rational 3-torsion (#E = 8r) | Hessian and twisted Hessian models do not exist over F_p. |
| j ≠ 0, 1728 | Aut(E) = {±1}: no GLV/GLS orbit folding. The strongest rho is the negation-map walk, `rho-neg`. |
| prime field | no Frobenius; no Weil restriction gain. |
| p ≡ 5 (mod 8) | 2^t ∤ p − 1 for t ≥ 3: the PKM Kummer tower exists only for t ≤ 2. p − 1 = 4 × 3³ × 7 × 19 × 73 at k = 20, so odd multiplicative subgroups exist (lead L11, §9). |
| cofactor 8, cyclic | every point has a cofactor coset c ∈ Z/8. A sum of two base points lands in the subgroup iff their cosets cancel. This is the quantity every algebraic-track gain in this atlas comes from (§5). |

## 2. Coordinates and curve models

Every separable degree-2 coordinate f: E → P¹ is the quotient by an
involution P ↦ T′ − P; conjugating by a translation moves T′ to O, so
f = N(x(P + S)) for a Möbius map N ∈ PGL₂ and a point S
(`src/cryptanalysis/coordinate_search.rs`, checked numerically there).
A factor base "f(P) in an interval" is therefore a Möbius frame of x,
translated by S. The translation shifts every log by log S; on the
algebraic track that changes the coset of every pair sum by 2·c(S) and
nothing else.

**The census result that settles the model question.** On five family
curves, over 2,000 random translation frames and 2,000 random Möbius
frames per (curve, t), Weierstrass lo = 0 and Edwards y are ordinary
draws: 49 of 50 cells inside the central 98% (P1), translation ≡ Möbius
with mean ratio 0.986–1.012 (P2) (`curve25519_frame_search_20261103`).
Edwards "beat" Weierstrass in the earlier study only because lo = 0 was a
below-average draw on that curve.

**What does work: choosing the frame.** The F4 system is identical in
every frame. Choosing lo per (curve, t) by the log-free coset count J
(read from [r]P) raises coverage 2.5–2.8× (t = 5), about 2× (t = 6),
1.5–1.7× (t = 7) over the mean frame, at unchanged per-target cost; in
the engine the F4 work per relation fell 4.71× (t = 5) and 3.33× (t = 6)
against lo = 0 at k = 20.

## 3. Summation polynomials and their symmetry

### 3.1 The forms in use

- Weierstrass S₃(x₁, x₂, x₃) for y² = x³ + ax + b, as a quadratic in x₃:
  (x₁ − x₂)² x₃² − 2[(x₁ + x₂)(x₁x₂ + a) + 2b] x₃ + (x₁x₂ − a)² − 4b(x₁ + x₂)
  (`pkm_semaev.rs`, `s3_in_u`). S₄ is the resultant of two S₃'s.
- Montgomery form in u: the same polynomial after x = u + A/3; its
  coefficients involve A only. Over F_p coefficient size is irrelevant to
  F4, and the term structure is the same.
- Edwards y form: S₃ with x_i = (1 + s) + (1 − s) y_i over 1 − y_i,
  denominators cleared (`Membership::EdwardsBits`). Same degree.
- T₀-quotient form: the ordinary S₃ of E/⟨T₀⟩ at w_i + s equals
  S₃(z₁, z₂, z₃) · S₃(d/z₁, z₂, z₃) up to a monomial (unit tests
  `t0_symmetrised_polynomial_is_s3_times_its_t0_image`, and the baseline
  identity S₃(1/z₁, 1/z₂, z₃) = S₃/(z₁z₂)²). It vanishes exactly when
  ±P₁ ± P₂ ± P₃ ∈ {O, T₀}.

### 3.2 The symmetry group of the relation on this family

A symmetry of E acts on the x-line iff it normalises [−1], i.e. is a
translation by 2-torsion or an automorphism. On the relation
P₁ + ⋯ + P_{m+1} = O:

| symmetry | available here? | scope | effect on S_{m+1} |
|:--|:--|:--|:--|
| S_m permutations of the summands | always | global | S_{m+1} is symmetric in x₁…x_m; writing it in elementary symmetric functions divides the Gröbner work by about m! only for m ≥ 3 |
| negation | always | per point | already quotiented out by using x |
| translation by T₀ ∈ E[2](F_p) | yes (one point) | pairwise: applied to an even number of summands | the invariant ring halves the degree in the invariant variable (FGHR); on E/⟨T₀⟩ this is the quotient's own S₃ |
| translation by the other 2-torsion points | no (irrational) | — | — |
| translation by 4- or 8-torsion | no action on the x-line | — | per-point coordinates cannot see it (§1) |
| Aut(E)/±1 | trivial (j ≠ 0, 1728) | — | no orbit collapse |
| Frobenius | none (prime field) | — | no Weil restriction |

So the group acting is S_m × Z/2, and it is the smallest case in the
literature. What it buys, measured:

- At m = 2, S₃ has degree 2 in each variable, and symmetrisation cannot
  lower it. Measured: the T₀ form has the same solving degree, the same
  terms and the same F4 cost per target as the plain form in every pair
  (`curve25519_t0_algebraic_20261102`). Its only effect is on the base:
  closing the base under u ↦ 1/u doubles the sums reached (R and R + T₀),
  but at equal column count the closed base has fewer distinct sums, so
  per relation it costs 1.2–3.0× more.
- At m = 3, S₄ has degree 4 per variable and symmetrisation would matter
  more, but m = 3 is 2,297× (t = 4) and 6,643× (t = 5) costlier per
  relation than selected m = 2 at k = 20 (T3). A factor of a few from
  symmetry does not close that.
- The pairwise T₀ action on the *target* side (accept relations for
  R + K, K ∈ E[8], the `lifts` oracle) is the generic-track projection in
  disguise: on the generic track projection recovers all 8 cosets and
  beats T₀ keying by 3.3–4.0×; on the algebraic track, after frame or
  seed selection, lifts add only 1.00–1.12× (T1).

### 3.3 Why the symmetry story is short on this family

FGHR's degree reduction comes from E[2] ≅ Z/2 × Z/2 (or from
Frobenius over F_{p^n}), and the big index-calculus gains in the
literature come from m ≥ 3 with those symmetries. Curve25519's rational
2-torsion is Z/2 and its field is prime, so the only symmetric reduction
available is the T₀ one, and at m = 2 it reduces nothing. Everything the
algebraic track gained here came from *selection* (frames, seeds, G),
not from symmetry.

## 5. Cosets: the mechanism behind every algebraic-track gain

Write c(P) ∈ Z/8 for the cofactor coset of a base point, read off [r]P
without any discrete log. A pair P_i ± P_j is a usable relation for a
subgroup target iff c_i ± c_j ≡ 0 (mod 8).

- For a signed pair of uniformly random cosets, P(c_a ± c_b ≡ 0) =
  14/64 ≈ 0.22. The census counts something different: covered subgroup
  points (±R counted separately) over unordered column pairs
  (n(n+1)/2). On that denominator a uniform base gives about 0.50
  (seed 0: 172/325 = 0.53, seed 1093: 528/1081 = 0.49).
- Selection pushes this up. For seed 1308 at t = 6, 498 covered points
  over 36·37/2 = 666 column pairs: 0.75, that is 1.5× the uniform value
  on the same denominator (validator E4).
- The selectors used: J = number of cancelling pairs (frames, seeds);
  J/columns (T9) because end-to-end cost ∝ columns × r / coverage. J/cols
  picked the best seed of all 2,000 in 14 of 15 (k, t) cells.
- Filtering a base to a single coset class cannot help: keeping only
  coset 4 (or only 0) keeps about 1/8 of the columns and makes every pair
  cancel (at most 2× the selected base's 0.75 on this denominator), which
  loses to keeping 8× the columns (cost ∝ 1/(columns × p_cancel)).
- Restricting cosets *algebraically* (2-descent, T₀ quotient) raises the
  polynomial degree and loses; *selecting* among parameterisations with an
  unchanged system wins. That is the lesson of waves 1–3.
- Torsion-shifted targets (decompose R + T_c, c chosen from the coset
  histogram) add 1.00–1.12× after selection (T1 failed its 1.10× rule in
  19 of 20 cells).

## 7. The grid limit, stated plainly

The winner (t = 5, G = 4) solves 16 tiny F4 systems per target, 52,816
multiply-adds in total. The exhaustive engine on the same base evaluates
S₃ on every pair of the 32 x-values of V: about n² + 7n = 1,248 F_p
multiplications per target from the grid code. So the best F4 arm is
still roughly 40× above brute force on its own factor base, and the
generic pair table (`proj-a`) is 7.4·10³× ahead of it at w = 0.1. The
joint (t, G, seed) optimisation has moved the F4 track *toward* the grid
limit, not past it. Lead L13 (wave 4) measured this end to end with
`engine=exhaustive` and `mitm` on the winner's base: grid ÷ F4 = 0.0238
at w = 0.1, so the F4 arm is 42× above its grid. The pair table is
another 76× below the grid, and is still 2.4–3.3× behind proj-a at every
weight. On this base no oracle reaches proj-a; the 22-column base sets
the floor (§11.3). Until the algebraic
track beats its own grid, its value is in how it scales, not in its
constant.

## 8. Scaling

At fixed (t, G), mpt does not depend on k (T4-rep, T6, T10), and the
*distribution* of columns and covered over seeds at fixed t does not
either (the selected values vary by curve, 28/198 … 39/364 at t = 6), so
E = columns × r × mpt / covered is linear in r up to that spread,
against rho's √r. The algebraic track at m = 2 can only beat exponent 1 if the
optimal t grows with k and mpt(t, G) grows slower than coverage. At
k = 20 the optimal t is 5 and mpt jumps 13× from (5, 4) to (6, 4). Lead
L10 (wave 4) measures the optimum at k = 16, 18, 22, 24 and fits the
exponent. No 255-bit statement is made or implied.

Ideas considered and not pursued, with the reason:

- **Two towers with complementary cosets** (cross pairs only): bounded by
  1/0.75 ≈ 1.33× over the selected single tower, below the measurement
  noise of a 16-target session.
- **Montgomery-form S₃ per-target cost**: coefficient size is irrelevant
  mod p and the term structure is identical; the only measured
  form-dependence (Edwards, G = 0) vanishes at the cells that matter.
- **Edwards-x bases**: a translate by an order-4 point; identical to a
  frame plus lifts.
- **y-coordinate or higher-degree coordinates**: degree penalty, as T2.
- **Working on E/⟨T₈⟩ or another isogenous curve**: the discrete logarithm
  is isogeny-invariant and the isogenous curve has its own cofactor
  cosets; nothing is gained beyond moving the frame.
- **Weil restriction to F_{p²}**: the target and base stay in E(F_p); no
  new symmetry appears.
- **Coset-pure filtering**: loses to selection (§5).
- **Multi-target amortisation** (precompute base logs once): a reporting
  convention (`ecbench amortize`), not a new method; it does not change
  the per-relation F4 cost that dominates.

### 11.1 L10: the exponent (validated)

40 engine cells, k ∈ {16, 18, 20, 22, 24} × t ∈ {4, 5, 6, 7} at
G = t − 1 with the J/columns seed, plus controls. All seven predictions
held.

- **mpt is k-invariant to the digit** in every hit-free cell:
  17,592 (t = 4, G = 3), 52,816 (5, 4), 146,464 (6, 5), about 4.2·10⁵
  (7, 6). Seed changes it by < 2%.
- **The optimal t is 4 at every k**, with t = 5 second at 1.7–2.5×.
  T10 had scanned t ≥ 5 only, so its winner (t = 5, G = 4) is not the
  optimum. The next end-to-end candidate is **t = 4, G = 3** with the
  J/columns seed (1193 at k = 20: 8 columns, 52 covered), modelled 2.04×
  below T12's winner.
- **E_best(k) ∝ r^1.03** (frozen analysis 1.0297; 1.0315 with the
  validator's per-target native-work correction). Per step in t the ratio
  columns × mpt / covered grows 1.6–2.5×, so beating exponent 1 would need
  coverage to grow 8–19× per step against the measured 2–5×.
- **The gap to rho grows as r^0.53**: in the F4-phase model, 1.5·10⁴×
  (k = 16) to 2.8·10⁵× (k = 24) at w = 0.1, with rho's S fixed at 1.5
  (a conservative bound; T12 measured 1.65 at w = 0.1).
- Errata: the protocol carried r₁₈ = 32,719 instead of 32,887 (0.5%,
  no verdict changes); the frozen script priced native work per 100
  targets instead of per target (b moves 1.0297 → 1.0315).

This is the atlas's main structural result: on this family, m = 2
algebraic index calculus with F4 scales *worse* than rho by a factor
that itself grows like √r, whatever frame, seed or guess depth is chosen.

### 11.2 L12: F4 trace replay (validated, both stages)

Learning one refutation trace per branch system and replaying it per
target cuts the winner's per-target F4 work **0.4717×** (24,912 against
52,816 mul-adds) with identical verdicts, zero fallbacks, and a learning
cost of exactly one plain target. At (t = 6, G = 4) the ratio is 0.454
for both seeds; at (5, 3) it is 0.566, so the frozen "≤ 0.5 in every
cell" rule failed there. End to end (8 targets × 2 rounds, four arms), all four predictions held:
S(trace)/S(plain) = **0.4742 [0.47418, 0.47425]** at w = 0.1 and 0.4741
at w = 1, with the walk, relations and recovered scalars identical
(w = 0 ratio exactly 1). Charging the unpriced learning by hand moves it
to 0.4744. Distance to rho-neg at w = 0.1: 2.63·10⁴× [1.52·10⁴,
4.54·10⁴]. Of 1.6·10⁶ replays, 223 fell back to plain F4 (59 genuine
replay failures). Counted units only: solver wall time is 0.95×, not
0.47×.

### 11.3 L13: the grid-limit control (validated)

Five arms on the winner's own base, 16 one-target chunks, 32 runs each,
all verified. 6 of 8 predictions held.

- **grid ÷ F4 = 0.0238 [0.02382, 0.02384]** at w = 0.1 (frozen point
  0.0245), with identical trials, relations and GAE in all 32 pairs.
  The best F4 arm is 42× above its own brute-force limit.
- Pair table ÷ grid = 0.0131 at w = 0.1 and 0.0028 at w = 1; 1.047 at
  w = 0 (its 506 table additions).
- **Even the pair table on this base loses to proj-a at every weight:
  2.39× / 2.54× / 3.32×** at w = 0 / 0.1 / 1. With the oracle held
  fixed, the 22-column isogeny base loses to the 32-column projected
  base. The grid is 2.28× / 194× / 1,181× behind proj-a.
- P4 failed on its per-pair clause: GAE(mitm) − GAE(grid) is 437–489,
  not 506 ± 2 × relations, because the grid and F4 arms pay about 4 extra
  lift additions per relation that the table's signed probe does not. P8 failed as the frozen-rule defect: the grid
  count is exactly 1,248 × solver calls in 32/32 runs, not 1,248 × trials.
  Neither is retuned.

The consequence for the algebraic track: none of the three m = 2 oracles
on this base (t = 5, seed 1307, k = 20) overtakes proj-a, and no oracle can
go below the w = 0 row, already 2.3× behind. The preregistered model puts

10.1× proj-a's); this run does not isolate it. Validated pass_with_errata.

### 11.4 L14: digit-set frames (census validated; lead open)

x = lo + Σ c_j b_j with arbitrary digits c_j keeps the F4 system
identical to `bits`. Exact census at k = 20:

- Random digit sets and random translation frames have the same
  coverage distribution (mean ratios 0.985–1.015; the max-ratio rule
  failed at t = 5 on one outlying translation frame).
- **A greedy digit choice (B = 2,000 candidates per digit, selected by J)
  covers 522 points on 22 columns at t = 5 and 1,268 on 43 at t = 6,
  against 248 and 570 for the best of 2,000 translation frames: 2.1× and
  2.2×** (rule ≥ 1.3×; the point prediction of 1.1–1.45× failed on the
  high side).
- Mechanism (exploratory): the greedy builds an additive tower inside a
  single cofactor coset (16 of 22 points in coset 0 at t = 5), so
  covered/column is 2.3–2.5× the best translation frame. Replicates
  spread 1.19–1.49× depending on the starting lo.
- Where it lands: the bits form's per-target F4 cost is 17–64× the
  isogeny form's at equal (t, G), so a digit-set bits arm models about
  6.9× behind the T10 winner at (5, 3).
- **L15 (engine, all five predictions held):** with `Membership::Digits`
  (`b847203692`) the greedy sets keep the `bits` system exactly (same
  degree and terms, per-target cost within 1–3%) and deliver their census
  coverage, so F4 work per relation falls 0.48× (t = 5) and 0.44× (t = 6)
  against the best translation frame. At matched (t = 5, G = 4) the digit
  set stays 10.5× behind the isogeny winner (per-target 1,375,663 against
  52,816 mul-adds).
