## Convention and notation

Let k be a field. There are B Boolean bits, partitioned into G actual groups of widths w_g>=1, with sum_g w_g=B. An empty group is omitted. Write

\[
q_g=2^{w_g}-1,\qquad N=\sum_g q_g.
\]

Let R=k[b_1,\ldots,b_B], let

\[
\mathcal B=(b_i^2-b_i:1\leq i\leq B),
\]

and let f_1,...,f_m be the original additional constraints. The original affine ideal is I=\mathcal B+(f_1,\ldots,f_m).

Let S=k[u_{g,a}:a\in\{0,1\}^{w_g}\setminus\{0\}], and let J be generated by

\[
u_{g,a}^2-u_{g,a},\qquad
u_{g,a}u_{g,b}\quad(a\ne b),
\]

with each unordered distinct pair included once. There are no exclusions between different groups.

For a finite generating list \(\mathcal F\), discard zero polynomials before taking highest homogeneous parts and put

\[
d_{\rm reg}(\mathcal F)=
\min\{d\geq0:(\mathcal F^{\rm top})_d=S_d\}.
\]

This is the convention of Caminata–Gorla Definition 1.4. In these systems the threshold exists because local highest parts already give an Artinian quotient. It is an invariant of the stated highest-part generating ideal, and is not asserted to depend only on the affine ideal, to be a first/last fall degree, or to equal Castelnuovo–Mumford regularity or solving degree. A nonzero constant generator gives threshold 0. For the zero-variable empty local system, k has nonzero degree 0 and zero positive degrees, so the local threshold is 1.

## Joint local_models: holds

Over a field, u^2-u=u(u-1)=0 forces u=0 or u=1. If two different indicators in a group were 1, their exclusion product would be 1, violating a constraint. Conversely the all-zero vector and each of the q_g unit vectors satisfy every local equation. Thus each group has exactly q_g+1=2^{w_g} solutions: the zero word and one solution for each nonzero word.

There is no characteristic restriction. In particular, characteristic 2 does not identify 0 and 1. The argument uses the absence of zero divisors in a field, and is not an assertion about arbitrary nonreduced coefficient rings.

This argument also determines the affine quotient, beyond just its rational points. In one group, the relations reduce every polynomial to

\[
c_0+\sum_a c_a u_a.
\]

Evaluation at the zero vector gives c_0, and evaluation at the a-th unit vector gives c_0+c_a. If all evaluations vanish then every coefficient vanishes. Therefore these q_g+1 normal forms are a basis, and the local affine quotient is the product of q_g+1 copies of k. Tensoring across groups gives

\[
S/J\simeq k^{\,2^B}.
\]

All-zero indicators are essential. No digit-0 variable and no equation requiring an active indicator are needed.

## Joint bijection_and_ideals: holds

For position j within group g define

\[
v_{g,j}=\sum_{a:a_j=1}u_{g,a},
\qquad
\phi:R\longrightarrow S,\quad b_{g,j}\longmapsto v_{g,j}.
\]

At a local zero vector every v is zero; at the a-th unit vector the projected bit tuple is exactly a. This is a bijection between local states and Boolean words.

An explicit inverse assignment polynomial for a nonzero word a is

\[
\delta_{g,a}(b)=
\left(\prod_{j:a_j=1}b_{g,j}\right)
\left(\prod_{j:a_j=0}(1-b_{g,j})\right),
\]

where the two parenthesized products are multiplied. Equivalently,

\[
\delta_{g,a}(b)=\prod_{j=1}^{w_g}
b_{g,j}^{a_j}(1-b_{g,j})^{1-a_j}.
\]

The zero-word indicator is

\[
\delta_{g,0}=\prod_j(1-b_{g,j})
=1-\sum_{a\ne0}\delta_{g,a}\pmod{\mathcal B}.
\]

Define \(\psi:S\to R\) by \(u_{g,a}\mapsto\delta_{g,a}\). Boolean evaluation, or the product-of-fields description just proved, gives

\[
\phi(\mathcal B)\subseteq J,\qquad
\psi(J)\subseteq\mathcal B,
\]

and the induced maps

\[
\bar\phi:R/\mathcal B\longrightarrow S/J,\qquad
\bar\psi:S/J\longrightarrow R/\mathcal B
\]

are inverse isomorphisms. Their two compositions send each bit or indicator to itself in its respective quotient. This also proves correspondence over every extension field of k; no algebraic-closure assumption is needed.

Set

\[
K=J+(\phi(f_1),\ldots,\phi(f_m)).
\]

Then

\[
R/I\simeq S/K,\qquad
K=J+\phi(I)S,\qquad
\phi^{-1}(K)=I.
\]

These are the appropriate ideal statements in the two ambient polynomial rings. The two ideals are not literally equal subsets of one ring. Both affine quotients are products of copies of k corresponding to the same subset of Boolean assignments, and in particular both affine ideals are radical.

Keeping the projected Boolean generators is optional for affine equivalence. It also does not alter the highest-part ideal here: \(v_{g,j}^2\) consists entirely of same-group quadratics already in \(J^{\rm top}\).

## Joint representation_counts: holds

With the original Boolean generators replaced by the local list, the exact counts are

\[
N=\sum_g(2^{w_g}-1),\qquad
E_{\rm local}=\sum_g\left(q_g+\binom{q_g}{2}\right)
=\sum_g\frac{q_g(q_g+1)}2.
\]

The full explicit list has E_local+m equations. If all B projected original Boolean equations are retained redundantly, its listed count is E_local+B+m instead. A duplicated symmetric exclusion is not a distinct unordered exclusion in these counts.

For maximum block width w and B=qw+r, 0<=r<w, use q genuine width-w groups and, only if r>0, one width-r group. The remainder contributes \(2^r-1\) variables and \((2^r-1)2^r/2\) local equations. Padding it to width w changes both the state space and the counts unless additional constraints are charged.

For the elliptic specialization, group within each coordinate separately. Put

\[
s=2q+e,\quad e\in\{0,1\}.
\]

There are 2q groups of width 2 and 2e groups of width 1 in total. Hence

\[
G=2q+2e=2\lceil s/2\rceil,\quad
N=6q+2e=3s-e,\quad
E_{\rm local}=12q+2e=6s-4e.
\]

The full S3 list has 6s-4e+1 equations, or that many plus 2s if redundant projected Boolean equations are retained. The original explicit list has 2s variables and 2s+1 equations.

For one group, width 1 contributes one variable and one equation; width 2 contributes three variables and six equations. These are actual shortened final groups. If instead the two coordinates are concatenated and an odd trailing bit from one coordinate is paired across the coordinate boundary, the counts and the later separated-coordinate proof are different; that is not the grouping analyzed here.

## Joint hilbert_series: holds

The highest parts of the local equations generate exactly

\[
L=J^{\rm top}=
\left(u_{g,a}u_{g,b}:g\text{ fixed},\ a,b\text{ arbitrary, including }a=b\right).
\]

A monomial lies outside this monomial ideal exactly when it contains at most one indicator from each group. The residue classes of these monomials form a basis. In particular,

\[
C=S/L\simeq\bigotimes_{g=1}^G(k\oplus V_g),
\quad \dim V_g=q_g,\quad V_gV_g=0,
\]

as graded algebras. The graded Hilbert series is therefore

\[
H_C(t)=\prod_g(1+q_gt),\qquad
\dim_k C=\prod_g(1+q_g)=2^B.
\]

The coefficient in degree G is \(\prod_g q_g>0\); all higher coefficients vanish. Thus the exact local generating-list threshold is G+1. For separate paired coordinates,

\[
H_C(t)=(1+3t)^{2q}(1+t)^{2e}.
\]

This C is the quotient by local highest parts. It is not the affine quotient S/J, despite the matching total dimensions, and it is not generally the quotient by the full system's highest parts. Extra constraints can reduce its Hilbert function.

## Joint regularity_bound: holds with the stated scopes

For any raw substituted additional constraints, the full highest-part ideal contains L. Consequently

\[
d_{\rm reg}(\mathcal F_{\rm grouped})\leq G+1.
\]

The same upper bound holds if the substituted constraints are first reduced modulo J, since the explicit local generators remain in the list. This proves no general equality: adding the constant equation 1 gives threshold 0, and lower-degree extra constraints can kill the top component. Without extra constraints the bound is already attained.

For fixed-target S3, write the original coordinate substitutions as

\[
x=h_x+X,\quad X=\sum_{j=1}^s\alpha_jb_j,\qquad
y=h_y+Y,\quad Y=\sum_{j=1}^s\beta_jc_j.
\]

The only degree-4 part of the displayed S3 polynomial is

\[
X^2Y^2.
\]

The terms with r have degrees at most 3, and the remaining A,B terms have degrees at most 2 or 1. Hence the original and raw substituted S3 polynomials have ordinary degree 4 in their respective polynomial rings. The disjoint coordinate variables and nonzero weights ensure X and Y are nonzero linear forms.

In the grouped algebra C write

\[
X=\sum_{g\text{ in }x}X_g,\quad X_g\in V_g,\qquad
Y=\sum_{g\text{ in }y}Y_g,\quad Y_g\in V_g.
\]

For a word a in group g, the coefficient in X_g is the sum of the original weights selected by a. This sum can vanish; the proof never assumes every word weight is nonzero. Nevertheless X_g is nonzero, because a unit word selects a single nonzero original weight. The same holds for Y_g. Since each V_g has square zero,

\[
X^2=2\sum_{g<h\text{ in }x}X_gX_h,\qquad
Y^2=2\sum_{g<h\text{ in }y}Y_gY_h.
\]

The raw full highest-part quotient is exactly \(C/(X^2Y^2)\).

To prove its top degree G survives for paired groups, construct a further graded quotient. In every width-2 group replace V_g by \(V_g/kX_g\) for an x group, or \(V_g/kY_g\) for a y group. This leaves a nonzero vector space of dimension 2. Leave every width-1 group's one-dimensional space unchanged. Such a replacement is a graded algebra quotient of k⊕V_g because all products inside V_g vanish.

Each coordinate has at most one width-1 group. In this further quotient its linear form is therefore supported on at most one square-zero group; its square is zero. Thus X^2Y^2 maps to zero. Every group still has a nonzero degree-1 space, so the further quotient still has a nonzero degree-G tensor product. It is a quotient of \(C/(X^2Y^2)\), proving nonvanishing there in degree G.

Combining both directions gives the exact raw-list value

\[
\boxed{d_{\rm reg}(\mathcal F_{\rm grouped,raw})=G+1
=2\lceil s/2\rceil+1.}
\]

This is a proof for this S3 generating list and this grouping, not for arbitrary full systems. It is unaffected by lower coefficients or by whether the affine solution set is empty.

### Raw S3 and locally reduced S3 are separately checked

Affine reduction by J replaces same-group powers and products, giving the unique normal form with at most one indicator per group. This preserves the affine ideal after the explicit local equations are included, but can change the highest part of the extra generator. That possibility cannot be ignored.

For s>=3, each coordinate has at least two groups. The degree-4 normal component is the class of X^2Y^2 above, and it is nonzero: select two distinct x groups and two distinct y groups, and in each select a unit-word indicator with nonzero original coefficient. Their four-indicator monomial has coefficient 4 times the product of those four nonzero coefficients. It is nonzero since p>3. Thus the locally reduced S3 still has degree 4, and its degree-4 part differs from raw X^2Y^2 only by an element of L. The full highest-part ideals, and hence the exact thresholds, are the same.

For s=2, each coordinate is one width-2 group. Now raw X^2Y^2 belongs to L, so the raw full highest-part quotient is C itself, with Hilbert series (1+3t)^2 and threshold 3. The locally reduced S3 has degree at most 2. Its quadratic component is nonzero, as follows.

For nonzero weights a,b in characteristic different from 2, the set \(\{0,a,b,a+b\}\) contains at least three distinct elements. Hence the coordinate value sets each contain at least three elements. If all mixed coefficients of the reduced polynomial were zero, S3 on this Cartesian grid would be a sum of a function of x and a function of y. Subtract its restrictions at the two base coordinates. The resulting polynomial T(x,y) has degree at most 2 in each variable and vanishes on a grid with at least three distinct values in each coordinate. Univariate interpolation twice forces T to be identically zero. Its x^2y^2 coefficient is 1, a contradiction.

Thus the reduced polynomial has a nonzero bilinear degree-2 component. The degree-2 space of C has dimension 3·3=9. One nonzero quadratic generator removes exactly a one-dimensional subspace there; no degree beyond 2 survives even locally. The reduced full quotient has degree-2 dimension 8 and threshold 3. Therefore

\[
\boxed{d_{\rm reg}(\mathcal F_{\rm grouped,reduced})
=2\lceil s/2\rceil+1\quad(s\geq2).}
\]

The equality of the two thresholds is established by these separate arguments, rather than inferred from equality of affine ideals. Their maximum input degrees differ when s=2.

## Joint baseline_comparison: holds under the explicit hypotheses

The original highest-part quotient is

\[
D=A/(X^2Y^2),\qquad
A=A_x\otimes_k A_y,
\]

where

\[
A_x=k[b_1,\ldots,b_s]/(b_1^2,\ldots,b_s^2),\qquad
A_y=k[c_1,\ldots,c_s]/(c_1^2,\ldots,c_s^2).
\]

It has a natural bidegree. The dimension of degree i of either coordinate algebra is \(h_i=\binom{s}{i}\), with binomial coefficients outside 0,...,s interpreted as zero.

Phuong–Tran Theorem 2.7 gives the strong Lefschetz property for the sum of variables in one s-variable square-zero algebra when the characteristic is zero or greater than s. A diagonal change of variables carries that sum to X because every alpha_j is nonzero; the square-zero ideal is preserved. The same argument applies separately to Y. The brief's p>s is therefore sufficient. No theorem for the full 2s-variable tensor algebra, and no stronger hypothesis p>2s, is being invoked.

In each coordinate, multiplication by the square of its weighted linear form has maximal rank. Consequently its rank from degree i-2 to degree i is

\[
r_i=\min\left\{\binom{s}{i-2},\binom{s}{i}\right\}.
\]

Multiplication by X^2Y^2 in bidegree (i,j) is the tensor product of those two maps, so it has rank r_i r_j. Thus the exact bidegree Hilbert function of D is

\[
\dim_k D_{i,j}=h_i h_j-r_i r_j\qquad(0\leq i,j\leq s).
\]

Put \(c=\lceil s/2\rceil\). For 0<=i<=s,

\[
r_i=h_i\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad i\geq c+1.
\]

Indeed, for i>=2 compare \(i(i-1)\) with \((s-i+2)(s-i+1)\); equality or the former being larger occurs precisely for \(i\geq\lceil(s+2)/2\rceil=c+1\). At i=0,1 the map has zero source and is not surjective. Therefore D_{i,j} is zero exactly when both coordinate maps are surjective.

If i+j>s+c, both i and j must exceed c because each is at most s. Every bidegree of that total degree is consequently zero. On the other hand,

\[
\dim_k D_{c,s}=(h_c-r_c)h_s>0,
\]

using s>=2 so that r_s=h_s=1. This supplies the required nonvanishing at total degree s+c, not merely a vanishing upper bound. Hence

\[
\boxed{d_{\rm reg}(\mathcal F_{\rm bit})=s+\lceil s/2\rceil+1.}
\]

With s=2q+e, the original and grouped thresholds are respectively \(3q+2e+1\) and \(2q+2e+1\); their difference is q=floor(s/2)>0.

This is a comparison of specified generating-system invariants in different polynomial rings. The affine Boolean solution problem is isomorphic through the proved reconstruction maps. The number of variables and representation have changed. No special curve, membership table, target ordinate, or curve-specific scalar rule was used in this proof: the leading term of the supplied S3 expression and the field/weight hypotheses suffice. Nonsingularity and t!=0 remain assumptions of the intended elliptic setting; no additional elliptic equivalence or point-membership theorem is claimed here.

## Joint solving_degree_transfer: holds for the precise convention

Salizzoni Definition 2.5 uses a degree-compatible monomial order sigma and the least d for which the closure space V_{F,d} contains a Gröbner basis. The closure initially contains input polynomials of degree at most d and is closed under linear combinations and monomial multiplication whenever the resulting polynomial still has degree at most d. Newly obtained lower-degree polynomials may be multiplied again. This is the mutant-closure convention relevant to MutantXL/MXL2-type algorithms; it is more than the row span of only the original-generator multiples in one plain truncated matrix.

Proposition 3.10, read with its proof, gives

\[
\operatorname{sd}_{\sigma}(\mathcal F)
\leq\max\{d_{\rm reg}(\mathcal F)+1,\ \max_{f\in\mathcal F}\deg f\}.
\]

The explicit maximum-input-degree term matters when the threshold is below an input degree. This source result, with the defined closure and degree-compatible order, is the entire transfer used here. It is not a universal statement for every implementation called XL or every Macaulay truncation.

For an arbitrary grouped raw list with maximum input degree D_max, the local argument gives

\[
\operatorname{sd}_\sigma(\mathcal F_{\rm grouped})
\leq\max\{G+2,D_{\max}\}.
\]

Here D_max includes the degree-2 local generators. If all substituted additional constraints have original degree at most d, it is at most max(2,d). Reducing them modulo J cannot increase their ordinary degree, but changing to auxiliary circuit equations requires a new argument.

For the paired raw S3 list, D_max=4 for every s>=2. Hence

\[
\operatorname{sd}_\sigma(\mathcal F_{\rm grouped,raw})
\leq\max\{2\lceil s/2\rceil+2,4\}
=2\lceil s/2\rceil+2.
\]

At s=2 the actual raw d_reg is 3, less than its quartic input degree 4; Theorem 1.1 alone would not meet its input-degree hypothesis. Proposition 3.10 gives the displayed bound 4. For s>=3 the grouped threshold is at least 5 and all inputs have degree at most that threshold.

For the locally reduced list, D_max=2 when s=2 and D_max=4 when s>=3, as proved above. The same uniform upper bound follows:

\[
\operatorname{sd}_\sigma(\mathcal F_{\rm grouped,reduced})
\leq2\lceil s/2\rceil+2.
\]

For the original list, D_max=4 and its threshold is at least 4, so

\[
\operatorname{sd}_\sigma(\mathcal F_{\rm bit})
\leq s+\lceil s/2\rceil+2.
\]

These upper bounds differ by floor(s/2). They do not order the actual solving degrees: a lower bound on the original solving degree matching or exceeding the grouped bound has not been proved. No ordinary truncated-Macaulay equality, lower bound on runtime, or measured computational advantage follows.

## Joint cost_and_scope: holds for the accounted statement

For an actual width-w group, the variable count is 2^w-1 and the local equation count is \(2^{w-1}(2^w-1)\). Uniform fixed width therefore gives counts linear in B, with constants depending exponentially on w. If w grows, the assertion of uniformly linear size fails. A single group containing all B bits has a small degree threshold but already has 2^B-1 indicator variables and on the order of 4^B explicit local constraints.

The bit projection has exactly \(w2^{w-1}\) coefficient occurrences per width-w group. A dense indicator assignment can be decoded by scanning its 2^w-1 entries and writing w bits. From a Boolean word the active label is obtained directly, but writing a dense output indicator vector still costs on the order of 2^w entries. The inverse polynomials delta_a have degree w. Their existence proves algebraic equivalence, without making their expanded use free.

Let N_x,N_y be the respective grouped variable counts. The raw product X^2Y^2 has at most

\[
\binom{N_x+1}{2}\binom{N_y+1}{2}
\]

expanded quartic monomials before coefficient cancellations, with lower-degree terms also charged. This is an O(N_x^2 N_y^2) representation bound. A straight-line expression can store the same polynomial compactly, but replacing that expression by gate variables changes the generating list and does not inherit the proved regularity threshold automatically.

A plain degree-D matrix in N polynomial variables can have \(\binom{N+D}{D}\) columns for monomials up to degree D, or \(\binom{N+D-1}{D}\) in homogeneous degree D. Even when working in the local highest-part quotient, the degree-d standard-monomial count is the coefficient of t^d in \(\prod_g(1+q_gt)\), and its total dimension remains 2^B. The affine product-of-fields quotient also has that dimension before the extra constraints. These formulas do not specify the actual closure algorithm, row counts, fill, sparsity, rank cost, memory, or preprocessing needed to solve the full system.

For the paired specialization, the variable count increases from 2s to 3s-e while the proved degree-of-regularity threshold decreases. Both facts must be carried into any future complexity analysis. Finite-field arithmetic, construction and reconstruction, matrices, auxiliary encoding, and actual solution extraction would all need matched accounting. This review establishes no numerical timing, asymptotic solver advantage, novelty claim, efficient general algorithm, scalar recovery, or general prime-field ECDLP speedup.
