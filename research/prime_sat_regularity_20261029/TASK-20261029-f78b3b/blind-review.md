## Source contract and invariant

Provenance of all three literature citations is `retrieved`; verified reading was performed by this reviewer in this task.

[Caminata–Gorla, Definition 1.4](https://arxiv.org/html/2112.05579) defines the generating-list degree used here through vanishing of the quotient by the ideal generated by the individual highest homogeneous parts. Accordingly, for a finite explicit list F in a polynomial ring P, omit identically zero entries and set

\[
 J_F=(f^{\rm top}:f\in F),\qquad
 d_{\rm reg}(F)=\min\{d\geq0:(P/J_F)_d=0\}.
\]

The quotients below vanish in all sufficiently large degrees, so this minimum exists. This is not a first fall degree, last fall degree, regularity of the affine quotient, or an assertion about Castelnuovo–Mumford regularity of a homogenized system. Equal affine ideals can give different J_F when their generating lists differ.

[Phuong–Tran, Theorem 2.7](https://arxiv.org/html/2211.13548) gives maximal rank for every power of the sum of the variables on the square-zero algebra on s variables when the characteristic is zero or greater than s. In applying it below, each coordinate block has s variables; nonzero weights are accommodated by a diagonal algebra automorphism. No strong Lefschetz assertion for the tensor product on 2s variables is needed.

[Salizzoni, Definition 2.5 and Proposition 3.10](https://arxiv.org/html/2304.13485) were read with their surrounding definitions and the full proposition proof. The relevant space V_(F,D) starts with input polynomials of degree at most D and repeatedly allows linear combinations and monomial multiplication of any derived polynomial whenever the product's degree stays at most D. For a degree-compatible order, the solving degree is the first D for which this space contains a Gröbner basis. The applicable proposition gives

\[
 \operatorname{sd}_\sigma(F)\leq
 \max\{d_{\rm reg}(F)+1,\max_{f\in F}\deg f\}.
\]

The maximum input degree remains necessary when the regularity threshold is smaller than an input degree. The statement concerns this closure convention, including the mutant variants discussed in the source.

## Joint 1: local_models — holds

Let one actual nonempty group have width w, m = 2^w - 1 nonzero words, and polynomial ring k[u_a : a != 0]. Its affine local ideal is

\[
 I_w=(u_a^2-u_a,\ u_a u_b\ (a<b)).
\]

Over any field, u_a(u_a-1)=0 forces u_a to be 0 or 1 at a solution. A pair of active indicators contradicts their product equation, since 1 is nonzero in a field. Thus the solutions are the all-zero vector and each of the m unit vectors: exactly 2^w possibilities. The all-zero vector encodes the zero word. No characteristic restriction is needed, including in characteristic two, and the same argument applies over every field extension.

There is a stronger algebra statement. Define e_a=u_a for a != 0 and e_0=1-sum_(a!=0) u_a. Modulo I_w these are orthogonal idempotents and sum to one. Consequently the local quotient is the product of 2^w copies of k. One may also prove this directly: every polynomial reduces to a linear combination of 1 and the u_a; evaluation at the m unit vectors and the zero vector proves these 1+m elements independent. This proves both the local algebra and absence of hidden nonreduced solutions.

Widths mean actual widths. A shortened final group of width one uses one indicator and one Boolean equation. There is no width-zero group in the stated partition. If there are no bits at all, take the empty tensor product k; adding formal empty groups would not create a new choice or a degree-one factor.

## Joint 2: bijection_and_ideals — holds

Write the bits as b_(g,j) and define

\[
 L_{g,j}=\sum_{a:a_j=1}u_{g,a},\qquad
 \delta_{g,a}(b)=\prod_{j:a_j=1}b_{g,j}
                 \prod_{j:a_j=0}(1-b_{g,j}).
\]

For a Boolean assignment, the deltas select its unique group word. The inverse encoding sets u_(g,a)=delta_(g,a) for each nonzero a, leaving all u zero for the zero word. Projection of a unit-vector indicator assignment returns its word, and projection of the zero vector returns zero. These are mutually inverse maps on assignments.

The same formulas prove the quotient-ring assertion without identifying ideals in different polynomial rings. Let

\[
 B=k[b]/(b_i^2-b_i),\qquad C=k[u]/I_{\rm loc},
 \quad I_{\rm loc}=\sum_g I_{w_g}.
\]

The map b_(g,j) -> L_(g,j) induces B -> C because L_(g,j)^2=L_(g,j) modulo I_loc. The delta formulas induce C -> B: their classes are Boolean orthogonal idempotents. On B the composition returns each b_(g,j), by the Boolean partition of unity. On C,

\[
 \delta_{g,a}(L_{g,1},\ldots,L_{g,w_g})=e_{g,a}
\]

for every word a, including e_(g,0)=1-sum u_(g,a). This follows by expanding each L in the orthogonal idempotent basis: a polynomial acts componentwise at the corresponding binary word. Thus the other composition returns every u_(g,a), proving B is isomorphic to C as k-algebras.

For arbitrary additional equations f_1,...,f_r in the original bits, this isomorphism carries the ideal generated by their classes to the ideal generated by f_i(L). Therefore

\[
 \frac{k[b]}{(b_i^2-b_i,f_1,\ldots,f_r)}
 \ \simeq\ 
 \frac{k[u]}{(I_{\rm loc},f_1(L),\ldots,f_r(L))}.
\]

The solution correspondence is exact over k and all extensions. The ambient ideals are in different rings and are not asserted equal. Nor does this isomorphism preserve the standard degree filtration: the forward projection is linear, while the inverse deltas can have degree w_g. Coordinate-value collisions in a weighted sum do not invalidate the assignment bijection; they only mean that several assignments may represent the same coordinate.

## Joint 3: representation_counts — holds

Let n=sum_g w_g, G be the number of actual nonempty groups, and m_g=2^(w_g)-1. Then the indicator representation has

\[
 M=\sum_g m_g\quad\hbox{variables},\qquad
 C=\sum_g\left(m_g+\binom{m_g}{2}\right)
   =\sum_g 2^{w_g-1}(2^{w_g}-1)
\]

local equations. The first m_g equations are Boolean; each unordered distinct pair contributes exactly one product equation. With r additional original equations the natural substituted list has C+r entries, counting the r supplied entries even if one later turns out redundant or zero. Zero polynomial entries are discarded when computing highest parts. The original list has n+r entries.

Projected original Boolean equations are implied by I_loc and need not be retained. If they are mechanically retained as a redundant part of the list, there are n additional entries. Their highest parts L_i^2 already lie in the local highest-part ideal, so they do not affect the degree calculations in this review.

For two coordinates, each with s bits grouped separately in pairs, write s=2q+epsilon with epsilon in {0,1}. There are

\[
 G=2(q+\epsilon)=2\lceil s/2\rceil,\quad
 M=6q+2\epsilon,\quad C=12q+2\epsilon.
\]

Thus:

| Actual bit count per coordinate | Variables M | Local equations C | With the one S3 constraint |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| s even | 3s | 6s | 6s+1 |
| s odd | 3s-1 | 6s-4 | 6s-3 |

For odd s there are two genuine singleton groups in total, one in each coordinate. A fictitious extra bit, an extra digit-zero indicator, or a constraint fixing padded bits would define another generating list and cannot be inserted into these counts without being charged and reanalyzed.

## Joint 4: hilbert_series — holds

The highest parts of the local equations generate the monomial ideal

\[
 K=\sum_g (u_{g,a}u_{g,b}:a,b\ne0),
\]

where a=b is included. Each factor is the square of its group's variable ideal. A monomial survives exactly when it takes at most one variable from each group, with exponent one. Distinct such monomials are independent because K is a monomial ideal. Hence

\[
 A=k[u]/K\simeq\bigotimes_g(k\oplus V_g),\qquad
 \dim V_g=m_g,\quad V_g^2=0,
\]

and

\[
 H_A(z)=\prod_g(1+m_gz),\qquad
 \dim_k A=\prod_g(1+m_g)=2^n.
\]

For G>0, degree G has dimension product_g m_g>0, while every degree above G vanishes. Thus the exact local-list threshold is G+1. The same convention gives threshold one for the empty local list in the zero-variable ring k.

For a full substituted list F, the relevant highest-part ideal is J_F containing K. The quotient k[u]/J_F is a further quotient of A; its Hilbert function can decrease in every degree. The displayed product is the Hilbert series of the local highest-part quotient alone, not generally the full system. Neither quotient's grading should be confused with the affine Boolean solution algebra established in Joint 2.

## Joint 5: regularity_bound — holds with explicit sharpness scope

For arbitrary substituted additional equations,

\[
 d_{\rm reg}(F)\leq G+1
\]

follows immediately from the surjection A -> k[u]/J_F. This is an upper bound for arbitrary full lists, not a universal equality. For example, an added constant-one equation makes J_F the unit ideal and d_reg(F)=0. It is nevertheless a sharp general bound, since a list with no effective additional equation has the local value.

For the brief's paired S3 list, equality can be proved without a genericity assumption. Use

\[
 x=h_x+X,\quad y=h_y+Y,\quad
 X=\sum_{g\in\mathcal G_x}X_g,\quad
 Y=\sum_{g\in\mathcal G_y}Y_g,
\]

where X_g=sum_(a!=0) (sum_j alpha_j a_j)u_(g,a), with the analogous Y_g. Each X_g and Y_g is nonzero because a singleton word has the corresponding nonzero original weight as coefficient. There are a=ceil(s/2) groups per coordinate. In the ordinary polynomial ring the raw S3 equation has degree four and highest part X^2 Y^2. Terms involving h_x,h_y,A,B,r have smaller degree.

In A, within-group products vanish, so

\[
 X^2=2\sum_{g<h\in\mathcal G_x}X_gX_h,
 \qquad Y^2=2\sum_{g<h\in\mathcal G_y}Y_gY_h.
\]

For every width-two x-group choose a nonzero linear functional lambda_g on its three-dimensional V_g which annihilates X_g. Such a functional exists even if some sums of original weights vanish. On a possible singleton group choose any nonzero functional. There is at most one singleton per coordinate. Therefore every distinct pair of x-groups contains a width-two group, and the tensor-product functional on the top component A_x,a annihilates the degree-a image of multiplication by X^2. The assertion is also immediate when a=1, since X^2=0 in A_x.

Tensor this functional with any nonzero functional on the top y-component. It is a nonzero functional on A_G, G=2a, and annihilates every degree-G multiple of X^2Y^2. To see nonzero explicitly, in each group select a vector on which its functional is one; their product evaluates to one. Consequently

\[
 (A/(X^2Y^2))_G\ne0.
\]

The full raw list has no other highest-part generators beyond K and X^2Y^2 (apart from redundant projected Boolean ones). The local upper bound now gives the exact value

\[
 d_{\rm reg}(F_{\rm grouped,raw})=G+1=2a+1.
\]

This proves sharpness for every S3 instance satisfying the stated weights and pairing, not just an example or a dimension estimate.

### Raw S3 and locally reduced S3 are separate lists

The local affine equations admit a unique normal form consisting of monomials taking at most one indicator from each group: use u_a^2 -> u_a and u_a u_b -> 0 within a group. This normal form agrees with a polynomial function on the product of the group state sets. Replacing S3 by this normal form preserves the affine ideal in the same indicator ring, but does not in general preserve its highest-part ideal.

If s>=3, each coordinate has at least two groups. Reduction of the degree-four part retains

\[
 Q_4=4\left(\sum_{g<h\in\mathcal G_x}X_gX_h\right)
          \left(\sum_{g<h\in\mathcal G_y}Y_gY_h\right).
\]

This polynomial is nonzero: p>3, the individual group forms are nonzero, and different group supports cannot cancel each other. Reduction of lower-degree terms cannot contribute degree four. Thus the reduced S3 has actual degree four, and its highest part has the same class in A as the raw highest part. The two highest-part ideals agree after adjoining K. The preceding nonvanishing proof establishes d_reg=2a+1 for the reduced list as well.

If s=2, there is one three-dimensional indicator group for each coordinate. Every local normal-form polynomial has degree at most two. The reduced S3 actually has a nonzero mixed quadratic part. Here is a direct proof that does not assume this from raw degree four. Let f(x,y)=S3(x,y,r), u=x-h_x, v=y-h_y. The mixed difference is

\[
\begin{split}
 &f(h_x+u,h_y+v)-f(h_x+u,h_y)-f(h_x,h_y+v)+f(h_x,h_y)\\
 &=uv\big[(2h_x+u)(2h_y+v)
       -2r(2h_x+u+2h_y+v)-2(r^2+A)\big].
\end{split}
\]

The coefficient of u_(x,a)u_(y,b) in the normal form is precisely this difference for the corresponding nonzero word increments. Each set {alpha_1,alpha_2,alpha_1+alpha_2} and {beta_1,beta_2,beta_1+beta_2} has at least two distinct nonzero values when the weights are nonzero and p>3. Were all these mixed coefficients zero, the bracket, a bilinear polynomial with uv coefficient one, would vanish on a two-by-two grid of distinct nonzero increments. Its mixed difference on that grid is the nonzero product of the two increment differences, a contradiction.

Thus the reduced S3 has degree two. The top local component A_2 has dimension 3*3=9, and one quadratic generator spans only one dimension there. Degree two remains nonzero, while A_3=0. Hence its exact regularity threshold is three, agreeing numerically with the raw-list value. Its maximum input degree is two, whereas the raw list still has maximum input degree four.

This agreement is specific to the construction just proved. For a counterexample to any general affine-ideal-to-regularity transfer, in k[u,v] compare the lists

\[
 \{u^2-u,v^2-v,u^2v^2\},\qquad
 \{u^2-u,v^2-v,uv\}.
\]

They generate the same affine ideal, because u^2v^2-uv=(u^2-u)v^2+u(v^2-v). The first highest-part ideal is (u^2,v^2), with threshold three; the second is (u^2,uv,v^2), with threshold two.

## Joint 6: baseline_comparison — holds under the stated hypotheses

The original list is the 2s explicit Boolean equations and the one raw S3 equation. Its maximum input degree is four. Its highest-part quotient is

\[
 T=\frac{B_x\otimes_k B_y}{(\ell_x^2\ell_y^2)},\qquad
 B_x=k[b_1,\ldots,b_s]/(b_1^2,\ldots,b_s^2),
\]

with the analogous B_y and weighted forms ell_x=sum alpha_j b_j, ell_y=sum beta_j c_j. Put h_i=binom(s,i), with h_i=0 outside 0<=i<=s. Each coordinate algebra has dimension h_i in degree i.

Theorem 2.7 applies separately to B_x and B_y because p>s. Scaling each variable by its nonzero original weight is an automorphism of its square-zero algebra, so the weighted forms have the stated maximal-rank property. In degree i define

\[
 \mu_i:B_{x,i-2}\xrightarrow{\ \ell_x^2\ }B_{x,i},\qquad
 \rho_i=\operatorname{rank}\mu_i=\min\{h_{i-2},h_i\}.
\]

The y-block has the same ranks. On bidegree (i,j), multiplication by the product ell_x^2 ell_y^2 is the tensor product of these two maps. Hence

\[
 \dim_k T_{i,j}=h_i h_j-\rho_i\rho_j.
\]

This calculation uses tensor products of the already established maps; it does not invoke a Lefschetz theorem requiring characteristic greater than 2s.

For 0<=i<=s the comparison of binomial coefficients shows

\[
 \mu_i\text{ is surjective}\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 i\geq\lceil s/2\rceil+1=a+1.
\]

For i=0 or 1 the source is zero; for i>=2 the inequality h_(i-2)>=h_i is equivalent to i>=(s+2)/2, with the appropriate integer rounding, giving exactly this cutoff. Therefore a bidegree with 0<=i,j<=s survives precisely when i<=a or j<=a. The largest possible total degree of such a bidegree is s+a.

Both sides of the threshold are proved. Every total degree greater than s+a vanishes, since any valid pair then has i>a and j>a. Bidegree (a,s) is nonzero because rho_a<h_a and rho_s=h_s=1, so its quotient dimension h_a-rho_a is positive. Consequently

\[
 d_{\rm reg}(F_{\rm bit})=s+a+1.
\]

For the paired indicator lists, Joint 5 gives 2a+1. Their exact difference is s-a=floor(s/2)>0 for s>=2. This is a strict comparison of these specified generating-list invariants for the same affine assignment problem after reparameterization, with different numbers of variables and local equations.

The lower coefficients of S3, the coordinate offsets, nonsingularity and the target's nonzero ordinate do not enter this highest-part computation. Nonsingularity and the target assumptions retain the intended elliptic interpretation. The degree proof uses the stated characteristic, separate coordinate blocks and nonzero original weights; it does not require a special curve, an injective coordinate encoding or a membership-table hypothesis.

## Joint 7: solving_degree_transfer — holds in the specified convention

For any full indicator list with actual maximum input degree d_max, the local bound and Proposition 3.10 give

\[
 \operatorname{sd}_\sigma(F)\leq\max\{G+2,d_{\max}\}.
\]

For the raw paired S3 list, d_max=4 and d_reg=2a+1, so

\[
 \operatorname{sd}_\sigma(F_{\rm grouped,raw})
 \leq\max\{2a+2,4\}=2a+2,
 \qquad s\geq2.
\]

At s=2, d_reg=3<4=d_max. Applying only the special theorem whose hypothesis is d_max<=d_reg would be invalid. The full proposition supplies the bound four. For s>=3 the maximum input degree four is at most d_reg. For the locally reduced list, the actual maximum input degree is two at s=2 and four for s>=3; the same upper bound 2a+2 follows. These statements cover all small-width boundaries in the stated domain s>=2.

For the original list, d_max=4 and d_reg=s+a+1>=4, so the same proposition yields

\[
 \operatorname{sd}_\sigma(F_{\rm bit})\leq s+a+2.
\]

The numerical upper bounds strictly differ by floor(s/2). There is no lower bound on the original actual solving degree in this argument that would make the grouped actual solving degree strictly smaller. Nor does the threshold equality for highest-part quotients supply one.

The degree-compatible term order and closure V_(F,D) are material assumptions. One fixed Macaulay matrix formed solely from monomial multiples of the raw inputs need not have this row space. For example, take F={x^2+y,x^2+x} and D=2. The difference y-x lies in V_(F,2), and multiplying that derived linear polynomial by y puts y^2-xy in V_(F,2). The raw degree-two Macaulay rows contain only the two original quadratics, whose span cannot contain a y^2 term. Thus a claim for every plain truncation, arbitrary term order or unspecified solver would exceed the cited result.

## Joint 8: cost_and_scope — holds with no performance conclusion

For uniformly fixed width w, M=G(2^w-1), C=G*2^(w-1)(2^w-1), and n=Gw, up to the actual shortened group. Variables and local constraints are linear in n only with constants depending on a fixed bound on w. If w grows, these are exponential in w; one group containing all n bits uses 2^n-1 indicators and 2^(n-1)(2^n-1) local equations. Its small formal threshold does not make its representation cheap.

The literal bit projections contain sum_g w_g*2^(w_g-1) nonzero summands before combining coefficients. Forward encoding of a known Boolean assignment identifies one word per group and activates its indicator, or none for zero. Reading a dense indicator representation and decoding selected word labels costs O(M+n); a sparse representation can identify the active groups and decode in O(G+n) after its own indexing and storage costs. The symbolic inverse deltas have degree w_g and can be evaluated by product circuits, but their expanded forms and total listing costs must be charged. These reconstruction facts do not reveal unknown bits or scalars.

With M_x and M_y indicator variables per coordinate, raw expansion of the quartic has at most O(M_x^2 M_y^2) degree-four terms, plus lower-degree terms. A constant-size outer S3 arithmetic circuit following the two linear forms is much smaller than a generic expanded quartic. These are different representation costs. Even with fixed width, the whole expanded polynomial system is not uniformly linear in n merely because its variable and local-equation counts are linear. Replacing a compact circuit by auxiliary variables introduces a new graded problem requiring its own proof.

At degree cap D, ordinary polynomial columns number

\[
 \binom{M+D}{D}
\]

for all monomials of degree at most D (or binom(M+D-1,D) in one homogeneous degree). The initial raw rows can number sum_(f in F,deg f<=D) binom(M+D-deg f,D-deg f). Closure can add derived rows. Exploiting the local normal basis changes these dimensions only if an actual algorithm is proved to do so: the number of normal monomials through D is

\[
 \sum_{j\leq D}[z^j]\prod_g(1+m_gz),
\]

which saturates at 2^n. In the two-coordinate case this full local dimension is 2^(2s), unchanged by the assignment recoding. Lower degree and more variables interact in matrix sizes; neither the degree gap nor the displayed column formula is a measured resource comparison.

No efficient end-to-end algorithm, actual solver speedup, novelty assertion, scalar recovery or general prime-field ECDLP advantage is established. No numerical measurements are supplied or implied. The unchanged affine problem is the explicit Boolean S3 constraint problem described in the brief.

## Known-false controls

All seven controls are resolved by identifying the hypothesis or conclusion that fails; none is silently passed under the original conclusion.

1. **Remove pairwise products.** For a width-two group, three independent Boolean indicators have eight assignments, whereas the group has four words. Multi-indicator assignments remain, and projections need not be Boolean outside characteristic two; even there the mapping is not bijective. In the highest quotient only the individual squares are killed, so same-group products such as u_(10)u_(01) survive in degree two. The claimed one-group threshold two fails: the quotient has Hilbert series (1+z)^3 and threshold four. Width one has no pair to remove and is not a counterexample to this control.
2. **Require a nonzero indicator without digit zero.** Together with the original local relations, an equation sum u_a=1 removes exactly the all-zero vector. The zero word has no image, so the correspondence with all Boolean assignments breaks. A designated zero indicator with an appropriately revised list would be another encoding requiring its own count and graded analysis.
3. **Introduce arbitrary circuit auxiliaries.** Even a uniquely reconstructible auxiliary can spoil the highest-part argument. From u^2-u=0 introduce z=u^2, giving z-u^2=0. The new affine quotient still determines z from u, but its highest-part ideal in k[u,z] is (u^2); all z powers survive and the regularity threshold is infinite. Thus the old threshold two does not transfer without additional graded reasoning.
4. **Use small characteristic or a zero original weight.** The encoding and local bound remain valid over every field, but the cited weighted Lefschetz baseline loses its justification. In characteristic two with s=2, ell_x^2=0 in the square-zero block, so the full original highest-part constraint adds nothing and the threshold is five, instead of the stated formula four. In characteristic greater than three, still with s=2 but alpha_2=0, the same vanishing ell_x^2=0 occurs and again gives threshold five. Small characteristic does not always force failure; it requires a renewed rank proof rather than automatic use of the sufficient hypothesis p>s.
5. **Add constant one.** The full affine system is inconsistent, the full highest-part ideal is the unit ideal and its degree threshold is zero. The local quotient still has threshold G+1. This explicitly refutes universal equality between local and full values; the upper bound survives.
6. **Let group width grow.** The exact exponential formulas for M and C above replace any fixed-width linear-size statement. The degree bound is not invalidated, but an uncharged uniformly linear representation or efficient-algorithm conclusion breaks.
7. **Promote the solving-degree bound to every plain truncation or to runtime.** Definition 2.5 requires iterative closure and a degree-compatible order. The explicit two-quadratic example in Joint 7 separates that closure from a plain raw-row truncation. More generally, neither two upper bounds on actual solving degrees nor a smaller degree threshold establishes strict actual solver performance or an ECDLP cost advantage.
