## 1. `definition_and_generators`: holds

I checked the producer's convention against [Caminata–Gorla, arXiv:2112.05579v2, Definition 1.4](https://arxiv.org/html/2112.05579v2). It takes the least degree in which the ideal generated by the *listed generators' highest homogeneous parts* fills the entire homogeneous component. This is the convention the proof uses; it is not a replacement by all highest parts of all affine-ideal elements.

For clarity, reconstruct the actual objects. Let `k=F_p`,
\[
 S=k[z_{11},\ldots,z_{1s},z_{21},\ldots,z_{2s}],\quad
 x_i=h_i+L_i,\quad L_i=\sum_jw_{ij}z_{ij},
\]
with `w_ij != 0`, and let `E:y^2=x^3+Ax+B`, `4A^3+27B^2 != 0`, and `R=(r,t)` with `t != 0`. Define
\[
 G(X,Y,r)=(X-Y)^2r^2
 -2\big((X+Y)(XY+A)+2B\big)r
 +(XY-A)^2-4B(X+Y),
\]
\[
 J_B=(z_{ij}^2-z_{ij}:i=1,2,\ j=1,\ldots,s),\quad F=G(x_1,x_2,r).
\]
The two actual generator lists are
\[
 \mathcal F_0=\{z_{ij}^2-z_{ij}\}_{i,j}\cup\{F\},\qquad
 \mathcal F_1=\mathcal F_0\cup\{M_1,M_2\}.
\]
Here `M_i` is the unique multilinear interpolation of the exact rejection function: value one for a nonsquare cubic value, zero for square values including zero. A zero `M_i` can be omitted; a constant-one `M_i` is a unit generator.

The baseline listed-highest-part ideal is `(z_ij^2,L_1^2 L_2^2)`. It is Artinian because of the variable squares. Its first vanishing degree is therefore one plus its largest nonzero degree, whereas the explicit-unit case has degree of regularity zero. The producer keeps this distinction. Equality of the affine ideals established later does not require equality of their listed-highest-part ideals and does not make this invariant depend only on the common affine ideal.

## 2. `block_lefschetz_and_characteristic`: holds

I checked [Phuong–Tran, arXiv:2211.13548, Theorem 2.7](https://arxiv.org/html/2211.13548). It gives the required maximal ranks for the sum of the variables in one square-zero block in characteristic zero or characteristic greater than that block's size `s`. The diagonal automorphism `z_j -> w_j z_j` is valid when all weights are nonzero and transports this property to `L=sum w_j z_j`. This scaling is performed in the graded square-zero algebra, not on the affine Boolean equations.

Writing `c_d=binom(s,d)` and setting it to zero outside `0,...,s`, multiplication by `L^e` into degree `d` has rank
\[
 r_d=\min(c_{d-e},c_d).
\]
This agrees with the producer's map and rank orientation. Only the individual block theorem is used. No hypothesis `p>ms` is needed for the tensor argument. In the elliptic application, `s>=2` and `2^s<=p` imply `p>s`, so the stronger window-size premise supplies the needed characteristic condition.

Controls: in characteristic two and `s=2`, `(z_1+z_2)^2=0` in the square-zero algebra, so maximal rank from degree zero to degree two fails. With zero weight, for example `L=z_1` in a two-variable block, `L^2=0` likewise fails. These objects violate the actual hypotheses and do not receive the theorem. The diagonal scaling must not be interpreted as preserving `z^2-z`; the producer explicitly avoids that interpretation.

## 3. `tensor_exact_threshold`: holds

Put `a=ceil((s+e)/2)` and `t_0=a-1`. For target multidegree `(d_1,...,d_m)` within the block ranges, multiplication by `P=product_i L_i^e` has image rank `product_i r_(d_i)`. Thus
\[
 \dim(B/(P))_{\boldsymbol d}
   =\prod_i c_{d_i}-\prod_i r_{d_i}.
 \tag{R1}
\]
Summing over block degrees gives exactly the producer's Hilbert series
\[
 (1+T)^{ms}-\left(\sum_{d=0}^s r_dT^d\right)^m.
\]
There is no missing degree shift: `r_d` is indexed by the *target* degree.

For `d>=e`, binomial symmetry and increase toward the middle give
\[
 c_{d-e}\ge c_d\iff 2d\ge s+e.
\]
Below `e` the source is zero. Equivalently, compare squared distances to `s/2`: their difference is `e(s+e-2d)`. This handles the equal central pair for odd `s` without requiring a nonexistent strict inequality there. It follows that the block map is onto precisely for `d>=a`.

In (R1), equality of image and ambient dimensions occurs if and only if every block map is onto. A surviving component therefore has some `d_i<=a-1`, so its total degree is at most
\[
 D=(m-1)s+a-1.
\]
Conversely the component `(a-1,s,...,s)` has positive dimension
\[
 c_{a-1}-c_{a-1-e}>0,
\]
because the maps into each of the other block-top components have rank one. This proves both complete vanishing above `D` and nonvanishing in degree `D`. It agrees with the independently archived blind derivation.

Hence the first vanishing degree is exactly
\[
 (m-1)s+\left\lceil\frac{s+e}{2}\right\rceil.
\]
If `s+e` is even, the cutoff block map has equal source and target dimensions; if odd, the ceiling moves the cutoff to the next integer. At `e=1` the cutoff is `ceil((s+1)/2)`. At `e=s`, `P` is a nonzero scalar times the full top monomial, so the quotient's last nonzero degree is `ms-1`, as the formula says. At `s=e=1`, the last degree is `m-1`. Although the producer states `m>=2`, the baseline algebra argument also works for `m=1`; its strict-augmentation consequence need not.

Controls outside the range: at `e=0`, `P=1` and the quotient is zero; at `e>s`, each block power vanishes and `P=0`, leaving last degree `ms`. These do not contradict the formula, whose stated range excludes them.

## 4. `membership_top_degree_kill`: holds

For nonzero homogeneous squarefree block form `h` of degree `delta` in `1,...,s`, choose a support `S_0` with nonzero coefficient `c`. Multiplying by its complementary squarefree monomial gives exactly
\[
 h z_{S_0^c}=c\,\Omega,\qquad \Omega=z_1\cdots z_s.
\]
Any different support of the same size meets the complement and vanishes after a variable is squared. Thus `(Omega)` is contained in `(h)`. The direction of the quotient comparison in the producer is correct:
\[
 B/(P,h_1,\ldots,h_m)
 \text{ is a quotient of }
 Q=B/(P,\Omega_1,\ldots,\Omega_m).
\]

In `Q`, target block degrees range only up to `s-1`. In these degrees the original block map has unchanged source and target, because the source degree `d-e` is also below `s`. Also `r_s=1`. Therefore the producer's exact Hilbert series for `Q` is correct:
\[
 \big((1+T)^s-T^s\big)^m
 -\left(\sum_{d=0}^s r_dT^d-T^s\right)^m.
\]
At least one deficient block must have degree at most `a-1`; all other blocks have degree at most `s-1`. The maximal total surviving degree is exactly `(m-1)(s-1)+a-1`. Nonvanishing is witnessed by `(a-1,s-1,...,s-1)`: its dimension is
\[
 c_{a-1}s^{m-1}-r_{a-1}r_{s-1}^{m-1}>0.
\]
The first term is strictly larger because `c_(a-1)>r_(a-1)` and every other factor satisfies `s>=r_(s-1)`.

Consequently
\[
 d_{\rm reg}(\text{augmented})
 \le(m-1)(s-1)+a
 =d_{\rm reg}(\text{baseline})-(m-1).
\]
This agrees with the blind derivation, which used descending surjectivity on the block quotients instead. Equality for all `h_i` scalar multiples of `Omega_i` is also correct; arbitrary `h_i` may yield a smaller value. No universal exact augmented value is claimed.

A nonzero constant gives the zero quotient and `d_reg=0`; it is handled separately. A zero predicate does not kill `Omega`. In particular the two-block strict bound cannot be asserted unconditionally when one block's predicate vanishes identically. These exceptions appear in both the proof and its machine-readable status.

## 5. `s3_top_and_affine_equivalence`: holds

I checked the polynomial definition and algebraic-closure equivalence in [Kosters–Yeo, arXiv:1503.08001v3, Section 2 and Proposition 2.1](https://arxiv.org/html/1503.08001). Specializing its coefficients to the short-Weierstrass model gives `b_2=0`, `b_4=2A`, `b_6=4B`, `b_8=-A^2`, and gives exactly `G` displayed above. That proposition alone would not prove individual rational membership. The producer supplies the necessary separate lift, which I also derived before reveal.

The quartic term after substitution is `L_1^2 L_2^2`; all other terms have total degree at most three in the bits. Modulo the variable squares, each block square contributes `2 sum_(j<k) w_ij w_ik z_ij z_ik`. It is nonzero for `s>=2` and `p>3`. Boolean multilinear reduction lowers the degrees of terms with repeated variables; its quartic part differs from the original by elements of the square ideal. Thus it leaves the listed-highest-part ideal (including the variable squares) unchanged. The exact baseline is
\[
 d_{\rm reg}(\mathcal F_0)
   =s+\left\lceil\frac{s+2}{2}\right\rceil
   =1+s+\left\lfloor\frac{s+1}{2}\right\rfloor.
\]
For nonzero nonconstant predicates in both blocks, their highest parts satisfy the preceding block lemma, giving
\[
 d_{\rm reg}(\mathcal F_1)
   \le s+\left\lfloor\frac{s+1}{2}\right\rfloor.
\]
Constant-one predicates instead give value zero. For `s=2u` the two values/bounds are `3u+1` and `3u`; for `s=2u+1` they are `3u+3` and `3u+2`. Both producer floor/ceiling rewritings are correct.

For the affine step, put
\[
 U=x_1+x_2,\quad V=x_1x_2,\quad N=V-rU-2r^2-A.
\]
Direct expansion using `t^2=r^3+Ar+B` gives
\[
 G(x_1,x_2,r)=N^2-4t^2(U+r).
\]
On `G=0`, the rational slope `lambda=N/(2t)` satisfies `lambda^2=U+r`. Then
\[
 X^3+AX+B-[\lambda(X-r)-t]^2
  =(X-r)(X-x_1)(X-x_2).
\]
The `X^2` coefficient follows from `lambda^2=U+r`, the `X` coefficient from `2t lambda=N`, and the constant follows by the root `X=r`. This verifies the signs, including the `-2r^2` in `N` and the line through `-R`.

It follows that both points `(x_i,lambda(x_i-r)-t)` have rational ordinates and lie on the curve. The nonvertical line meets the smooth cubic with multiplicities at those points and `-R`, so their sum is `R`. Equal abscissas, equality with `r`, and a triple intersection at `-R` are handled by the same factorization and tangent multiplicities. The proof does not divide by `x_1-x_2` or `x_i-r`. Conversely, two finite points summing to finite `R` give a line through `-R` with those multiplicities and hence `G=0`; a vertical line would give the identity target instead.

Thus all Boolean zeros of `F` already have zero rejection predicates. Because
\[
 S/J_B\simeq\prod_{u\in\{0,1\}^{2s}}\mathbb F_p,
\]
functions vanishing on the zeros of `F` belong to `(F)` in that quotient. The producer's explicit identity
\[
 M_i=M_i F^{p-1}=(M_iF^{p-2})F\pmod{J_B}
\]
is valid: at nonzero `F` it is Fermat's identity, and at a zero of `F` both sides vanish. Equality of Boolean evaluations is equality in the displayed split reduced algebra. Hence the two affine ideals, and not merely their zero sets or radicals, are equal.

A known-false control outside the target assumption is `E/F_5:y^2=x^3+x`, `R=(0,0)`: the S3 equation is `(x_1x_2-1)^2=0`, which holds at `x_1=x_2=1`, but cubic value `2` is nonsquare. This is an exact counterexample to removing the nonzero-ordinate premise. Singular curves and characteristics 2/3 do not inherit this proof.

A higher-arity control is also explicit. Take a rejected abscissa `a` and a nonrational point `P=(a,alpha)` over the quadratic extension. Then `P+(-P)+R=R` gives a three-summand decomposition with rational abscissas but two invalid rational-membership coordinates. Thus the algebra-only tensor lemma cannot justify arbitrary higher-summand augmentation. The producer rejects that extension rather than asserting it.

## 6. `nonzero_membership_window_bridge`: holds

For odd group order, rational points of ordinate zero cannot occur because they would have order two. With `n_E=#E(F_p)`, all cubic values have character `+1` or `-1`, so exactly
\[
 U_-=p-\frac{n_E-1}{2}=\frac{2p+1-n_E}{2}
\]
abscissas are rejected. I retrieved and checked [Sutherland, MIT 18.783 Lecture 7 (2023), Theorem 7.3](https://math.mit.edu/classes/18.783/2023/LectureNotes7.pdf), including its proof on pages 1–2. Its Hasse estimate implies
\[
 U_-\ge p/2-\sqrt p>0\qquad(p\ge5).
\]
The point-count identity is derived here; the Hasse inequality is the retrieved external theorem. This fills the literature-verification limitation explicitly disclosed in the blind artifact without altering that artifact.

Choose an abscissa `h` whose *cubic value* is nonsquare. The producer says a rejected coordinate; it does not incorrectly replace this with an abscissa that is itself merely nonsquare. For ordinary weights `2^j`, the window `h+{0,...,2^s-1}` modulo `p` is injective when `2^s<=p`: any nonzero difference has absolute value less than `p`. No coefficient is zero. Its zero word is rejected, so `M(0)=1` and `M` is nonzero. This proves a new-window guarantee, not a guarantee about arbitrary nonzero weights or every pre-existing fixed domain. For other weights, injectivity remains an explicit assumption of the producer's application.

The proof keeps `p>3`; this matters. In the excluded characteristic-three example `y^2=x^3-x+1`, the cubic is identically one on `F_3`, giving seven points and no rejected abscissa. Thus an unqualified all-characteristic odd-prime-order formulation would fail, but the actual stated theorem does not make it.

For independent uniform samples in `F_p`, the success probability is `U_-/p` and the expected sample count is `p/U_-`, at most `1/(1/2-1/sqrt(p))`. This expectation requires that sampling convention; it is not a worst-case deterministic guarantee. One may instead scan at most `p` coordinates. At `O(log p)` field multiplications per cubic/residuosity test, that fallback costs `O(p log p)` field multiplications. The producer does not state a fast deterministic algorithm; this review makes that boundary explicit.

A rejected-only window has `M=1` and an empty factor base, not a useful relation base. The two-summand lifting argument then also implies that the baseline Boolean system has no solution. A fixed all-valid window has `M=0`; it cannot be repaired by changing the window while claiming to preserve the fixed problem. Both cases are explicitly recognized by the producer. No guaranteed useful factor-base size or relation yield is proved.

## 7. `cost_and_claim_boundary`: holds

The construction charges can be derived directly. For `n=2^s` Boolean words per distinct domain:

* Table construction takes `n` cubic/residuosity tests, which can be implemented with `O(n log p)` field multiplications using Euler's criterion, plus coordinate and polynomial-evaluation additions.
* Multilinear interpolation is a subset Möbius transform: `c_S=sum_(T subset S)(-1)^(|S|-|T|)v(1_T)`. An in-place implementation has `s n/2` field subtractions, hence `O(s n)` field operations.
* A dense coefficient table has `n` field elements, or `O(n log p)` bits. The value table may use `O(n)` packed bits, but coefficients are field elements. Construction can use `O(n)` field-element workspace. Distinct domains require separate construction/storage; identical domains may share the table without identifying their variable blocks.
* Coordinate search also costs the tests described in Section 6; its expected sample count is not itself the total arithmetic cost. Nothing here proves efficient deterministic search in `log p` or polynomial-size output in `s`.

These agree with the producer's analytic claims and its machine-readable construction fields. No benchmark or scientific trial was executed for this review, and no implementation was inspected. In particular, the producer's sentence about prior PNEXT measurements showing greater search work was **not independently validated**: those run artifacts are outside the present allowed reads. That historical aside is not used as evidence for any verdict here.

A degree-of-regularity reduction for two specified equivalent generator lists is a valid mathematical statement. It does not, by itself, establish an improved solving degree, first/last fall degree, practical solver cost, relation yield, scalar extraction, unknown-scalar recovery, or matched ECDLP attack advantage.
