## 1. Mathematical inputs and the invariant being computed

Let
\[
 B_i=k[z_{i1},\ldots,z_{is}]/(z_{i1}^2,\ldots,z_{is}^2),\qquad
 A=\bigotimes_{i=1}^m B_i,
 \quad L_i=\sum_{j=1}^s w_{ij}z_{ij},\quad w_{ij}\ne0.
\]
Assume `s >= 1`, `m >= 1`, `1 <= e <= s`, and characteristic zero or characteristic `p > s`. Write
\[
 P=\prod_{i=1}^m L_i^e,\qquad
 a=\left\lceil\frac{s+e}{2}\right\rceil.
\]
Every variable has degree one; the block multigrading will also be used.

The external linear-algebra input is the strong Lefschetz assertion for the sum of the variables in one square-zero block, under characteristic zero or characteristic greater than that block's variable count. This is exactly the scope of [Phuong–Tran, Theorem 2.7](https://arxiv.org/html/2211.13548). Applying the diagonal automorphism `z_ij -> w_ij z_ij` gives the same assertion for `L_i`. Only `p > s` is used: there is no application of a Lefschetz theorem to all `ms` variables at once.

For a specified affine generator list `F` in a standard polynomial ring `S`, the invariant used here is
\[
 d_{\rm reg}(\mathcal F)
   =\min\{d\ge0:(\mathcal F^{\rm top})_d=S_d\},
\]
where the ideal is generated by the highest homogeneous parts of the *listed generators*. This is [Caminata–Gorla, Definition 1.4](https://arxiv.org/html/2112.05579). If its graded quotient is nonzero and Artinian, this is one plus the largest nonzero quotient degree. If the listed generators include `1`, the value is zero. These uses of the two papers are retrieved primary-source inputs; the remaining arguments below are derived here. The standard Hasse inequality will be explicitly identified as an additional named mathematical theorem in Section 6; it was not independently reproved or retrieved from an additional source.

In particular, the ideal generated by the listed highest parts is not automatically the ideal of highest parts of *every* element of the affine ideal. Solving degree, last fall degree, and first fall degree are not computed here.

## 2. Exact tensor threshold, including nonvanishing

Use the convention `binom(s,d)=0` outside `0 <= d <= s`. In a block, multiplication by `L_i^e` into degree `d` has rank
\[
 r(d)=\min\left\{\binom{s}{d-e},\binom{s}{d}\right\}.
\]
This follows from strong Lefschetz. For `0 <= d <= s`, it is surjective precisely when
\[
 d\ge a.
\]
Indeed a degree below `e` has zero source. Otherwise binomial symmetry and strict increase toward the middle imply
\[
 \binom{s}{d-e}\ge\binom{s}{d}
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad 2d\ge s+e.
\]
One way to check the comparison is to compare the distances of `d-e` and `d` from `s/2`: the difference of their squared distances is `e(s+e-2d)`.

For a block degree vector `d=(d_1,...,d_m)` with every `0 <= d_i <= s`, multiplication by `P` is the tensor product of the block maps. Consequently
\[
 \dim_k(A/(P))_{\boldsymbol d}
  =\prod_{i=1}^m\binom{s}{d_i}
    -\prod_{i=1}^m r(d_i).
 \tag{1}
\]
This component vanishes exactly when *all* the block maps are surjective, equivalently when every `d_i >= a`. If any block degree is outside `0,...,s`, the ambient component is zero.

A surviving component must therefore have at least one block degree at most `a-1`, while every other block degree is at most `s`. Its total degree is at most
\[
 D_0=(m-1)s+a-1.
 \tag{2}
\]
This proves the vanishing side: all degrees strictly greater than `D_0` vanish.

For the other side, take the specific multidegree
\[
 (a-1,s,\ldots,s).
\]
Every map into a block-top component is surjective of rank one, since `s >= a`. Thus (1) gives the strictly positive dimension
\[
 \binom{s}{a-1}-\binom{s}{a-1-e}>0.
 \tag{3}
\]
Equivalently, choose a vector outside the image of `L_1^e` in degree `a-1` and tensor it with the top monomial in every other block. Its quotient class is nonzero. This identifies an explicit nonzero multidegree immediately preceding complete vanishing, without an assumption about total-degree genericity.

It follows that
\[
 \boxed{\max\{d:(A/(P))_d\ne0\}
    =(m-1)s+\left\lceil\frac{s+e}{2}\right\rceil-1.}
 \tag{4}
\]
The corresponding first vanishing degree is
\[
 \boxed{(m-1)s+\left\lceil\frac{s+e}{2}\right\rceil.}
 \tag{5}
\]
These are exact, not upper bounds alone.

For the parity and endpoint checks: if `s+e` is even, `a=(s+e)/2` and the boundary block map has equal source and target dimensions; if it is odd, `a=(s+e+1)/2`. At `e=1`, `a=ceil((s+1)/2)`. At `e=s`, `a=s`, and `P` is a nonzero scalar multiple of the full `ms`-variable top monomial, so the largest surviving degree is `ms-1`, agreeing with (4). At `s=e=1`, (4) gives `m-1`.

## 3. Adding one nonzero homogeneous squarefree form per block

Let `q_i` be nonzero and homogeneous squarefree in block `i`. First suppose `1 <= deg(q_i) <= s`. Expand
\[
 q_i=\sum_{|T|=\delta_i}c_T z_{i,T}
\]
and choose a set `T_0` with `c_(T_0) != 0`. Multiplication by its complement gives
\[
 q_i z_{i,T_0^c}=c_{T_0}z_{i,\{1,\ldots,s\}}
 \quad\text{in }B_i.
 \tag{6}
\]
Every different support of the same size meets the complement, and its term has a squared variable. Thus the entire one-dimensional block-top component belongs to `(q_i)`. In
\[
 C_i=B_i/(q_i)
\]
all degrees at least `s` vanish.

The block map by `L_i^e` on `C_i` remains surjective into any degree `d >= a`: a preimage in `B_i` descends to a preimage in `C_i`. After quotienting by all `q_i`, the residual multiplication by `P` is still the tensor product of these induced maps. A nonzero component of
\[
 A/(P,q_1,\ldots,q_m)
\]
therefore has all block degrees at most `s-1` and at least one block degree at most `a-1`. Hence
\[
 \boxed{\max\{d:(A/(P,q_1,\ldots,q_m))_d\ne0\}
    \le (m-1)(s-1)+a-1,}
 \tag{7}
\]
whenever this quotient is nonzero. The first vanishing degree is at most
\[
 \boxed{(m-1)(s-1)+a.}
 \tag{8}
\]
This is an upper bound, not a proof of equality for arbitrary `q_i`. Relative to (5), the guaranteed decrease in this bound is `m-1`. Thus it is strict for `m >= 2`; for `m=1` this reasoning promises no decrease. For example, adjoining a form already in `(L_1^e)` can change nothing.

A nonzero homogeneous degree-zero form is a unit: normalize it to `1`. In that case the quotient is zero in *every* degree, there is no largest nonzero degree, and a polynomial system explicitly containing that constant has `d_reg=0`. It must be treated as the unit-ideal case rather than as a nontrivial equality in (7). A zero polynomial provides no block-top killing at all. In particular, if one of the required `q_i` is zero, the all-block proof above cannot be used without modification.

## 4. The actual two-summand Boolean systems

Let
\[
 E/\mathbb F_p:\quad y^2=x^3+Ax+B,\qquad 4A^3+27B^2\ne0,
\]
with `p > max(3,s)`, `s >= 2`. Let `R=(r,t)` be finite rational with `t != 0`, and write
\[
 S=\mathbb F_p[z_{11},\ldots,z_{1s},z_{21},\ldots,z_{2s}],\quad
 x_i=h_i+L_i,\quad L_i=\sum_jw_{ij}z_{ij},\quad w_{ij}\ne0.
\]
Define the explicit polynomial
\[
 S_3(X,Y,r)
  =(X-Y)^2r^2
    -2\big((X+Y)(XY+A)+2B\big)r
    +(XY-A)^2-4B(X+Y).
 \tag{9}
\]
Set `F=S_3(x_1,x_2,r)` and
\[
 J_B=(z_{ij}^2-z_{ij}:i=1,2,\ 1\le j\le s).
\]
The baseline generator list and its ideal are
\[
 \mathcal F_0=\{z_{ij}^2-z_{ij}\}_{i,j}\cup\{F\},
 \qquad I_0=J_B+(F).
 \tag{10}
\]
These are affine Boolean equations, with no replacement of `z^2-z` by `z^2` in the affine system.

For each block define a Boolean function
\[
 v_i(u)=
 \begin{cases}
 0,&x_i(u)^3+Ax_i(u)+B\text{ is a square in }\mathbb F_p\text{, including }0,\\
 1,&x_i(u)^3+Ax_i(u)+B\text{ is a nonsquare}.
 \end{cases}
\]
Its exact multilinear representative is
\[
 M_i(z_i)=\sum_{u\in\{0,1\}^s}v_i(u)
     \prod_{j:u_j=1}z_{ij}\prod_{j:u_j=0}(1-z_{ij}).
 \tag{11}
\]
The augmented generator list is
\[
 \mathcal F_+=\mathcal F_0\cup\{M_1,M_2\},
 \qquad I_+=I_0+(M_1,M_2).
 \tag{12}
\]
Thus `M_i=0` requires rational curve membership for the block abscissa, including rational points of ordinate zero if such points exist. It does not choose an ordinate sign, nor recover a scalar.

The quartic highest part of (9) after affine substitution is
\[
 F^{\rm top}=L_1^2L_2^2.
\]
Therefore the ideal of the listed highest parts in (10) is
\[
 H_0=(z_{ij}^2,L_1^2L_2^2).
 \tag{13}
\]
Its quotient is exactly the tensor construction with `m=e=2`. The weights and characteristic hypotheses ensure that the squarefree degree-two part of each `L_i^2` is nonzero. In particular the quartic survives in the square-zero quotient, also for `s=2`.

If `F` is first reduced to its unique multilinear Boolean representative, its quartic part is the squarefree part of `L_1^2L_2^2`. It differs from that quartic by an element of `(z_ij^2)`, and the two listed-highest-part ideals are the same. This observation concerns this explicit reduction; arbitrary replacement by other generators of the same affine ideal need not preserve this invariant.

Specializing (4)–(5) gives
\[
 \max\{d:(S/H_0)_d\ne0\}
       =s+\left\lceil\frac s2\right\rceil,
 \qquad
 \boxed{d_{\rm reg}(\mathcal F_0)
       =s+\left\lceil\frac s2\right\rceil+1.}
 \tag{14}
\]
If **each** `M_i` is nonzero and nonconstant, its highest part `q_i` is a nonzero homogeneous squarefree form. Equations (7)–(8) then imply
\[
 \max\{d:(S/(\mathcal F_+^{\rm top}))_d\ne0\}
       \le s+\left\lceil\frac s2\right\rceil-1,
 \qquad
 \boxed{d_{\rm reg}(\mathcal F_+)
       \le s+\left\lceil\frac s2\right\rceil.}
 \tag{15}
\]
For even `s=2u`, the baseline and augmented bounds are `3u+1` and `3u`; for odd `s=2u+1`, they are `3u+3` and `3u+2`. If either `M_i=1`, the augmented list instead contains a unit and its degree of regularity is zero. If a membership polynomial is identically zero, adding it changes nothing; the two-nonzero-block premise in (15) cannot be silently retained.

## 5. Affine lifting, equality of solutions, and equality of ideals

The following is separate from all graded arguments. Put `f(U)=U^3+AU+B`, and, for two rational candidate abscissas `x,y`, put
\[
 u=x+y,\quad v=xy,\quad
 N=v-ru-2r^2-A.
\]
Using `t^2=r^3+Ar+B`, a direct expansion of (9) gives
\[
 S_3(x,y,r)=N^2-4t^2(u+r).
 \tag{16}
\]
If `S_3=0`, define the rational slope
\[
 \lambda=\frac{N}{2t}.
\]
Then `lambda^2=u+r`. The polynomial identity
\[
 f(U)-\big(\lambda(U-r)-t\big)^2
       =(U-r)(U-x)(U-y)
 \tag{17}
\]
follows from this equation and `2t lambda=N`: the quadratic coefficient agrees by `lambda^2=u+r`, the linear coefficient agrees by `2t lambda=N`, and the constant follows from the root `U=r`.

In particular,
\[
 y_1=\lambda(x-r)-t,\qquad
 y_2=\lambda(y-r)-t
\]
are in `F_p` and satisfy `y_1^2=f(x)`, `y_2^2=f(y)`. Both abscissas are rational curve members. Geometrically (17) is the intersection, counted with multiplicity, of the nonvertical rational line through `-R=(r,-t)` with the nonsingular cubic. The three intersection points sum to the identity; hence
\[
 (x,y_1)+(y,y_2)=R.
\]
This also covers `x=y`, `x=r`, and `x=y=r`: repeated roots encode the appropriate tangent multiplicities. No division by `x-y` or `x-r` was made. Conversely, finite rational points summing to finite `R` are collinear with `-R` with these multiplicities, so their abscissas satisfy (9). The vertical-line case would sum two finite points to the identity, which is excluded by finite `R`.

It follows that every Boolean zero of (10) already has `M_1=M_2=0`. Thus (10) and (12) have the same affine Boolean solutions. In fact their ideals in `S` are equal, not merely their radicals. The exact reason is the split reduced algebra
\[
 S/J_B\simeq\prod_{u\in\{0,1\}^{2s}}\mathbb F_p.
 \tag{18}
\]
In this product, the ideal generated by the function `F` has coordinate zero exactly at the zeros of `F`, and is the entire field at every other coordinate. A function belongs to this ideal if and only if it vanishes at every zero of `F`. Hence the membership functions belong to `(F)` modulo `J_B`, and
\[
 \boxed{I_+=I_0.}
 \tag{19}
\]
More explicitly, define a function `H_i(u)=M_i(u)/F(u)` when `F(u) != 0`, and `H_i(u)=0` otherwise. Multilinear interpolation gives `M_i-H_iF in J_B`. This is an existence proof of ideal membership, not a claim that enumerating this multiplier is inexpensive.

For an arbitrary proposed exact membership predicate, the necessary and sufficient condition for equal ideals here is that it vanish on every Boolean zero of the original `F`; (18) proves both directions. The rational lift establishes that condition for the stated two-summand system. Injectivity of the digit map is not needed for this argument. Nor is odd prime group order needed in Sections 4–5.

The hypothesis `t != 0` is essential to this proof and to an unrestricted assertion of the lift. As an exact excluded-hypothesis control, over `F_5` the nonsingular curve `y^2=x^3+x` has target `(0,0)`, and (9) becomes `(xy-1)^2`. It vanishes at `x=y=1`, although `f(1)=2` is a nonsquare in `F_5`. Adding exact membership predicates can therefore remove solutions in that excluded case. Singular cubics or characteristics 2 or 3 are outside the stated group-law hypotheses. Nothing here proves an analogous individual-rational-membership implication for more than two summands.

## 6. Constructing new windows and its cost

Continue with `p > 3`. Suppose now that `#E(F_p)=q` is odd prime. In fact, odd group order suffices for the counting step below. There are no rational points of order two, so `f(a) != 0` for every `a in F_p`. Let `N_+` and `N_-` count square-valued and nonsquare-valued abscissas. Point counting gives exactly
\[
 q=1+2N_+,\qquad N_- =p-N_+=\frac{2p+1-q}{2}.
 \tag{20}
\]
The standard Hasse inequality `|q-(p+1)| <= 2 sqrt(p)` implies
\[
 N_-\ge\frac{p-2\sqrt p}{2}>0\qquad(p\ge5).
 \tag{21}
\]
Thus an abscissa `a_0` with **nonsquare cubic value** `f(a_0)` exists. Saying only that the abscissa itself is a quadratic nonresidue would not establish this fact. The point-count identity is derived above; (21) explicitly invokes Hasse's theorem as a standard external theorem, not as a result of these two retrieved papers or a fresh proof of that theorem.

The phrase “any odd-prime-order curve” must retain `p > 3`. It is false without an appropriate small-characteristic exception: on `F_3`, `y^2=x^3-x+1` has cubic value identically `1` as a function, and therefore has seven rational points and no nonsquare-valued abscissa. This is an exact identity control, not a sampled observation.

For `2^s <= p`, choose **new** digit windows
\[
 h_i=a_0,\quad w_j=2^{j-1},\quad
 x_i(z_i)=a_0+\sum_{j=1}^s2^{j-1}z_{ij}\pmod p.
 \tag{22}
\]
Each window enumerates `2^s` distinct abscissas, even if the translated interval wraps modulo `p`: the difference of two distinct integers in `0,...,2^s-1` is nonzero with absolute value less than `p`. All weights are nonzero. Its zero word maps to `a_0`, so
\[
 M_i(0,\ldots,0)=1.
\]
Consequently each exact membership polynomial is nonzero. This construction may reuse the same starting coordinate in both blocks, or find separate such coordinates.

Nonzero does not mean nonconstant or useful. If every abscissa of a chosen window is invalid, its predicate is `1`; the factor base there is empty, and the original Boolean system is also unsatisfiable by Section 5. The constant-one case is already handled separately. Guaranteeing a useful nonempty factor base requires an additional condition or construction. The present argument guarantees nonzero predicates, which is the premise requested, and does not conceal the empty-base case.

For a pre-existing fixed factor base, changing `h_i` or the digit window changes the relation problem. Equation (22) constructs a new factor base; it does not prove a nonzero-predicate statement for every unchanged fixed window. In a fixed window on which every abscissa is valid, `M_i=0`, and adding it gives no unconditional strict reduction.

All costs below are theoretical operation bounds, not measurements. Let `n=2^s` and `b=ceil(log_2 p)`.

* Finding `a_0`: uniform independent candidate coordinates succeed with exact probability `rho=N_-/p`. The expected number of tests is `1/rho=2p/(2p+1-q)`, bounded using (21) by `2/(1-2/sqrt(p))`. This is an expectation for a specified randomized construction, not a deterministic guarantee and not a sampling result from this task. A deterministic scan has at most `p` tests. Testing one coordinate by polynomial evaluation and Euler's criterion uses `O(log p)` field multiplications; a standard bit-cost upper bound is `O(M(b) b)`, where `M(b)` bounds multiplication with modular reduction. If `q` is not already known, its use in the exact expression is analytical; the search can test cubic values without computing `q`.
* Exact Boolean table: one block needs `n` cubic-value/residuosity tests, hence `O(n log p)` field multiplications using Euler's criterion, plus `O(n)` field additions to enumerate the consecutive coordinates. The binary validity table needs `O(n)` bits if packed. This cost is required even after a starting coordinate has been found by the simple construction.
* Multilinear coefficients: from the value table, coefficients satisfy `c_S=sum_(T subset S) (-1)^(|S|-|T|) v(1_T)`. The in-place subset Möbius transform takes `s n / 2` field subtractions, hence `O(s n)` field operations, and `O(n)` field elements of workspace. In general the coefficients are field elements, even though table values are bits.
* Storage and multiple blocks: a dense coefficient representation has `n` field slots per block, or `O(n log p)` bits. Retaining all `m` block polynomials uses `O(m n)` field elements, plus any retained tables. Blocks can be constructed sequentially with `O(n)` additional field-element workspace. Summed construction bounds are `O(m n log p)` field multiplications and `O(m s n)` transform additions, in addition to coordinate search. Sparse output may be smaller, but no sparsity theorem is supplied here.

No claim that this exponential-in-`s` construction is cheap follows from the degree bound. No solver, relation collection, full ECDLP algorithm, unknown-scalar recovery, or matched end-to-end resource comparison has been established.

## 7. Known-false controls, proof status, and unresolved scope

The assumptions are active. In characteristic two, already in a two-variable block `(z_1+z_2)^2=0`, so the maximal-rank map from degree zero to degree two fails. With a zero weight, diagonal rescaling is unavailable; for instance `L=z_1` in a two-variable square-zero block also has `L^2=0`. If `e=0`, the product generator is `1` and the quotient is zero; (4) does not apply. If `e>s`, the relevant block power is zero and `P=0`, so the quotient is `A`, with largest nonzero degree `ms`; this lies outside the positive-range theorem. Zero predicates do not kill top degrees; constant-one predicates kill the whole ring. The target-ordinate and fixed-window controls are addressed in Sections 5 and 6.

The exact baseline threshold (4)–(5), the conditional augmented upper bound (7)–(8), and the affine two-summand ideal equivalence (19) have been derived independently here. The new-window nonzero guarantee is proved under the stated `p>=5`, odd-order and window-size assumptions, using the explicitly named standard Hasse theorem. No unresolved algebraic step remains in these scoped deductions, apart from accepting the cited Lefschetz theorem, the definition, and the standard elliptic-curve facts and Hasse theorem as mathematical inputs. This artifact does not independently prove those external theorems.

The following stronger statements remain unproved here: equality in the general augmented bound; strict decrease when the nonzero-per-block premise fails; guaranteed useful factor-base size for the simple window choice; applicability to an unchanged arbitrary fixed window; any higher-summand rational lift; any identification with first/last fall degree or solving degree; any solver or attack speedup.
