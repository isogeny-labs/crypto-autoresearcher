## 0. Result summary

1. **PROVED (Theorems 1–2, under the setup in §1):** for the single-vertex translation/negation quiver of an odd-prime-order subgroup G = ⟨P⟩ ≅ Z/n of a prime-field curve, the evaluation kernel is the normal closure of (i) commutators [t_i, t_j], (ii) the dihedral torsion ν² = 1 and (ν t_i)² = 1 (forced by negation having order 2), and (iii) the **commutative** subset-sum relation lattice L = ker(Z^B → Z/n). Every word-level relation descends, via commutators and forced torsion only, to an ordinary signed-subset-sum relation of no larger ℓ1 weight — at **any** word degree, any B, and any factor base satisfying §1.
2. **PROVED (Proposition 1):** the *unqualified* claim "kernel = commutator ideal" is false as stated (ν² − 1 is an explicit kernel element outside the commutator ideal); the clean unqualified form holds for the translations-only subquiver: ker = ⟨⟨commutators⟩ ∪ L⟩.
3. **PROVED + numerically verified (Corollary 2):** the degree-d value set is exactly {ε + Σ a_i s_i : |a|₁ + m(ε,a) ≤ d} with m ∈ {0,1,2} the minimal negation count — this **exactly reproduces the measured strict inclusion** (p=431, B=4, n=439: NC 350/439 vs commutative 437/439) and proves S_nc(d) ⊆ S_comm(d) for all d. The anomaly is boundary-shell pruning of the *image of a truncated ball*, fully consistent with the kernel theorem, which is about *relations*.
4. **CONJECTURE (Conjecture 1):** the collapse persists for Frobenius-augmented quivers over extension fields F_q (skew-group-ring form, module lattice); precise statement and proof route given, two named missing steps.
5. **OPEN GAPS:** (G1) multi-vertex isogeny-graph quivers (groupoid evaluation); (G2) the search-**cost** layer (relation-set collapse is proved; a complexity-level simulation theorem is not); (G3) non-affine arrows — none exist within endomorphisms of a cyclic group, which is the structural reason prime-field quivers collapse.

Consequence within the odd-prime-order setup of §1: candidate C3's promotion gate is unreachable on that translation/negation quiver family **by proof** — the "NC relation class with no commutative shadow" that the gate requires is shown not to exist there. H-NCP-001's scoped rejection (DEC-20260718-009) is upgraded from toy-experiment scope to a theorem for this stated family.

## 1. Setup

Let E/F_p be an elliptic curve over a prime field, G = ⟨P⟩ ≤ E(F_p) a cyclic subgroup of prime order n (n odd), and P_i = s_i P with s_i ∈ (Z/n)^× for i = 1..B (the factor base; all factor-base points lie in G, so the whole setup lives inside G).

**Order-two boundary.** The odd-order assumption matters to the *full kernel* claims. For n = 2, negation fixes every point of G, so the action evaluation also kills ν itself. The stated group presentation only imposes ν² = 1: with B = 1, s₁ = 1, and t₁² = 1, its quotient is (Z/2) × (Z/2), where ν is nontrivial, while the actual affine permutation group of G is Z/2. Likewise ν − 1 lies in the path-algebra evaluation kernel but is not in the stated ideal J. Thus Theorems 1–2 and the identical-action iff in Corollary 1 cannot be extended to n = 2 as written. The affine normal form and the forward shadow-to-action implication remain valid. This scope correction does not change EV-NCP-001: its recorded subgroup orders start at 13. The historical source data and graphs for those measured instances are unchanged.

**Formalization boundary.** The current NCP Lean targets formalize the affine normal form, shadow reachability, and a witness that commutators alone do not generate the kernel. They do not state or kernel-check Theorem 1's full normal-closure equality or Theorem 2's full ideal equality. “PROVED” in this historical note means the written argument in §2, not a claim that the full equalities have been accepted by the Lean kernel. This distinction also applies to the theorem-level consequence in §3.

**Quiver.** One vertex; loop arrows {t_1, …, t_B} (translation by P_i) and ν (negation). W = free monoid on {t_1,…,t_B, ν}; F = free group on the same letters; A = Z⟨t_1,…,t_B, ν⟩ the path algebra (over Z; everything below holds over any commutative base ring).

**Actions.** Arrows act on points of G: t_i : X ↦ X + P_i; ν : X ↦ −X. Every word therefore acts as an **affine** map X ↦ εX + βP with ε ∈ {±1}, β ∈ Z/n. The affine maps of G of this form constitute Aff(G) = G ⋊ {±1} ≅ Z/n ⋊ Z/2, the generalized dihedral group of G, with composition (b, ε)(b′, ε′) = (b + εb′, εε′).

**Three evaluation maps** (the mission statement's "KQ → Z/n (or Z[E(F_p)])" splits into):
- (E1) *action evaluation* φ : F → Aff(G), t_i ↦ (s_i, +1), ν ↦ (0, −1). Reading a word as a composition with the **leftmost letter applied first** (the experiment's convention), evaluation is an anti-homomorphism W → Aff(G), equivalently a homomorphism W^op → Aff(G); since the target is a group, replacing φ by w ↦ φ(w)^{−1} gives a homomorphism F → Aff(G) with the *same kernel*, so all kernel statements below are convention-independent and are stated for the homomorphism form.
- (E2) *algebra evaluation* ρ̂ : A → Z[Aff(G)], the ring homomorphism extending φ (the path-algebra evaluation used in Theorem 2). The point action separately induces a Z-algebra map ψ : Z[Aff(G)] → End_Z(Z[G]); ψ need not be injective, so ker(ψ ∘ ρ̂) can be larger than ker ρ̂. Theorem 2 identifies only the latter kernel.
- (E3) *orbit value at P* v : W → Z/n, v(w) = ev_P(φ(w)) where ev_P((b, ε)) = ε·1 + b ∈ Z/n. This is the experiment's value map: eval(w, P) = v(w)·P, and a Q-reaching word (Q = kP) is a word with v(w) = k — the ECDLP relation.

The target distinction is concrete even when the action of Aff(G) on G is faithful. For G = Z/3, let T_k(x) = x + k and R_k(x) = k − x for k ∈ G. The six permutations are distinct, so Δ = Σ_k [T_k] − Σ_k [R_k] is nonzero in Z[Aff(G)]. For each basis point [x] of Z[G], both sums send [x] to Σ_y [y]; hence ψ(Δ) = 0. A faithful group action therefore does not embed its integral group algebra into point operators. This counterexample concerns the operator target, not Theorem 2's group-algebra target. The [independent J2 review](../coordination/formal-reviews/ncp-pr2417-20261031/reviews/TASK-20261031-8a54e2/review.md) records the six coefficients, zero matrix and a concrete elliptic-curve realization.

**Shadow.** σ : W → {±1} × Z^B, w ↦ (ε(w), a(w)), where ε(w) = (−1)^{#ν(w)} and a_i(w) = Σ over occurrences of t_i in w of (−1)^{(#ν-letters to the right of the occurrence)}. This is verbatim the frozen instrument's `shadow_of_word` (experiments/EXP-NCP-001/ncp1_quiver_gb.sage, lines 110–122).

## 2. Statements and proofs

### Lemma 1 (affine normal form) — PROVED

Every word w acts on G as X ↦ ε(w) X + β(w) P with β(w) = Σ_i a_i(w) s_i (mod n). Hence v(w) = ε(w) + Σ_i a_i(w) s_i (mod n): **the action and the orbit value of a word depend only on its shadow**.

*Proof.* Induction on |w|. Base: empty word, (ε, β) = (1, 0), shadow (+1, 0). Step: append a letter to w with state (ε, β).
- Append t_i: action X ↦ εX + βP + s_iP, so (ε, β) ↦ (ε, β + s_i). The new occurrence has zero ν's to its right (contributes +1 to a_i) and no earlier occurrence's right-ν count changes, so a ↦ a + e_i. Consistent.
- Append ν: action X ↦ −(εX + βP) = (−ε)X + (−β)P, so (ε, β) ↦ (−ε, −β). Every earlier occurrence gains exactly one ν to its right (sign flips), so a ↦ −a, and ε = (−1)^{#ν} flips. Consistent.
Evaluating at X = P gives v(w) = ε(w) + β(w). ∎

(The instrument's `scalar_eval_word` is exactly this reduction; its positive control verified it against point arithmetic on 2400 random words, EV-NCP-001.)

### Lemma 2 (shadow = dihedral homomorphism; reachability formula) — PROVED

Let D = Z^B ⋊ Z/2 with Z/2 acting by negation (the *shadow group*), and π : D → Aff(G), (a, ε) ↦ (Σ a_i s_i mod n, ε).

(a) σ extends to a group (anti-)homomorphism σ̂ : F → D with the same convention as (E1): the incremental updates of Lemma 1 — append t_i : (a, ε) ↦ (a + e_i, ε); append ν : (a, ε) ↦ (−a, −ε) — are precisely **left**-multiplication by (e_i, +1) and (0, −1) in D, i.e. σ(wℓ) = σ(ℓ)·σ(w). With the homomorphism convention of (E1), φ = π ∘ σ̂.

(b) **Reachability.** A shadow (ε, a) ∈ D is represented by a word of length ≤ d if and only if |a|₁ + m(ε, a) ≤ d, where
m(ε, a) = 0 if ε = +1 and a ∈ N^B; m(ε, a) = 1 if ε = −1; m(ε, a) = 2 if ε = +1 and a ∉ N^B.

*Proof of (b).* Necessity: a representing word needs ≥ |a|₁ t-letters (each occurrence contributes ±1 to one coordinate of a) and its #ν must have parity ε. If ε = −1, #ν ≥ 1. If ε = +1 and some a_i < 0, at least one occurrence must have an odd number of ν's to its right, so #ν ≥ 1; parity then forces #ν ≥ 2. Sufficiency by construction (blocks denote the indicated occurrences in any order):
- (ε=+1, a ∈ N^B): word = (all occurrences); length |a|₁.
- (ε=−1, a ∈ N^B): word = ν (all occurrences) — each occurrence has 0 ν's to the right; length |a|₁ + 1.
- (ε=−1, −a ∈ N^B): word = (all occurrences) ν — each has 1 ν to the right; length |a|₁ + 1.
- (ε=−1, mixed a): word = (negative-part occurrences) ν (positive-part occurrences); length |a|₁ + 1.
- (ε=+1, a ∉ N^B): word = ν (negative-part occurrences) ν (positive-part occurrences) — negative-part occurrences have exactly 1 ν to the right, positive-part 0, #ν = 2; length |a|₁ + 2.
All constructions preserve the shadow (adjacent pairs of ν's beyond the construction can pad length by +2 without changing it; the stated bound is "≤ d", so no parity issue arises). ∎

### Theorem 1 (kernel, group form) — PROVED

Let R = { [t_i, t_j] : 1 ≤ i, j ≤ B } ∪ { ν² } ∪ { ν t_i ν t_i : 1 ≤ i ≤ B } and let L = { a ∈ Z^B : Σ_i a_i s_i ≡ 0 (mod n) } (the commutative subset-sum relation lattice). Then

ker(φ : F → Aff(G)) = ⟨⟨ R ∪ { t^a : a ∈ L } ⟩⟩, where t^a = t_1^{a_1} ··· t_B^{a_B}.

*Proof.* Step 1: F/⟨⟨R⟩⟩ ≅ D. The presentation ⟨t_1,…,t_B, ν | [t_i,t_j] = 1, ν² = 1, ν t_i ν = t_i^{−1}⟩ is the standard semidirect-product presentation of Z^B ⋊ Z/2 (note ν t_i ν = t_i^{−1} ⟺ ν t_i ν t_i = 1 given ν² = 1): the relations of R hold in D, and conversely in F/⟨⟨R⟩⟩ every element reduces to a normal form t^a ν^e (a ∈ Z^B, e ∈ {0,1}) by moving ν's right (ν t_i = t_i^{−1} ν), merging them (ν² = 1), and sorting t's; the normal form is unique (e is read off the quotient D → Z/2 killing translations; a is read off the translation subgroup, free abelian on the t_i). Step 2: ker π = L × {+1}: the Z/2-component of π((a, ε)) is ε itself, so kernel elements have ε = +1, and then Σ a_i s_i ≡ 0 ⟺ a ∈ L. Therefore ker φ is the preimage of L × {+1} in F, i.e. the normal closure of R together with lifts t^a, a ∈ L. (Since conjugation by ν sends t^a to t^{−a}, no further conjugates are needed: ker π is generated as a subgroup by {t^a}.) ∎

### Corollary 1 (commutator collapse of relations; the experiment's negative control is a theorem) — PROVED

Two words w, w′ act identically on G iff their shadows differ by an element of L × {+1}. In particular every Q-reaching relation v(w) = k has the **commutative shadow relation** ε + Σ_i a_i s_i ≡ k (mod n) with |a|₁ ≤ |w| — a signed subset-sum relation of ℓ1 weight at most the word degree. Consequently an NC word search at degree ≤ d can find no relation whose shadow is absent from the degree-≤d commutative quotient — at every degree d, not only d ≤ 6. This is exactly the scoped-negative condition of EXP-NCP-001 ("the commutative quotient reproduces all found relations"), now proved for the whole quiver family. (The measured 0 violations on full histograms and 94,472 shadow replays were therefore algebraically guaranteed, as the specification's invalidation rule anticipated: "algebraically impossible by construction".)

### Proposition 1 (the unqualified claim is false; clean form on the translations-only subquiver) — PROVED

The kernel of ρ̂ is **not** generated by commutators alone. Explicitly: ν² − 1 ∈ ker ρ̂ (negation has order 2 in Aut(G)), but the quotient of A by the two-sided ideal generated by all commutators xy − yx is the commutative polynomial ring Z[t_1,…,t_B, ν], in which ν² − 1 ≠ 0; so ν² − 1 lies outside the commutator ideal. The same holds for (ν t_i)² − 1. This torsion is *forced* by the order-2 automorphism, not optional bookkeeping. However: (i) modulo this forced torsion every remaining relation is commutative (Theorem 2 below); and (ii) for the translations-only subquiver (drop ν) Theorem 1 specializes to ker = ⟨⟨ [t_i,t_j] ⟩ ∪ {t^a : a ∈ L} ⟩ with no torsion — the unqualified "commutators + commutative lattice" form is exactly true there.

### Theorem 2 (kernel, path-algebra form) — PROVED

Let J ⊲ A be the two-sided ideal generated by
- commutators: t_i t_j − t_j t_i (1 ≤ i, j ≤ B);
- dihedral torsion: ν² − 1 and (ν t_i)² − 1 (1 ≤ i ≤ B);
- commutative lattice binomials: t^u − t^v for u, v ∈ N^B with Σ (u_i − v_i) s_i ≡ 0 (mod n).

Then ker ρ̂ = J.

*Proof.* (⊇) Each generator evaluates to 0 in Z[Aff(G)]: translations commute (G abelian); negation is an involution; and (ν t_i)² = 1 since, with (b,ε)(b′,ε′) = (b + εb′, εε′), we have ν t_i ↦ (0,−1)(s_i,+1) = (−s_i, −1) and (−s_i,−1)² = (−s_i + (−1)(−s_i), +1) = (0, +1). Finally t^u − t^v ↦ 0 iff Σ(u−v)s = 0 in Z/n.

(⊆) In A/J: n·e_i ∈ L (since n s_i ≡ 0), so t_i^n = 1 and every t_i is invertible; from (ν t_i)² = 1 and ν² = 1, ν t_i = t_i^{−1} ν. Hence every monomial reduces to t^u ν^e with u ∈ N^B, e ∈ {0,1}, and t^u depends only on the class of u in N^B/L. Since s_1 ≠ 0 and n is prime, N^B → Z/n is surjective and N^B/L ≅ Z/n, so A/J is generated as a Z-module by the ≤ 2n monomial classes {t^u ν^e : u ∈ N^B/L, e ∈ {0,1}}. The induced map f : A/J → Z[Aff(G)] is surjective (translations hit all of Z/n since s_1 generates; ν hits negation) onto a free Z-module of rank 2n. Writing g : Z^{2n} → A/J for the spanning surjection, f ∘ g : Z^{2n} → Z^{2n} is a surjective endomorphism of a noetherian module, hence an isomorphism; so g is injective, hence g and f are isomorphisms, and ker ρ̂ = J. ∎

(The commutative factor of this presentation is the standard lattice-ideal presentation of Z[Z/n] as a quotient of the polynomial semigroup ring Z[N^B] — cf. Sturmfels, *Gröbner Bases and Convex Polytopes*, lattice ideals; the proof above is self-contained.)

### Corollary 2 (degree-d value-set law; accounts for the measured strict inclusion) — PROVED, numerically verified

S_nc(d) := { v(w) : |w| ≤ d } = { ε + Σ_i a_i s_i (mod n) : (ε, a) ∈ {±1} × Z^B, |a|₁ + m(ε, a) ≤ d }, with m as in Lemma 2(b).

*Proof.* Immediate from Lemma 1 (value depends only on shadow) and Lemma 2(b) (which shadows are represented at length ≤ d). ∎

Consequences:
- S_nc(d) ⊆ S_comm(d) := { ε + Σ a_i s_i : |a|₁ ≤ d } for every d (proved; matches the measured 0 value-inclusion violations).
- Strictness criterion: S_nc(d) ⊊ S_comm(d) iff some boundary shadow (|a|₁ = d with m ≥ 1, or |a|₁ = d−1 with m = 2) hits a value not covered by reachable shadows. Word order **prunes** the commutative shadow set at the boundary shells; it can never extend it.
- The measured strict-inclusion instance (EV-NCP-001, unexpected observation 1; p=431, B=4, seed 20260718, n=439, d=6: NC reaches 350/439 values vs commutative 437/439) is reproduced **exactly** by the formula: S_pred(6) has 350 elements (verification §4). The anomaly is a property of the *image of the degree-truncated ball* (reachability), not of the *kernel* (relations); the two are consistent because the kernel theorem constrains which relations exist, while truncation constrains which shadows are representable in ≤ d letters.

### Conjecture 1 (Frobenius-augmented quiver over extension fields) — CONJECTURE

Let q = p^r, G = ⟨P⟩ ≤ E(F_q) cyclic of prime order n, stable under the q-Frobenius π_q, which then acts on G as multiplication by some λ ∈ (Z/n)^×. Add a Frobenius arrow φ acting as π_q. Then for the evaluation φ_ext : F(t_1,…,t_B, ν, φ) → G ⋊ ⟨λ, −1⟩ (with ⟨λ, −1⟩ ≤ (Z/n)^×):

ker φ_ext = ⟨⟨ [t_i,t_j], ν², ν t_i ν t_i, φ t_i φ^{−1} t_i^{−λ}, φ^{ord(λ)}, { t^a : a ∈ L } ⟩⟩,

i.e. the collapse persists: Frobenius contributes only the scaling conjugation and its order torsion, both visible in the commutative Z[λ]-module shadow. *Proof route:* identical normal-form argument with the semidirect product Z^B ⋊ (⟨λ⟩ × ⟨−1⟩). *What exactly is missing:* (i) verification that L is λ-stable so no cross-relations between the Frobenius orbit and L arise beyond the module lattice (expected since G is π_q-stable, but not checked); (ii) degenerate cases −1 ∈ ⟨λ⟩ (group then cyclic; the torsion family must be re-presented). Not needed for the prime-field closure (over F_p, π_p acts trivially on G, λ = 1, and the arrow adds only central torsion).

### Open gaps

- **G1 — multi-vertex (isogeny-graph) quivers.** The theorem is single-vertex: all arrows act on one group. Isogeny arrows map between different curves; evaluation then lands in a groupoid algebra. No collapse theorem is proved there (for ordinary curves the endomorphism/ideal-class action is itself abelian, suggesting an analogous collapse, but this is unproved).
- **G2 — search-cost layer.** Theorems 1–2 prove relation-**set** collapse: no NC relation without a commutative shadow exists, at any degree. They do not prove that an NC-GB **search** has no computational advantage in *finding* shadow relations (constant-factor or exponent differences in search cost are a complexity question, not a kernel question). The measured equal-budget cost ratios 0.679…4.430 at toy scale (EV-NCP-001) are consistent with no advantage, but no complexity-level simulation theorem (e.g., truncated NC-GB simulated by commutative search with polynomial overhead) is proved here.
- **G3 — non-affine arrows.** The collapse's structural cause: on a cyclic group every group endomorphism is scalar multiplication, so {translations} ∪ {automorphisms} always act affinely and the shadow group is metabelian with commutative translation lattice. An arrow acting non-affinely on G (e.g., divisor-level or multi-point correspondences, not automorphisms of G) would escape the model; none exists within End(G) for the prime-field translation/negation quiver.
- **G4 — point-operator algebra kernel.** Theorem 2 targets Z[Aff(G)]. Evaluation into End_Z(Z[G]) composes it with ψ and has an additional representation kernel, as the n=3 element Δ above shows. A presentation for the resulting kernel, or a proof that a particular application avoids those extra linear relations, remains open.

## 3. Consequences for the research direction

- **C3 / H-NCP-001 translation/negation relation-set claim under §1.** For the translation/negation quiver over an odd-prime-order subgroup of a prime-field curve — any B, any factor base satisfying §1, and any word degree — every relation an NC-GB/syzygy search can ever certify descends via commutators and forced dihedral torsion to a commutative signed-subset-sum relation of no larger ℓ1 weight (Theorem 1, Corollary 1). The promotion gate's required object ("an NC relation class with no commutative shadow") does not exist within this stated model; the gate is unreachable there regardless of budget or scale. The theorem is scale-independent within its assumptions, so unlike the experiment it is **not** toy-scoped; the search-cost layer, G2, remains open. The order-two full-kernel exception is recorded in §1.
- **The measured negative control was a theorem.** 0 violations on value-set inclusion and on 94,472 shadow replays (EV-NCP-001) are entailed by Corollary 1.
- **The measured strict inclusion is explained exactly.** 350/439 vs 437/439 at (p=431, B=4, n=439, d=6) is the boundary-shell pruning of Corollary 2, reproduced exactly by the m-formula (§4). No conflict with the kernel claim: kernel = relations; strict inclusion = reachability of a truncated ball.
- **Where non-collapse could still live within §1:** G1 (groupoid/isogeny setting) or G3 (non-affine correspondences) lie outside the proved quiver model. Conjecture 1 asserts collapse survives Frobenius augmentation over extension fields. The order-two full-kernel exception is also outside §1; it does not reverse the measured odd-prime-order observations.
