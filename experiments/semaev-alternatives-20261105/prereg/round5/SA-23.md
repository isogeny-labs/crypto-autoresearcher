# SA-23

**Why.** Report section 3.2: every presentation tested in rounds 1-4 is a coordinate system on V_R = E^{m-1}, and the factor-base condition "x <= B" is not algebraic. That is what limits every lattice oracle to delta < 2/(2^{m-1} m (m+1)). "Small" is an algebraic condition in exactly three settings: p-adic valuation (anomalous curves only), subfield membership (F_{p^n} only), and degree over a function field. Only the third allows a large ambient group. For an elliptic surface S -> P^1 over F_p with fibre S_{t0} = E, the Mordell-Weil group MW = S(F_p(t)) = Z^r is a lattice under the canonical height; "small" = "integral section of height <= 2 chi" is a polynomial condition on the coefficients of (X(t), Y(t)); and specialisation sp: MW -> E(F_p) is a group homomorphism. Index calculus in this representation has no relation collection and no linear algebra: all relations among the r specialised generators are the lattice itself. Its total cost is (r + 2) lifts, a lift being: given R in E(F_p), find an integral section through (t0, R). The lift is a zero-dimensional polynomial system over F_p in about 5 chi unknowns, with no box. This experiment measures whether the representation can cover the group at all (Gate A) and what a lift costs (Gate B).

**Paper bound, stated before the run.** Shioda-Tate gives r <= 12 chi - 4. When every fibre is irreducible, every integral section has height exactly 2 chi, so the integral sections are the minimal vectors of MW and number at most the kissing number in rank r, which is <= 2^{0.401 r} <= 2^{4.8 chi}. Covering a fibre of size about p therefore needs 4.8 chi >= log2 p, so the lift system has U = 5 chi + 2 >= 1.04 log2 p unknowns. Allowing sections of height <= 2 chi h (poles permitted, degree about 2 chi h) and counting by ball volume gives at best U = 0.55 log2 p at h = 1 and worse for h > 1, before the determinant penalty (supersingular Mordell-Weil lattices have determinant a power of p). Prediction: coverage needs U >= 0.55 log2 p, the measured lift cost is at least 2^{0.9 U}, and the total exponent is >= 0.5. The representation survives only if the lift solver is strongly sub-exponential in U on this structured system.

**Family (frozen).** S_d: y^2 = x^3 + t^d + 1 over F_p, 6 | d, p = -1 (mod d) (the supersingular case, maximal rank over the algebraic closure), chi = d/6. All fibres are irreducible: type II at t^d = -1, smooth at infinity. An integral section is X, Y in F_p[t] with deg X <= 2 chi, deg Y <= 3 chi and Y^2 = X^3 + t^d + 1. Fibres have j = 0; the target group G is the largest prime-order subgroup of S_{t0}(F_p). Secondary family, if time allows: Legendre y^2 = x(x+1)(x+t^d), d | p + 1 (Ulmer 2014), reducible fibres, chi = d/2.

Scout values computed before freezing (2026-11-10, pure-Python enumeration, `/home/user/semaev-runs/SA-23/scout/count_sections.py`): d = 6 (rational elliptic surface, E8 over the closure) has 8 F_p-rational integral sections at p = 11, 23 and 47, Gram rank 4, greedy-basis Gram determinant 16. These are controls, not evidence.

**Protocol.**

Gate A, coverage.
1. For d in {6, 12, 18, 24, 30} and at least 4 primes p = -1 (mod d) per d (p from 11 up to about 2^12, larger where enumeration allows), enumerate all F_p-rational integral sections; S(p, d) is their number. Method: exhaustive over X in F_p[t] of degree <= 2 chi with a polynomial square test, in C, when p^{2 chi + 1} <= 10^12; otherwise the zero-dimensional coefficient system (unknowns: coefficients of X and Y; equations: coefficients of Y^2 - X^3 - t^d - 1) solved by msolve, rational points only. Cross-check one cell per d by both methods.
2. Per cell: Gram matrix of the height pairing <P, Q> = chi + P.O + Q.O - P.Q (P.O = 0 for integral sections; P.Q = deg gcd(X_P - X_Q, Y_P - Y_Q) plus the order at infinity), the rank r(p, d), the determinant of the lattice spanned by the integral sections, and the minimum height.
3. Specialisation coverage: for at least 3 fibres t0 per cell, the number of distinct points cofactor * sp(sigma) in G over all integral sections sigma, as a fraction of |G|.
4. Controls: d = 6 at every p (must give 8 sections and rank 4); an ordinary cell p = +1 (mod d) at d = 12 (expected: far fewer sections); random X of the same degree (expected square probability about p^{-3 chi}, so 0 hits).
5. Fit log2 S(p, d) = alpha d + beta log2 p + gamma over cells with S >= 2; bootstrap 95% CIs over cells. Derive d*(p), the smallest d with predicted S >= p/2, and U(p) = 5 d*(p)/6 + 2.

Gate B, lift cost (only on cells with S >= 8).
6. Per cell, for at least 20 uniformly random targets R in G and 20 planted targets (known images), time msolve on "integral section with X(t0) = x_R, Y(t0) = y_R" (U unknowns, 6 chi + 3 equations). Record wall time, number of solutions, and verify every returned section on the surface and through R. Also time two baselines: enumerate all S sections and specialise; and meet-in-the-middle on the known lattice.
7. Fit log2 T_lift against U over cells; report the per-unknown exponent u (T about 2^{u U}) with CI. Combine with Gate A: c_hat(k) = u * U(2^k) / k at k = 64, 128, 256, with bootstrap CIs over cells, under the same out-of-sample discipline as SA-22 (fit on all but the largest cell, validate on it, then refit).
8. End to end: on the largest cell with coverage >= 1/2, solve at least 5 discrete logarithms in G by r + 2 lifts, verify [k]G = Q, and compare wall time with a matched rho on the same group (SA-21's rho binary or an equivalent single-thread implementation).

**Prediction (frozen).** alpha <= 0.8 (at or below the kissing-number regime), S(p, d) bounded in p at fixed d, u >= 0.9, c_hat >= 0.5 at every k. The d = 6 control gives 8 sections at every p.

**Criteria (frozen).**
- SUCCESS: c_hat's 95% CI lies entirely below 0.5 at k = 64, 128 and 256, and the end-to-end solve at the largest cell is faster than matched rho with the ratio's CI excluding 1.
- KILL: c_hat's 95% CI lies entirely above 0.5 at k = 64, 128 and 256; or Gate A yields no cell with d <= 30 and S(p, d) >= p/2 for any p >= 47 while alpha's CI lies entirely below 0.8 (then U(p) >= 1.04 log2 p, and the lift would need u < 0.48).
- INCONCLUSIVE: otherwise. Nothing is reinterpreted; every departure goes to DEVIATIONS.md.

**Limitations known before the run.** The family has j = 0 fibres only; a positive result would transfer to other j only through a different surface family. Rational points over F_p(t) are about half of those over F_{p^2}(t) (the scout's rank 4 of 8 at d = 6); working over F_{p^2} would double r and U alike. Gate B measures msolve, one solver; a structured solver that uses the explicit lattice is listed as a baseline, not excluded.

**Cost and tier.** Sonnet executor; one CPU, 8 GB, 24 h; C for enumeration, msolve and SageMath (`/opt/conda-sage`) as available. No security claim about any deployed curve.
