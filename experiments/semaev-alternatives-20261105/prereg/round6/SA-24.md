# SA-24

**Why.** A Jacobian has a free decomposition oracle: every element has a unique reduced divisor, and "decompose" means "factor its Mumford polynomial u". If E is a factor of Jac(C) for a curve C over F_p, a factor base of points of C might give E a relation source that does not go through the box problem. Accounting argument stated before the run: the reduced divisor of a random element is a random g-tuple. It splits over a factor base of B points with probability about (B/p)^g / g!. Index calculus in Jac(C) then costs at least p^{2-2/g} (Gaudry; Gaudry-Thome-Theriault-Diem), at least p for g >= 2. The cover adds a group of order about p^g in which E is a subgroup. This experiment measures the splitting law and the end-to-end cost, to replace the argument with data.

**Construction (verified before freezing, `prereg/round6/verify/g2check.sage`).**
- Genus 2, bielliptic. Take g(X) = (X - r^2) h(X) with h quadratic, over F_p. Then C: y^2 = g(x^2) has quotients E1: Y^2 = g(X), via (x, y) -> (x^2, y), and E2: Y^2 = X^3 g(1/X).
- Odd model: x = r + 1/z and w = y z^3 give w^2 = F(z) with deg F = 5.
- Pullback: R = (X0, Y0) on E1 maps to the Mumford pair u = monic((r^2 - X0) z^2 + 2 r z + 1), v = Y0 z^3 mod u.
- Checked at p = 1009 and 65537: #Jac(C)(F_p) = #E1 * #E2 exactly, and pullback(P + Q) = pullback(P) + pullback(Q) on 20/20 random pairs (compared up to sign; the executor checks exact equality).
- Genus 3. C3: y^2 = g4(x^2) with g4 = (X - r^2) h3(X). Quotients: E1: Y^2 = g4(X), elliptic since it has the point (r^2, 0), and the genus-2 curve H: Y^2 = X g4(X). Odd model by x = r + 1/z (degree 7).
- Target group: the largest prime-order subgroup E1[n1], with n1 not dividing #E2 (genus 2) or #Jac(H) (genus 3), so that pullback is injective on it and Jac(C)[n1] is cyclic.

**Factor base.** F = {P in C(F_p) : x(P) in [-B, B] minus {0}}, with x the coordinate of the even model, so the "small" notion of rounds 1-4 is kept. B = p^delta. Both P and -P are in F, so a fibre of pullback (x = +-sqrt(X0)) can split.

**Arms.**
- A1, end to end, genus 2. Index calculus in Jac(C)[n1]:
  1. Generate random R_j = a_j pullback(P) + b_j pullback(Q) and reduce.
  2. A relation is found when u splits into linear factors over F_p whose roots map into F.
  3. Collect |F|/2 + 10 relations; solve mod n1 by sparse elimination; recover k; verify [k]P = Q on E1.
  4. B is tuned per size (scan delta in steps of 0.05; report the optimum).
  5. Sizes p = 2^10 to 2^20 in steps of 2 bits (larger if the implementation allows); 3 curves x 3 targets per size.
- A2, splitting law, genus 2. For p in {2^12, 2^16, 2^20, 2^24} and delta in {0.3, 0.4, ..., 0.9}, sample 10^5 random elements of Jac(C)(F_p) (more when the expected count is below 50). Record the split fraction against the exact null: the number of reduced divisors supported on F divided by #Jac(C)(F_p).
  - Fibre control: 10^4 pullbacks of random points of E1. Expected split rate about #{X0 = x^2 : |x| <= B} / p.
- A3, splitting law, genus 3. As A2, at p in {2^12, 2^16, 2^20} and delta in {0.5, ..., 0.9}. The null counts reduced divisors of degree <= 3 supported on F.
- Baseline: Pollard rho with negation on E1[n1], 15 solves per size, the same 3 curves.

**Metrics and pricing.**
- Primary metric: counts. Jacobian reductions and additions are counted, then converted to E1-addition equivalents using a measured ratio from the same implementation language and host. Linear-algebra operations mod n1 are counted at 1 per multiply-add. Rho counts E1 additions.
- Secondary metric: wall time on the same host, single thread.
- Fit total cost proportional to n1^alpha per arm, with a bootstrap 95% CI over curves and targets (2,000 resamples, seed 24).
- Implementation: genus-2 Cantor or explicit formulas (Lange) in C is recommended once Sage's arithmetic limits the sizes. Sage is acceptable for A3 and for cross-checks. Every C routine is cross-checked against Sage on 10^3 random operations before use.

**Predictions (frozen).** A2 and A3: measured/null ratio in [0.8, 1.25] at every cell, with no trend in p. Fibre control matches its null. A1: alpha about 4/3 for the basic algorithm (optimum B about p^(2/3)), and at least 1 under every B; rho alpha = 0.50 +- 0.03.

**Criteria (frozen).**
- SUCCESS: A1's alpha CI lies entirely below rho's. Or, at either genus, the log of the measured/null splitting ratio has a slope against log2 p whose 95% CI lies entirely above 0 (an excess that grows with p).
- KILL: A1's alpha CI lies entirely above rho's, and the measured/null ratio lies in [0.5, 2] at every A2 and A3 cell with slope CIs containing 0.
- INCONCLUSIVE: otherwise. Departures go to DEVIATIONS.md; nothing is reinterpreted.

**Excluded, with reasons.**
- Searching the linear system |D| for divisors supported on F: this is a summation-polynomial problem on C, measured in its Riemann-Roch form by SA-04.
- Degree-3 covers (Frey-Kani (3,3)-gluing): their construction over F_p needs tooling not in the environment. The quantity that decides them is the same reduced-divisor statistic A2 measures. Listed as a limitation.

**Cost and tier.** Sonnet executor; one core; about 1 day. C (gcc), SageMath at `/opt/conda-sage` (no Cremona database; build curves from coefficients), PARI.
