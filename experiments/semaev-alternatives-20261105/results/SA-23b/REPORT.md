# SA-23b REPORT: Jacobi-sum ranks of y^2 = x^3 + t^12 + 1 against exhaustive enumeration

## Verdict: INCONCLUSIVE

Enumeration confirms the mechanism at every new prime; the (73,12) control passes; but no rule in the frozen candidate set reaches 99% (best 50.8% of held-out primes). By the prereg ("enumeration confirms the ranks but no rule in the candidate set reaches 99%") this is INCONCLUSIVE, not SUCCESS. KILL conditions did not occur.

## Residue-symbol law (frozen class)

Exponent strings take only 12 distinct values over the 2374 primes, each about 1/12 of primes (183 to 216). Within the frozen class (one quartic symbol of u and one cubic symbol of v, u,v in the 24-element set, per-piece lookup table on the 12 symbol cells) the best rule gets 50.8% held-out. The cell partition generated by the symbols of -1, 2, 3 jointly (quartic -1,2,3; cubic 2,3; 72 cells) is not deterministic either: majority agreement 55.4%. So the failure is not a matter of picking the wrong (u,v); the candidate set's symbols do not carry enough information.

## Borderline readings / flags (not resolved)

- Post-hoc, NOT part of the verdict: adding (a mod 3, b mod 3) of the primary Z[i] prime a+bi (p = a^2+b^2) to the symbols of -1,2,3 gives 144 cells in which the exponent string is a function in all 2374 primes (in-sample). Out-of-sample check, fit below a cutoff and test above: cutoff 10^4: 91.1% of held-out primes fall in a seen cell, and 100% of those are correct; cutoffs 3x10^4 and 5x10^4: all held-out primes covered, 100% correct (n=1582 and 1110). This is a different rule class from the frozen one (it uses a residue of the Gaussian prime itself, not a symbol of a small integer), found after seeing the data, so it counts for nothing here. It suggests a follow-up preregistration with that class.
- The frozen wording "predicts all 20 exponents at >= 99% of held-out primes" was read as all 20 correct per prime; the per-exponent reading (80.3%) also fails.
- The exponent labelling depends on the choice of prime of Z[zeta12] above p; the canonical convention is mine (see DEVIATIONS.md). A Galois-covariant true law makes a table fit insensitive to this, but I have not proved the law is covariant.
- R_J = Gram rank + 1 at six of nine cells (rank 3 vs 4): the "or R_J - 1" clause of the criterion carries those cells, as pre-registered.

## Limitations

- Enumeration is exhaustive only for integral sections of the stated shape (SA-23 enumerator); Gram rank is a lower bound for the Mordell-Weil rank if non-integral generators exist (this is what R_J - 1 is read as).
- Three new primes only; the rank-16 prediction rests on one prime (157) and the poor-cell predictions on two.
- Tate/Shioda-Tate is used for R_J, not re-derived; the match to Gram ranks at 9 cells is the evidence.
- Shared host, load 1.2 to 4.8, so wall times are not comparable across cells; no fit uses timings.
- The Jacobi job and Gram runs overlapped with two chunks briefly (see DEVIATIONS.md).
